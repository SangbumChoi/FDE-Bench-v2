At a glance

What the AI has to do

Write a Python program for a hospital's outpatient booking office. At the end of every working day it books the day's appointment requests into the doctors' free 20-minute slots of the next 10 working days, refills freed slots from the waitlist, and reports for each department the free slots, the slots held back for urgent patients and the waiting requests. The aim is to leave as little doctor time empty as possible without breaking the hospital's booking rules. The program is tested on 20 working days (9 November – 4 December 2026) that are not in the data.

What the AI is given

  • The client's request: from Monday 9 November the booking office wants a program that books patients into the doctors' free slots every working day and says where each department stands; "above all we want to stop losing doctor time to empty slots". It names none of the booking rules.
  • booking_system_export.sql: the booking system as of Friday 6 November. 14 doctors in five departments (internal medicine, cardiology, dermatology, orthopedics, pediatrics) with the languages they speak and their weekly clinics, the clinic calendar to 20 November, four weeks of requests and appointments with what happened (about one patient in five did not turn up; the first two morning slots held urgent patients only and mostly stayed empty), cancellations, the waitlist (45 requests still waiting) and the 362 appointments already booked from 9 November.
  • mail/: seven e-mails to the booking office: past absences of two doctors and an interpreter, a department head asking for a list, the director's office asking to book an acquaintance "tomorrow morning", and the outpatient office's notes on City Day (Monday 23 November; the calendar was not changed for it) and on the data codes.
  • pilot_day_input.json and pilot_day_output.json: one day's input exactly as the program will get it every day (the calendar of the next 10 working days, new requests, notices such as cancellations and absences, the current bookings and the waitlist) and the answer of the client's pilot program, which shows the output format.

What the AI must find out by asking the client

  • How urgent requests are handled: each is booked the day it arrives, for a day within 2 working days of the first day it can come. The first two or three slots of every morning session (09:00 and 09:20, or 09:00 to 09:40, depending on the version) are kept for urgent patients, and, new from 9 November, the empty ones are opened to everyone on the working day before (the office used to keep them empty).
  • Which clinic is closed on City Day, Monday 23 November, although the calendar still lists its sessions: Dermatology, Orthopedics or Pediatrics, depending on the version.
  • How many in-person interpreters the hospital has per day: 2, 3 or 4, depending on the version. Every new-patient visit with a doctor who does not speak the patient's language needs one; follow-ups use phone interpreting, without limit.
  • The waitlist order, depending on the version: by urgency, then by date received; urgent first, then everyone else by date received; or by urgency, then by the earliest day the patient can come. A request may not be booked while a higher-priority one for the same department, visit type and doctor still waits and could have taken that slot.
  • The booking basics: a new patient takes two consecutive 20-minute slots, a follow-up one, with the doctor the patient saw last time (a habit; the head of the outpatient office can grant exceptions, and such a visit counts a tenth of a slot less). A changed date or time must be at least 3 working days after the day of the change, and every change costs points. Staff notes carry no weight.
  • What the daily status table counts over the next 10 working days: empty slots anyone may book, empty urgent slots two or more days ahead, and waiting requests (on hard, waiting patients), leaving out the closed clinic on City Day and, on medium and hard, absent doctors' days and expired referrals. Each of its 15 numbers must be exact, and every wrong one costs as much as two empty slots.
  • On medium and hard: a new patient's referral letter expires on a set date; a fasting blood test must be done at least one working day before the visit, and the lab takes 5, 6 or 7 a day, depending on the version; when a doctor or an interpreter is away, the affected appointments are moved or put back on the waitlist the day the notice arrives.
  • What is out of scope on easy (absences, referral letters, blood tests: book as if they were not there) and on easy and medium (no overbooking; a patient's second request gets its own appointment).
  • On hard: each patient has their own no-show rate, about 1 percentage point higher per working day booked ahead; two follow-ups may share a slot, at most 1, 2 or 3 such slots per doctor and session, depending on the version, and a shared slot where both come costs as much as three empty slots; a second request by the same patient for the same department is the same need and is never booked.

How it is scored

Scores 0 if the deliverable…

  • Crashes or takes more than 10 seconds on any day, returns a malformed action, books a request that is not waiting, or books outside the next 10 working days.
  • Puts two appointments in one slot (on hard: more than two follow-ups, a new patient sharing, or more shared slots per doctor and session than allowed), or books off the calendar's slots, with another department's doctor or before the patient's earliest date.
  • Books someone in the closed clinic on City Day, or more new-patient visits needing an interpreter on a day than there are interpreters; on medium and hard also a visit after the referral expired, a blood test missing, too late or over the lab's daily limit, or an appointment left with an absent doctor.
  • Leaves an urgent request unbooked at the end of the day it arrived or books it beyond its 2 working days, or puts a non-urgent patient in one of the reserved morning slots two or more days ahead.
  • Gives an appointment a new date or time less than 3 working days ahead, or books a request while a higher-priority one that could have taken that slot still waits.
  • Returns a day's status table without a row for every department or without its three whole numbers.
  • On hard, gives a patient two appointments in one department; or sends a follow-up to another doctor, unless the company policy lets this rule bend and the AI asked the client for an exception first.

If it is valid, quality is…

Higher is better. The operations value in points, measured against the reference program's: +10 per 20-minute slot of every visit that takes place (a new patient's visit is two slots), −0.4 per working day a request waits (×3 if urgent, ×2 soon, ×1 routine), −10 per request never booked, −4 per appointment the hospital moves or cancels and −1 per follow-up seen by a colleague and −20 per status-table number that is not exactly right (15 a day). The client's own way (score 0) books the same way but never opens the reserved morning slots for the next day and never double-books; the reference solution (score 1) opens them, refills freed slots from the waitlist in order and, on hard, double-books follow-ups of patients likely to stay away.

What the AI is asked

The client's request: the only text the AI gets at the start. It is the same in every version of the case.

From Monday 9 November our outpatient booking office wants to work through a program: every working day it gets that day's new appointment requests, cancellations and notices together with our current bookings, books patients into the doctors' free slots and tells us where each department stands. Above all we want to stop losing doctor time to empty slots. Could you build it? Attached are an export of our booking system (doctors, clinic calendar, four weeks of requests and appointments with what happened to them, cancellations, attendance and the waitlist), the e-mails our booking office received (absences, notes, the outpatient office's instructions) and one pilot day (what the program receives and what our pilot answered).

Deliverable spec (the program interface)

A program/ directory with scheduler.py defining on_day(day). The engine loads it once and calls it once per
working day of the test period (days the AI never sees), in order, in the same process (module-level state survives between calls);
10 s per call, Python standard library only, no network and no access to the data files at run time.
day = {"date", "day_index", "days_in_period", "period_end", "booking_window": {"first", "last"}, "doctors",
"sessions", "new_requests", "notices", "attendance", "book", "waitlist"} exactly as in the pilot-day input file.
Return {"actions": [{"type": "book" | "move", "request_id", "doctor_id", "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
"start": "HH:MM", "test_date": "YYYY-MM-DD" or null} | {"type": "release", "request_id"}],
"status_table": {department_code: {"free_slots": int, "held_back": int, "waitlist": int}}} in the format of the
pilot-day output file. Actions are applied in order; a request not booked stays on the waitlist.

Case notes (README)

Hospital Appointment Scheduler — healthcare/hospital_appointment_scheduler

A mid-size outpatient hospital wants its booking office to run day by day through a program. Every working day the program gets the day's new appointment requests, the notices of the day (patient cancellations, a doctor off sick or at a conference, an interpreter away, staff notes), today's attendance and the current booking book and waitlist (the effect of its own earlier decisions). It books requests into the doctors' free 20-minute slots of the next ten working days, re-books appointments a notice affects, fills freed slots from the waitlist and reports a daily status table per department. The agent delivers program/scheduler.py:on_day; the evaluator replays a hidden period of 20 working days (9 November – 4 December 2026: 1,184 new requests, 45 waiting and 362 booked at the start) in one isolated process. The core of the case is slot utilization: as many patients seen as possible in the doctors' time, without breaking a rule. The setting is country-neutral: no national insurer or payer rules.

The agent sees an export of the booking system as a plain-text SQL dump (14 doctors in five departments with languages and weekly sessions, the calendar, four weeks of requests and appointments with their outcome — 952 appointments, 19% of the attended-or-missed ones missed; 09:00 and 09:20 held urgent patients only and stayed empty on most mornings; 74 of the 571 follow-ups were seen by a colleague of the patient's doctor — cancellations, attendance, the waitlist's history and the 362 appointments already booked from 9 November), the booking office's e-mails (absences, an interpreter's absence, staff notes, a director's-acquaintance request and the outpatient office's instructions: City Day on 23 November, the calendar not updated for it) and one pilot day: an input exactly as the engine sends it (one notice of each kind, an acquaintance note) and the answer of the client's pilot program under its interim rules (it keeps 09:00 and 09:20 empty and reports held_back 0). The data shows the office's old practice and nothing else: every client fact the score rewards differs from it. A setting is a tier (easy / medium / hard: which rules are in force) and a rule seed, which draws the client's rule values from candidate pools: the clinic closed on City Day, the size of the urgent reserve, the interpreters per day, the waitlist order, the lab cap and the overbooking cap. The public panel is rule seeds 1, 10 and 64 (nine settings); any other seed is a private draw of the same pools. Every setting is one world: the history, the hidden period, every request, cancellation intent, notice and note are the same, and the agent's view (request, data, deliverable spec) is byte-identical (checked by the validator). Only the hidden requirements and the evaluator differ. The engagement knobs apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

The daily flow

day 0 … 19 (end of the working day):
  engine: today's appointments happen (attendance) ─► patients' cancellations free slots ─► day input
          (new requests, notices, sessions of the next 10 working days, book, waitlist)
                                   │
                                   ▼
                           on_day(day) ─► actions (book / move / release) + status table
                                   │
                                   ▼
  evaluator: applies the actions in order; checks every rule on the changed appointments and on the
             upcoming book; scores the table against the true one; the book carries over to tomorrow
end:       operations value over the clinic days 10 Nov – 7 Dec (days 1–20, final after the last call)

Program state survives between calls (one process), but the input repeats everything that matters: the book (every upcoming appointment with its request) and the waitlist (requests without an appointment). A patient's cancellation arrives as a notice with the slot already free (the request closes); a patient cancels k working days before the appointment (hidden per-request intent, 135 of the requests), so how many cancellations happen depends on what the program booked.

The status table and the score

Status table (per department, after the day's actions, over the booking window d+1…d+10):

  • free_slots: calendar slots of the department's doctors with no appointment that anyone may be booked into — not held back, not in the closed clinic on City Day, (medium+) not on a notified absence day;
  • held_back: empty urgent-reserve slots (the first reserve_slots slots of a morning session: 09:00 and 09:20, or 09:00 to 09:40) on d+2 or later (same exclusions);
  • waitlist: waiting requests of the department; medium+ without new patients whose referral has expired (valid until before d+1); hard counts patients (a patient's duplicate requests once) and not patients who hold an upcoming appointment there or had one take place in the period.

An entry is right only when exact, and every entry that is not costs 20 in the operations value (as much as two empty slots: the department staffs for a wrong number); a failed call counts its 15 entries wrong. The table is mandatory (status_table): a call that answers without it, or without a department's row or its three whole numbers, makes the program invalid.

In the reference runs the seen term is 15,250–15,370 of easy's 11,423–11,593 operations value (utilization 97.4–97.8%), against 3,050–3,690 of waiting cost in every tier: utilization is the largest weight by far. Utilization = seen patient-slots / the bookable slots of days 1–20 (easy counts the absent doctors' slots: a locum covers them).

Objective (maximize) = operations value / the reference program's operations value in the same setting, the table's cost included (the reference's table is exact). Before the audit it was 0.8 × the ratio + 0.2 × accuracy, so the pilot's table, right on a third of its entries, still kept 0.87 of the objective; the audit made it the ratio × the table's accuracy, under which one entry wrong every day cost 0.12–0.29 of the quality scale, one department's row 0.36–0.86 and counting expired referrals (medium) up to all of it; the check made a wrong entry a cost in the client's terms instead (GT.md part 3 has what each slip costs now: one entry every day 0.10–0.16, one row 0.30–0.50, the pilot's table everything). The reference value is recomputed deterministically in-process (cached), so the reference scores exactly 1.0. Quality is normalized between the baseline (0) and the reference (1): q = (objective − baseline) / (1 − baseline). A program that books nothing but reports a perfect table is invalid (urgent requests unbooked).

Baseline (baseline_solution): the booking office's practice without an engineer, as the export shows it: the same program (earliest feasible slot in the priority order, every rule kept, the same exact status table), but the urgent reserve stays with urgent patients and is never released for tomorrow's clinics, and nothing is ever double-booked. The client itself names the released reserve as one of the two places it loses most doctor time today. It is valid in every combination of the pools (and on world seeds 1–3) and scores 0.76–0.77 in easy and 0.74 in medium with a reserve of two slots, 0.61–0.63 in hard; a reserve of three (seed 64) leaves more slots unused, 0.65 / 0.60 / 0.45.

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review of healthcare/hospital_appointment_scheduler

Findings of the case review (F1–F9), with the verifier's verdicts and what this change did about each. The numbers are for seed 20261116 unless they say otherwise; "q" is normalized quality (baseline 0, reference 1). A later change replaced the three fixed rule sets A/B/C with rule seeds that draw each rule value from a pool (section "Rule seeds" at the end). It resolves F3 and moves the numbers of F1, F2, F4 and F5; the findings below keep the rule-set numbers they were written with.

F1 — Utilization sits at the ceiling, so packing earns little (high, partly)

Capacity is about 79 slots a day and demand about 83–86, so the reference already fills 98% of the slots in easy and medium. The best valid variants we know (packing; in hard also a lower overbooking threshold and the true no-show rates) add only +0.1% to +1.5% to the objective, which is q = 1.005–1.056 against the new baseline. Valid programs still spread from about 0.79 to 1.0, but through a few coarse levers (releasing the reserve, the D10 note, overbooking at all) rather than through packing. The original "clipped at 1.0" point is gone with the new scale, but the policy cap of 1.5 is now out of reach: no known valid program earns more than about 70% of the quality credit.

Status: open — proposal: decide first whether better optimization should be measurable here. If yes, make placement harder rather than lowering demand (more follow-ups tied to one doctor, interpreter- and test-heavy days, mixed visit lengths) and add a stronger reference or an upper bound, so packing and look-ahead move the score by several percent. If not, set quality_cap in case.yaml to about 1.1, so that a program at the best known level gets full credit.

F2 — Overbooking does not need the per-patient no-show estimate (high, confirmed)

Overbooking every eligible follow-up slot up to the cap (threshold 0) scores 0.999 / 1.005 / 1.000 in hard A/B/C (q 0.997 / 1.015 / 1.000), all valid; a threshold of 0.10 scores slightly above the reference and no overbooking 0.943–0.952. So whether to overbook matters (about 5%), but the risk selection the hard tier is meant to test does not. Until this change the 10-against-12 trade-off was stated only as a score weight, which the simulated client may not reveal.

Status: open — proposal: make risk selection decide the score: raise the collision cost (or add a doctor overtime cost) so blanket overbooking is clearly net negative, and widen the spread of no-show rates so that selective overbooking clearly beats it. Add a validate-case check that the threshold-0 program scores at least 3% below the reference. (This change, under F6, already states the trade-off as a business fact in the hard requirements: when both patients come the doctor runs late, which costs a little more than an empty slot.)

F3 — Rule-set numbers are inert or one-sided (high, confirmed)

urgent_days = 1, 3 or 10 gives objective 1.0000 with no violation in all nine settings: urgent requests are always local-language, test-free, due tomorrow and generated only when tomorrow's reserve is untouched. Before this change the most conservative value of every number (2 interpreters, lab 5, overbooking 1, notice 4, urgent 1) was valid in 8 of 9 settings at 0.98–1.00, so only waitlist_order (and weakly closed_dept) separated the rule sets. After the F8 fix that envelope is invalid in 8 of 9 (waitlist_priority: unused interpreters and lab places no longer excuse a skipped patient), but notice 4 + overbooking 1 + urgent 1 alone is still valid in 6 of 9 at 0.988–1.000.

Status: fixed in the rule-seed change (section "Rule seeds"). Until then it was open with this proposal: make the remaining numbers two-sided, so the most cautious value is also wrong (for example a maximum rebooking delay next to the notice period, or an urgent window with a triage earliest day so that "tomorrow" is not always right). Generate some urgent requests that need an interpreter or a test, or that arrive when tomorrow's reserve is already used, so urgent_days binds. Add the envelope program to validate-case's distinctness check and require it to be invalid or more than 3% worse. What the change did instead: the two numbers that do not bind are no longer drawn (the urgent window is fixed at 2 working days, the notice period at 3), the reserve size becomes a two-sided drawn value, and the envelope is invalid in 8 of the 9 public settings and 14% worse in the ninth. validate-case's binding check (every drawn parameter must change the answer) replaces the distinctness check; the envelope itself is not a validator check.

F4 — Bending the follow-up habit rewards the blanket bend, not the targeted one (high, confirmed)

Booking every follow-up with whichever colleague is earliest (287–332 exceptions) raises the objective by 2.0–5.8%; bending only for patients of an absent doctor (43–51 exceptions) by 0.0–1.3%. With the new scale the blanket bend is worth q 1.11–1.29, i.e. +0.04 to +0.115 on the final score before the penalty, so it now pays under startup_velocity (penalty 0.05) in 5 of 9 settings, while the clinically sensible targeted bend (+0.000 to +0.024) never pays. One approval covers all the exceptions.

Status: open — proposal: give some of the sick pediatrician's follow-ups a clinical deadline inside the absence, so that keeping the habit leaves them unseen and only a targeted, approved exception recovers the value. Add a small operations cost per follow-up seen by a doctor other than the previous one, so the blanket bend scores below the targeted one. Report bending in final-score terms in the README. Rule seeds: unchanged in substance. On the public panel the blanket bend (302–330 exceptions) adds 2.0–5.7% and the targeted one (44–57) 0.0–1.4%.

F5 — The visible history contradicts the hospital's rules (medium, confirmed)

In the export, 106 of the 151 history appointments at 09:00/09:20 are S/R requests booked two or more working days ahead, which the reserve rule forbids; interpreted new-patient visits reach 10 a day (cap 2–4) and fasting tests 10 a day (cap 5–7). _history fills slots at 92% with no reserve, interpreter or lab logic, while the initial book is rule-consistent. The pilot answer uses waitlist order received (rule set B's) and no reserve.

Status: open — proposal: generate the history under the rules all three rule sets share (reserve kept, at most 2 interpreted new-patient visits and 5 tests a day), or have an outpatient-office mail say that the reserve, interpreter rota and lab cap start on 9 November. Reword the lab cap as "fasting draw slots in the outpatient phlebotomy room" to make the small number plausible, and give the pilot an interim order that matches none of the rule sets (or say in the README that it matches B). Rule seeds: still open. The pilot's order received is now one of the three candidates of waitlist_order (seed 64 draws it). The initial book holds 09:00 and 09:20 free, i.e. a two-slot reserve; when a seed draws three slots the requirements now say, in all five locales, that the office kept only two until 9 November and that the 09:40 appointments already booked stay where they are.

F6 — Scoring targets the client is told never to reveal (medium, partly)

scoring and scoring_hard were score formulas and weights, and status_table ("20% of quality") and several_departments ("costs 3") carried weights too, while the client prompt forbids describing scores or weights; Ask-F1 recall could not reach 1. The "already visible" part did not hold: in the default interact_req condition the agent sees only request.md and the data, so interface, visit_length and the period end still have to be asked.

Status: fixed in this change — the four targets are rewritten in all five locales as business facts (empty doctor time is the biggest loss; waiting, unbooked requests and changes cost; what counts with no-shows is the patients who come; a double-booked slot where both come costs a little more than an empty one; extra trips are a cost) with no weights. The hard requirements gain the one sentence the last fact needs; scoring is unchanged. interface, visit_length and evaluation_period are kept.

F7 — Lower-tier scope written as test instructions (medium, partly)

Easy and medium said "each request is its own visit, even when the same patient asks twice" and "tests out of scope", which reads like an instruction to the program rather than a fact of the hospital; deduplicating there is in fact invalid (waitlist_priority, 187–342 cases). The notes guidance (director's acquaintance, D10's afternoons) is stated only in hard, although following D10's note costs 6.7–7.5% in easy and medium.

Status: fixed in this change — the easy and medium scope bullets now say why, as the client would, in all five locales: the referral desk and the lab handle referral letters and fasting tests themselves this round; no-shows are not planned for; a second request gets its own appointment because the office cannot tell from a request whether it is the same complaint. Scoring is unchanged, and the matching targets are reworded. Left as a proposal: move the notes paragraph and the staff_notes target into all tiers, since the notes are in every tier's data.

F8 — Loose waitlist-priority excuses and a notice loophole (low, confirmed)

Reproduced with the old checker: a program that never books an interpreted new-patient visit was valid in 9 of 9 settings (0.950–0.988), one that books test-needing requests last was valid in 6 of 6 medium/hard settings (0.998–1.006), and a follow-up released on one day and re-booked the next day for the day after broke no rule in rule sets A and C (notice 3 and 4; with notice 2 it cannot happen).

Status: fixed in this change — a waiting interpreter-needing new patient is excused only when that day's in-person interpreters are all taken at the end of the call; a test-needing one only when no working day before the slot (from tomorrow, not before its earliest date) has lab room, the booked request's own test place counting as room; move_notice now also covers a released request booked again later (notice from the release day; the released date and time again need none), and the matching release excuse counts from the release day.
