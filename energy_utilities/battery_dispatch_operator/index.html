What the agent is asked

The client's request, the only text the agent gets up front. It is the same in every setting.

Can you plan when the battery at our site in Spain should charge and discharge next week (Monday 22 to Sunday 28 March) to make the most money? I've attached the price forecast, the solar forecast for the co-located solar farm, the battery spec sheet, our connection agreement and last week's dispatch log.

Deliverable spec (shown to the extractor that reshapes the agent's files, not to the agent)

dispatch.csv, ASCII header: timestamp,charge_mw,discharge_mw
One row per 15-minute interval of the week 2027-03-22 00:00 .. 2027-03-28 23:45, local time, timestamp = interval
start as "YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM". charge_mw / discharge_mw = average battery power at the meter over the interval in
MW, both >= 0.

Settings: one dataset, 9 ground truths

The agent sees the same request and data in every setting. The tier (= difficulty) decides which rules apply; the rule seed draws each rule parameter, independently of the others, from its candidates below. Each difficulty is reported as the mean over rule seeds: the public ones below, or private seeds that draw from the same candidates.

Case notes (README)

Battery Dispatch Operator — energy_utilities/battery_dispatch_operator

The operator of a 40 MWh / 20 MW battery at a solar-plus-storage site in Spain (Llano Verde: the battery and a 30 MWp solar farm share one point of connection and one meter) asks the agent to plan when the battery should charge and discharge next week, Monday 22 to Sunday 28 March 2027, "to make the most money". The deliverable is dispatch.csv (timestamp,charge_mw,discharge_mw), one row per local quarter-hour of the week. The plan is settled against the week's actual prices, which differ from the forecast the agent sees; the scorer simulates the state of charge quarter by quarter and checks every rule of the setting.

The case has settings: three tiers × a public panel of three rule seeds (141, 70, 334). The agent sees the same request and the same data in every setting (the validator checks that its prompt and workspace are byte-identical); a setting changes which rules apply and the operating values the client would have to tell the engineer. A rule seed draws each value on its own from a candidate pool, so any other seed is a private setting with its own ground truth. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

What an agent must ask

  • All tiers: how efficiency splits by direction (the sheet gives only the round trip); the warranty SOC window (the sheet gives the manufacturer's); the end-of-week SOC; the current export limit (the agreement is stale); that settlement is quarter-hourly against an hourly price file; the clock change on 28 March (668 rows); what "money" means (profit against solar alone, with export and import as metered).
  • Medium and hard: the off-peak import window, the evening reserve's share and hours, and the degradation cost.
  • Hard: whether any notices change the week. There are three, each with its own day (and the DSO's limit). Also how to treat the trading desk's push on the reserve: it is policy, and only an approved exception (request_exception) lifts it.

Last week's log (dispatch_log_last_week.csv) was planned under last week's conventions (10–90%, 92% each way, 50% start and end, a 20% reserve 17:00–21:00, import 00:00–06:00, a 25 MW cap) by last week's planner. Under every public setting at least one of its values is wrong. The old fixed problem's plan is invalid in all nine settings, because its 25 MW cap is above every candidate. An agent that reads the conventions off the log instead of asking fails.

SOC is checked to within 0.02 MWh (0.05% of capacity), so a plan rounded to two or three decimals is not rejected for rounding alone. Quality is the battery's profit at actual prices: per quarter, site export revenue (price × 0.25 × min(net, export limit)) minus grid import cost, minus what the solar farm alone would have earned (price × 0.25 × min(solar, export limit)), minus degradation EUR per MWh of charge plus discharge. The export limit of a quarter is export_cap, 30 MW after the works end and dso_mw in the DSO's hours.

Situations present

  • Stale document. The connection agreement says 30 MW; substation works cut the export limit to 12–18 MW. In hard the works end early and the agreement is right again from a given day.
  • Spec sheet vs. the client's model. The sheet quotes 84.6% round trip and 5%–95% SOC; the client models an efficiency per direction and its warranty may or may not be narrower.
  • Units and the clock. Prices are hourly but settlement is quarter-hourly. Clocks go forward on Sunday 28 March (EU summer time, last Sunday of March), so the week has 668 quarters; a file with 02:00–02:45 rows on that day is fatal.
  • Clipped solar. With a 12–18 MW export limit and 30 MWp of solar, much of the midday output is clipped unless the battery absorbs it; that energy is free. This is the main source of value a planner must not waste.
  • Off-peak import window (medium, hard). Charging from solar is allowed any time, from the grid only in the window.
  • Dated notices (hard). Three changes for named hours that the documents do not show.
  • Pressure on a bendable rule (medium, hard: the reserve; hard: the trading desk's push). Bending the reserve is worth +4% to +19% to the reference and +0% to +13% to the forecast LP. It pays most under a 30% reserve.
  • Price uncertainty. Actual prices are the forecast plus a daily bias and hourly noise (floored at 0); the agent plans on the forecast only.

Thirteen clarification targets: efficiency, soc_window, start_end_soc, export_cap, grid_charging, quarter_settlement, dst_clock and profit_scoring in every tier (8); evening_reserve and degradation from medium (10); works_end, dso_limit and maintenance_outage in hard (13).

Reference and baseline

The reference is a greedy two-cycles-a-day rule on forecast prices. Each day it fills up in the cheapest night quarters of the import window and sells into the dearest morning quarters (06:00–11:00). It then refills in the 16 cheapest sunny quarters (09:00–17:00), from the grid too where import is allowed at any hour, and always soaks up clipped solar. Finally it sells into the dearest quarters from 15:00 on. A quarter is sold only if its price beats the cycle's average buy price divided by the round-trip efficiency plus twice the degradation cost. It counts fewer MW in quarters whose export limit is below 20 MW, so more quarters are used there. Two refinements over last week's planner, which made the log:

  • The night fill leaves room for the daylight refill that the morning sale will not free: always for clipped solar, and for the whole refill when daylight is cheaper than night. The night state may drop below the reserve and the end target by what the refill brings before the reserve hours (counted at 80%).
  • Once the reserve hours are over, it sells the reserve too.

It keeps the reserve, skips offline quarters and plans the last day to end at the target. Under last week's values with the last-week switch it reproduces the old fixed reference exactly (18,084.35 EUR).

The baseline is the site without a dispatch plan: the battery rests at 50% all week and the farm sells solar alone, battery profit 0.00 EUR in every setting (valid; nothing moves, and no end target is above 50%). With a zero baseline the normalized quality equals the plain ratio to the reference.

The forecast LP (an analysis script with scipy/HiGHS over charge, discharge, export, import and clipping per quarter, with every rule of the setting, never discharging while solar is clipped) is what a careful agent can reach. On the four world seeds it reaches 1.03–1.30 of the reference. A perfect-foresight LP reaches 1.11–1.38, an upper bound no plan can reach. Under the default quality cap of 1.5 a forecast LP earns about 0.69–0.86 of the quality credit.

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review — energy_utilities/battery_dispatch_operator

Findings of the case review (F1–F9) and what this change did about each; numbers are at world seed 20270322 unless stated. Comments welcome on the open proposals.

F1 — Reference far below a plain LP; the task is convex, not combinatorial (high, confirmed)

The reference is a greedy two-cycles-a-day heuristic at 18,084.35 EUR. A linear program on the forecast prices (2,004 variables, about 3 s with HiGHS) reaches 21,343.2 EUR at actual prices (q = 1.180), and a perfect-foresight LP 23,099.9 (q = 1.277), so the reference sits at 85% of what a careful agent can reach. The review was written against a 1.0 quality cap, under which every valid LP saturated; on the new scale (baseline 0, cap 1.5) the forecast LP earns 1.180 / 1.5 = 0.79 of the quality credit, so the problem is now the opposite: the cap is out of reach for any plan made from the forecast. The LP never charges and discharges in one quarter, so the either/or rule never binds and task_type: Combinatorial Optimization does not fit.

Status: open — proposal: keep the heuristic as the reference and set quality_cap in case.yaml to the forecast LP's normalized quality (1.18 at the default seed, 1.11–1.16 at seeds 1–3; 1.15 would be a fair single value), so the best attainable plan earns full credit. The alternative, making the forecast LP the reference, needs an LP solver in case.py (scipy is not a dependency today). Relabel task_type as LP / convex optimization unless non-convex rules are added (minimum run blocks, a daily cycle cap with start counts).

Update (rule seeds): still open, with new numbers. The lower export caps make clipped solar the main source of value, so the reference got two refinements: the night fill leaves room for the cheaper daylight refill, and the reserve is sold once its hours are over. Across the nine public settings and world seeds 20270322 and 1–3, the forecast LP reaches 1.03–1.30 of the reference, and perfect foresight 1.11–1.38. A single quality_cap of about 1.2 would still fit most settings; the 25 EUR/MWh degradation settings (seed 334) sit at 1.11–1.30. task_type is unchanged.

F2 — Last week's log reveals most hidden rules (high, partly)

dispatch_log_last_week.csv is generated by the reference heuristic with the hidden constants, and its soc_pct column shows them: maximum 90.0%, minimum 20.0%, start and end 50.0%, at least 20% in 17:00–21:00, and SOC steps that give 0.92 per direction exactly. The data also shows the clock change (668 solar rows, 23 price hours on 28 March), and ignoring degradation costs only 0.1% (21,320 vs. 21,343 EUR). The verifier found the "one question is enough" claim overstated: the grid-charging window cannot be read from the log, and a plan without it or without the end-SOC rule is invalid.

Status: open — proposal: make the log stale evidence to check with the client, generated under last week's different parameters (e.g. a 15% floor, a different reserve window, an older efficiency), or drop or coarsen soc_pct. Then decide explicitly which targets are "confirm what the data suggests" and which are "only the client knows", and make degradation large enough to change the plan (see F3).

Update (rule seeds): partly resolved. The log is unchanged (byte-identical data). It still shows last week's values because last week's planner made it with last week's conventions (LOG_RULES in case.py). Every value it reveals is now a drawn parameter, though, and under every public setting at least one of them is wrong (always the 25 MW cap). So the log is stale evidence, and the requirements say so in the client's words ("planned with last week's settings, and not all of them are the same this week"). Degradation is a parameter (5/15/25 EUR/MWh) and changes the plan; 38–84% of the reference's objective at hard/s141. The proposal to coarsen soc_pct stays open.

F3 — No settings; several parameters are natural rule-set knobs (high, confirmed)

The case has one ground truth (setting.json is {}), no tiers or rule sets, and until this change no README. All parameters are constants in case.py. Degradation at EUR 5/MWh barely matters (an LP that ignores it cycles 886 instead of 808 MWh and loses only 0.1% of profit), while EUR 15–25/MWh cuts throughput to 523–433 MWh and removes most of the thin morning cycle.

Status: open — proposal: easy covers the SOC window, power, per-direction efficiency, end SOC, the 25 MW cap and the clock change, with the requirements saying that the evening reserve and the import window do not apply this week; medium adds the import window, the reserve, a binding degradation cost and a warranty cycle cap (e.g. 1.5 equivalent cycles a day); hard adds dated events and pressure (F4). Rule sets A/B/C could vary the export cap (25/22/27 MW), reserve (20% 17–21 h, 30% 18–22 h, 15% 16–20 h), import window (00–06, 01–05, 23–07), degradation (5/15/25 EUR/MWh), end SOC (50/40/60%) and warranty window (10–90, 15–85, 5–95%), checked so each rule set's reference is invalid or at least 1% worse under the others.

Status (rule seeds): fixed in this change: three tiers and a parameter pool per hidden value, drawn per rule seed (see "Rule seeds" below). This follows the proposal, with five differences. The export cap pool is 18/15/12 MW, not 25/22/27, because a cap at or above the battery's 20 MW rarely binds. The import windows are 00-06/01-05/02-08, so no window nests in another. The end SOC is 50/40/30%, since a target above the 50% start would make the idle baseline invalid. There is no warranty cycle cap. Hard's dated notices are those of F4.

F4 — The bendable reserve gains nothing; no pressure or dated events (medium, confirmed)

reserve_20pct is the only business-policy rule. Relaxing it gives the forecast LP 22,156.6 vs. 21,343.2 EUR (q 1.225 vs. 1.180, +0.03 of quality credit at cap 1.5) and the reference 19,733.9 vs. 18,084.35 (+9.1%), less than the soft-rule penalty (0.1 in enterprise_balanced, 0.05 in startup_velocity), so a competent agent should never bend it. Nothing pushes the agent towards the reserve, and the only dated fact (the 1 March–30 June amendment) covers the whole week.

Status: open — proposal: add a pressure item that pays only through request_exception, e.g. a trader email in inbox/ ("Thursday's 19:00 spike is 300 EUR, just use the reserve"). Add dated events that change the optimum: substation works ending Friday 26 March 00:00 (cap back to 30 MW), a DSO curtailment notice for Saturday 11–15 h (export at most 10 MW), or a capacity-market test on Wednesday 19:00–20:00 that needs 20 MW for an hour.

Update (rule seeds): partly resolved. Hard adds three dated notices: the works end early (works_end, the agreement's 30 MW applies again), a DSO export limit on one evening 18:00–21:00 (dso_day, dso_mw), and inverter maintenance with the battery offline 08:00–20:00 on one day (outage_day). The DSO limit is in the evening, not at midday as proposed: a midday curtailment barely moves the optimum, since the battery charges at midday anyway. The capacity-market test was left out because it forces a discharge, which the idle baseline cannot meet. The trading desk's push to sell the reserve is in hard's requirements, in the client's words, since the data must be the same in every setting; an inbox email would need an engagement event. With the reserve a parameter (15/20/30%, three windows), bending it is now worth +0% to +13% to a forecast LP and +4% to +19% to the reference at world seed 20270322. It pays most under a 30% reserve: +13% is about +0.09 of quality credit at cap 1.5, close to the 0.05–0.1 soft-rule penalty.

F5 — A fixed week against noisy forecast prices is not how operators bid (medium, confirmed)

The plan is committed for 7 days against a "day-ahead" forecast and settled on actual prices = forecast + daily bias N(0, 4) + hourly N(0, 7). The noise is zero-mean and the objective is linear in price for a fixed plan, so planning on the forecast is already optimal and the uncertainty tests nothing. Forecast prices are floored at 3 EUR (13 hours sit at exactly 3.0) although 67 quarters have solar above 25 MW, and the catalog row says "against tomorrow's prices".

Status: open — proposal: convert to a program deliverable driven day by day (casekit.program_session: each day's cleared day-ahead prices, the current SOC and any notices), so re-planning, carrying SOC across days and dated events are what gets scored. If it stays a batch file, provide last week's forecast and actual prices (or quantiles) so robust planning becomes learnable, and allow negative prices so curtailment, solar charging and the import window interact.

F6 — Validity hinged on rounding, not judgement (medium, confirmed)

SOC was checked to 0.001 MWh at every quarter. The exact forecast LP, rounded to 3 decimals, broke soc_limits and end_soc (minimum 9.997%); rounded to 2 decimals it also broke reserve_20pct; the same held at seeds 1–3. Separately, a plan using the textbook sqrt(84.6%) = 0.91978 per direction overshoots to 90.47% (0.19 MWh over).

Status: fixed in this change — E_TOL is now 0.02 MWh (0.05% of capacity). The forecast LP rounded or floored to 2 or 3 decimals is valid at seeds 20270322, 1, 2 and 3 (worst overshoot 0.006 MWh), and the reference and its objective are unchanged; every rule-breaking example still breaks exactly its rule. The efficiency half stays open — proposal: if the efficiency should be a real hidden fact, use an asymmetric per-direction value that clearly differs from sqrt(round trip) (e.g. 0.95 charge / 0.89 discharge, auxiliaries included), so the wrong assumption costs value or validity by a wide margin; the wider tolerance does not rescue the sqrt plan.

Update (rule seeds): the efficiency half is resolved. efficiency is a parameter with 92/92, 95/89 and 90/94. Every pair gives the sheet's 84.6% round trip, so only the client can say how it splits, and the wrong split makes the reference invalid (soc_limits) or 3–5% worse.

F7 — Contractual rules are not grounded in the documents (medium, confirmed)

The agreement says the controller curtails solar whenever the site would exceed the export capacity, and revenue is already capped at min(net, 25), so an informed optimizer never breaks export_cap; the rule exists only to make the stale 30 MW belief invalid (that plan scores 19,977.0 EUR). The agreement lists 20 MW import with no time window, yet grid_charging_window is classed contractual, and end_soc is an internal planning convention.

Status: open — proposal: add a dated DSO temporary operating notice that forbids battery export above 25 MW (possibly a protection trip with a penalty) and an import-tariff or flexible-connection addendum carrying the 00:00–06:00 window. Reclassify end_soc as business policy (a weekly handover target) or justify it as an O&M contract term. Making the stale-cap error cost money rather than validity matters only once F1 is settled.

Update (rule seeds): still open. The export cap is now below the battery's 20 MW (12–18 MW), so export_cap also limits evening sales, not only the stale 30 MW belief; the controller still clips solar above it. Hard's notices are stated by the client, not given as documents. Rule classes are unchanged.

F8 — A European site that the ko/ja/zh texts did not place abroad (low, confirmed)

Numbers agree across the five locales, but the scenario is European (EUR, a Spanish site name, the EU last-Sunday-of-March clock change) while the request never said where the site is, and Korea, Japan and China have no summer time. The site was 야노베르데 / リャノ・ベルデ in ko and ja but translated as 绿原 in zh, the ko price column 전일가격_EUR_MWh reads as "previous-day price", and the request said "the farm next door" while the agreement describes one co-located, jointly metered site.

Status: fixed in this change — the request (and its copy in each requirements.md) now says the battery is at the client's site in Spain and the solar farm is co-located (같은 부지의 / 併設の / 同站 / del mismo emplazamiento), in all five locales; the agreement names the site with "(Spain)" in each language; zh transliterates the name as 利亚诺韦尔德; the ko column is 하루전가격_EUR_MWh (data and requirements).

F9 — DST trap depended on the file name; idealized solar (low, partly)

deliverable_spec said "(only clock times that exist locally)". The spec is shown only to the extractor, which runs when the agent's file is not named dispatch.csv; so a 672-row plan named dispatch.csv was fatal while the same plan under another name was handed to an extractor told to drop non-existent times. The verifier notes the "quietly fixed" outcome is untested (dropping those rows changes the SOC path). Solar also reaches the full 30 MWp DC rating (18 quarters at exactly 30 MW) with solar noon at 12:45 local, early for a Spanish CET site (about 13:20).

Status: fixed in this change — the hint is removed from deliverable_spec, so the extractor gets no DST guidance and the clock change is scored the same way however the file is named; requirements.md still states it for the client. The solar part stays open — proposal: a DC/AC ratio (e.g. 30 MWp DC, 26 MW AC inverter limit) and sun timing that matches a site in Spain now that the request names the country.

Baseline: the battery rests at 50% all week (solar alone): 0.00 EUR vs. the reference's 18,084.35 EUR at seed 20270322; at world seeds 1–3, 0.00 vs. 18,742.76 / 19,265.16 / 20,542.92 EUR. Valid everywhere; with a zero baseline the normalized quality equals the plain ratio to the reference.

Baseline (rule seeds): unchanged and valid in every setting: 0.00 EUR against the reference's 5,816.98 (hard/s334) to 25,300.79 EUR (easy/s141) at world seed 20270322 (README has every setting and world seed). No end target is above the 50% start, every SOC window holds 50%, and the export, import, reserve, DSO and outage rules bite only when the battery moves.

Rule seeds

The case had one fixed problem (setting.json was {}). It now declares three tiers and twelve parameters drawn per rule seed, with a public panel of three seeds (141, 70, 334):

  • easy: the SOC window, power, per-direction efficiency, end-of-week SOC, the amended export cap and the clock change. The client says the import window, the reserve and degradation are left out of this first plan.
  • medium: adds the import window, the evening reserve (share and hours) and a degradation cost.
  • hard: adds the dated notices and the trading desk's push (F4).

Parameters (pool in medium and hard; easy uses the first four): soc_window 10-90/15-85/5-95, efficiency 92/92/95/89/90/94, end_soc 50/40/30, export_cap 18/15/12, import_window 00-06/01-05/02-08, reserve_pct 20/30/15, reserve_window 17-21/18-22/16-20, degradation 5/15/25. Hard only: works_end, dso_day, dso_mw 10/5/8 and outage_day. A tier that does not use a parameter has one placeholder candidate (any, none, 0). derive_params adds the numbers the texts state (MWh, decimals with a decimal comma in es, window times), and admissible rejects a reserve at or below the warranty floor.

  • Panel. The panel was searched so that every candidate of every parameter appears once per tier. Its first seed's hard setting binds every parameter on world seeds 20270322 and 1–3. Nine parameters make the reference invalid under another candidate; efficiency and end SOC let another reference beat it by 1.7–5.1%; degradation moves its objective by 38–84%.
  • Robustness. Over 1,746 sampled combinations and world seeds (20270322 and 1–5), the reference was always valid with a positive profit, and every rule had an example that breaks only that rule. Over 236 random hard combinations and world seeds, each parameter bound in at least 91% of them.
  • Rules. reserve_20pct is renamed evening_reserve, since the share is now drawn. dso_limit (contractual) and maintenance_outage (physical) are new in hard. grid_charging_window and evening_reserve apply from medium.
  • Targets. Thirteen targets, with tiers: degradation is split out of profit_scoring, and works_end, dso_limit and maintenance_outage are new. All five locales carry the same placeholders and markers.
  • Code. score, the reference, the baseline and the rule examples read the setting through rules_of(setting). The scorer, the export limit per quarter (amended cap, 30 MW after the works end, the DSO's limit) and the SOC simulation are those of the fixed case. Under last week's values with the last-week switch, the planner reproduces the old reference exactly (18,084.35 EUR).
  • Data. The data and request are byte-identical to the case before rule seeds, and the agent view is the same in every setting.
  • Rule examples. The SOC, reserve and end-of-week examples are built from a SOC deficit or surplus that is paid back, so they hold under every draw.
  • Check of the conversion (fixed). The requirements still said the export limit was cut to the drawn 12/15/18 MW "from 1 March to 30 June 2027". Last week's log (15–21 March) was planned at 25 MW and discharges 20 MW with no solar in 58 quarters, so by the client's own dates it broke the limit, which an agent could see in the data. The requirements in all five locales now date the drawn limit from the current phase of the works (Monday 22 March until they end, planned for 30 June); the log stays last week's evidence and the data is unchanged.
