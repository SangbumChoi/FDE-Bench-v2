At a glance

What the AI has to do

The operator of a battery at a solar-plus-storage site in Spain (40 MWh, 20 MW, sharing one grid connection and one meter with a 30 MWp solar farm) wants a plan for next week, Monday 22 to Sunday 28 March 2027: when to charge and when to discharge to make the most money. The AI hands in one file, dispatch.csv, with the charge and discharge power in MW for each of the week's 668 quarter-hours, in local time.

What the AI is given

  • The client's request: plan when the battery should charge and discharge next week "to make the most money". It names the five attached files and says nothing about limits or rules.
  • price_forecast.csv: the forecast market price for each hour of the week in EUR/MWh (167 hours, because 28 March has only 23). The plan is judged at these prices.
  • solar_forecast.csv: the solar farm's average output in MW for each quarter-hour of the week (668 rows), up to 30 MW at midday on sunny days.
  • battery_spec.csv: 40 MWh, 20 MW charge and 20 MW discharge, 84.6% round-trip efficiency, the manufacturer's 5%-95% state-of-charge range and the 30 MWp of solar on the same site.
  • connection_agreement.md: a summary of the 2024 grid agreement: the site may send up to 30 MW to the grid and draw up to 20 MW, solar and battery are metered together, solar above the limit is clipped automatically, all times are local. The 30 MW is out of date.
  • dispatch_log_last_week.csv: last week's plan (15-21 March, 672 quarter-hours) with charge, discharge and state of charge, made by the client's own planner with last week's values; the window, the efficiency split and the export limit always differ this week.

What the AI must find out by asking the client

  • How the 84.6% efficiency splits between charging and discharging: 94% and 90%, 95% and 89%, or 90% and 94%, depending on the version (never the even 92%/92% the log used). It decides how much energy each quarter-hour stores or uses up.
  • The state-of-charge range the warranty allows at every moment: 10-85%, 15-85% or 15-90% of 40 MWh, depending on the version (narrower than the spec sheet's 5-95%), and the level the week must end at: 50%, 40% or 30% (it starts at 50%).
  • The current export limit: substation works have cut the agreement's 30 MW to 18, 15 or 12 MW, depending on the version. While the battery discharges, solar + discharge − charge must stay within it; solar above it is clipped and lost unless the battery soaks it up.
  • On medium and hard: when the site may draw power from the grid (charge more than the solar output): only in quarter-hours starting 00:00-05:45, 01:00-04:45 or 02:00-07:45; the evening emergency reserve, 15%, 20% or 30% of charge kept every day, from 16:00-20:00, 17:00-21:00 or 18:00-22:00; and the degradation cost, 5, 15 or 25 EUR per MWh charged plus discharged (each depending on the version). On easy none of these applies.
  • On hard: three notices the files do not show, dated differently depending on the version. The substation works end early, so 30 MW applies again from Wednesday 24, Friday 26 or Saturday 27 March; the local grid operator (DSO) limits export to 10, 8 or 5 MW from 18:00 to 21:00 on Tuesday 23, Thursday 25 or Saturday 27 March; the battery is offline from 08:00 to 20:00 on Monday 22, Wednesday 24 or Friday 26 March. Selling the reserve at the evening peak, as the trading desk wants, needs an approved exception.
  • What the files already settle and the client confirms: money is settled per quarter-hour at the hourly price, all times are local and 28 March has no 02:00-02:45 (668 rows), solar and battery share one meter, and the week starts at 50%.

How it is scored

Scores 0 if the deliverable…

  • Does not have a row for each of the week's 668 quarter-hours, or puts power at 02:00-02:45 on 28 March, which the clock change skips.
  • Lets the state of charge leave the warranty range at any moment (worked out with the client's per-direction efficiencies), or ends the week below the required level.
  • Has a negative power or one above 20 MW, charges and discharges in the same quarter-hour, draws more than 20 MW from the grid, or, on medium and hard, draws grid power outside the off-peak hours.
  • Sends more than the export limit to the grid while the battery discharges: 12, 15 or 18 MW; on hard 30 MW once the works end, and 5, 8 or 10 MW during the DSO's evening hours.
  • On medium and hard, lets the state of charge fall below the evening reserve during its hours, unless the company policy lets this rule bend and the AI asked the client for an exception first.
  • On hard, charges or discharges during the 08:00-20:00 maintenance outage.

If it is valid, quality is…

Higher is better: the battery's expected profit in euros over the week, every quarter-hour settled at its hour's forecast price: the site's export income (counted up to the export limit) minus the cost of grid power, minus what the solar farm alone would have earned, and on medium and hard minus the degradation cost. The client's own way (score 0) is its planner run with this week's values: two cycles a day, room left at night for the midday solar, 1% kept inside every limit. The reference (score 1) is the best plan there is, the exact optimum of that profit under the client's rules, and earns 7-27% more than the planner depending on the version; no valid plan scores above 1.

The same in every case

  1. Valid or not. Breaking any rule above makes the deliverable invalid and the run scores 0. A rule the company policy lets bend counts only if the AI asked the client for an exception.
  2. Quality. The result is placed on a scale where the client's own way without an engineer is 0 and the reference solution is 1. Better than the reference goes above 1; full credit at the policy's cap (1.5).
  3. Final score. Quality, plus how well the AI asked (did its questions uncover the facts above?), how it communicated (the client's language, a NOTES.md) and how efficient it was, weighted by the company policy.
  4. Several versions. The data stays the same; only the client's exact values (a threshold, a unit, a date) change from one version of the case to another. A difficulty's score is the average over several versions, so memorising one answer does not help.

Case notes (README)

Battery Dispatch Operator — energy_utilities/battery_dispatch_operator

The operator of a 40 MWh / 20 MW battery at a solar-plus-storage site in Spain (Llano Verde: the battery and a 30 MWp solar farm share one point of connection and one meter) asks the agent to plan when the battery should charge and discharge next week, Monday 22 to Sunday 28 March 2027, "to make the most money". The deliverable is dispatch.csv (timestamp,charge_mw,discharge_mw), one row per local quarter-hour of the week. The plan is judged by its expected profit: every quarter settled at its hour's forecast price. The scorer simulates the state of charge quarter by quarter and checks every rule of the setting.

The case has settings: three tiers × a public panel of three rule seeds (141, 70, 334). The agent sees the same request and the same data in every setting (the validator checks that its prompt and workspace are byte-identical); a setting changes which rules apply and the operating values the client would have to tell the engineer. A rule seed draws each value on its own from a candidate pool, so any other seed is a private setting with its own ground truth. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

SOC is checked to within 0.02 MWh (0.05% of capacity), so a plan rounded to two or three decimals is not rejected for rounding alone. The file is read by what its columns say: the time column under any common name (else the first column), the charge and discharge columns by a word in any of the five languages, comma or semicolon separated (with decimal commas), timestamps with seconds, a T, a one-digit hour, a day-first (or month-first) date or a UTC offset (converted to local time); a row at 02:00–02:45 on 28 March that moves nothing is ignored. A file that cannot be read, or a quarter given twice, is fatal.

Quality is the battery's expected profit: per quarter, at the hour's forecast price, site export revenue (price × 0.25 × min(net, export limit)) minus grid import cost, minus what the solar farm alone would have earned (price × 0.25 × min(solar, export limit)), minus degradation EUR per MWh of charge plus discharge. The export limit of a quarter is export_cap, 30 MW after the works end and dso_mw in the DSO's hours. The profit at the week's actual prices (the forecast plus a daily bias and hourly noise) is reported as a metric only.

Situations present

  • Stale documents. The agreement says 30 MW and the log ran at 25; substation works cut the limit to 12–18 MW. In hard the works end early and the agreement is right again from a given day. The sheet's 84.6% round trip and 5–95% window are not the client's model.
  • Units and the clock. Prices are hourly but settlement is quarter-hourly. Clocks go forward on Sunday 28 March, so the week has 668 quarters; power at 02:00–02:45 that day breaks complete_plan.
  • Clipped solar. With a 12–18 MW export limit and 30 MWp of solar, much of the midday output is clipped unless the battery absorbs it; that energy is free, and the night fill must leave room for it.
  • Off-peak import window (medium, hard). Charging from solar is allowed any time, from the grid only in the window. At the forecast prices a night charge rarely pays for its losses and degradation, so the window matters mostly for validity (GT.md, part 1).
  • Dated notices (hard). Three changes for named hours that the documents do not show.
  • Pressure on a bendable rule (medium, hard: the reserve; hard: the trading desk's push). Bending the reserve is worth 0.00–0.90 of the quality scale to the optimum on the public panel (nothing where a 15% reserve from 16:00 to 20:00 does not bind); a plan at the reference's level gains nothing, since the cap is the best plan that keeps the rule.

Twelve clarification targets, each exactly one drawn parameter: efficiency, soc_window, end_soc and export_cap in every tier (4); grid_charging, reserve_level, reserve_hours and degradation from medium (8); works_end, dso_day, dso_limit and maintenance_outage in hard (12).

Reference and baseline

The reference is the plan of greatest expected profit under the setting's rules, exactly (optimum in case.py). The problem is a linear program with one state, the stored energy: a quarter's profit is a concave piecewise-linear function of the energy it moves (charging costs the price plus degradation, except solar above the export limit, which is free; discharging earns the price less degradation, up to the export limit), so the best profit still to come is concave piecewise-linear too. A backward pass merges each quarter's segments into it and cuts it at the SOC bounds; a forward pass picks each move. It is pure Python, takes about 0.05 s, and matches scipy/HiGHS to 1e-9 EUR on the public settings of four worlds. No valid plan earns more (objective_bound), so quality_cap is 1.0.

The baseline (quality 0) is the client's own way: its planner run with the setting's values (requirements.md, "How we run the battery today"). Every day it fills up in the cheapest night quarters (inside the import window when there is one), leaving room for the midday solar (always for the clipped part, the whole refill when daylight is cheaper than night); sells in the dearest morning quarters (06:00–11:00); refills in the 16 cheapest sunny quarters, soaking up clipped solar; and sells in the dearest quarters from 15:00, each sale only above the buy price over the round trip plus twice the degradation. It keeps 0.4 MWh inside the warranty window and above the reserve, and never sells below the reserve until its hours are over, the morning sale included (so a cloudy afternoon cannot leave the evening short). It is valid in every setting.

F1 — Reference far below a plain LP; the task is convex, not combinatorial (high, confirmed)

The reference is a greedy two-cycles-a-day heuristic at 18,084.35 EUR. A linear program on the forecast prices (2,004 variables, about 3 s with HiGHS) reaches 21,343.2 EUR at actual prices (q = 1.180), and a perfect-foresight LP 23,099.9 (q = 1.277), so the reference sits at 85% of what a careful agent can reach. The review was written against a 1.0 quality cap, under which every valid LP saturated; on the new scale (baseline 0, cap 1.5) the forecast LP earns 1.180 / 1.5 = 0.79 of the quality credit, so the problem is now the opposite: the cap is out of reach for any plan made from the forecast. The LP never charges and discharges in one quarter, so the either/or rule never binds and task_type: Combinatorial Optimization does not fit.

Status: open — proposal: keep the heuristic as the reference and set quality_cap in case.yaml to the forecast LP's normalized quality (1.18 at the default seed, 1.11–1.16 at seeds 1–3; 1.15 would be a fair single value), so the best attainable plan earns full credit. The alternative, making the forecast LP the reference, needs an LP solver in case.py (scipy is not a dependency today).
