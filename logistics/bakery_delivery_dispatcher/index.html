At a glance

What the AI has to do

A bakery's central kitchen delivers bread trays to its cafés every morning with four vans. The AI plans Monday morning's routes: which van serves which café, in what order, and when each van leaves, so the cafés get their trays on time and the drivers are out as little as possible. The result is routes.json: for each van used, its departure time and its cafés in visiting order.

What the AI is given

  • The client's request: three sentences asking who should drive where tomorrow (Monday) morning with the four vans, "so the cafés get their trays in time and the drivers aren't out longer than they need to be". It says nothing about limits, rules or driving times.
  • cafes.csv: the 18 cafés, C01 to C18, with map coordinates in km (x_km, y_km), the trays each one gets (8 to 29, 356 in all), premium or standard, opening time (06:00 to 08:00; the premium cafés open first, except the East Lake café, C07), latest delivery time (deliver_by, 30 to 60 minutes after opening) and whether it is a partner-brand café (C05, C10 and C16). One café is closed this week, but the file does not say which.
  • vans.csv: the four vans V1 to V4, each listed with max_trays 120; only V4 is refrigerated.

What the AI must find out by asking the client

  • How to turn the map into driving times: the coordinates are km from the kitchen at (0, 0), road distance is 1.3 times the straight line, and vans average 25 km/h all morning. On hard there is a morning rush instead: 30 km/h until 06:30, 07:00 or 07:30, depending on the version, and 20 km/h from then on, even for a van already on the road.
  • How a stop works: unloading takes 4 minutes plus 0.2 minutes per tray, a van that arrives before the café opens waits until it opens, and each café gets all its trays in one visit from one van.
  • The drivers' shift: vans may leave from 05:00 and must be back at the kitchen by 10:00 on easy and medium, and by 09:00, 09:30 or 10:00 on hard, depending on the version. On hard, V2 is in the workshop on Monday and cannot leave before 06:40.
  • This week's tray limit per van: 100, 110 or 120, depending on the version (always 110 on hard). The file's 120 is what the racks hold, but the new, deeper crates can lower it.
  • Which café is closed this week for renovation and must get nothing, depending on the version: South Hill café (C11), North Station café (C03) or Maple Row café (C14).
  • Which vans may serve the partner-brand cafés: on easy any van (the partner brings its chilled products itself this week); on medium and hard only the refrigerated V4.
  • Whether premium cafés must come before standard ones on every route: never on easy (the habit is suspended); on medium and hard it holds in some versions and not in others. On hard, the East Lake café (C07) manager's demand to be the very first stop, before 06:30, is not a rule.
  • What lateness costs: a late delivery is accepted, but each minute after the café's deliver_by time costs as much as 3 minutes of van time.

How it is scored

Scores 0 if the deliverable…

  • Cannot be read: no routes.json, a van or café id that is not in the data, two routes for one van, or a departure time not written as HH:MM.
  • Puts more trays on a van than this week's limit (100, 110 or 120; 110 on hard).
  • Sends a van out before 05:00 or brings one back to the kitchen after the end of the shift (10:00; on hard 09:00, 09:30 or 10:00); on hard, also sends V2 out before 06:40.
  • Delivers to the closed café, leaves an open café out, or serves a café more than once.
  • On medium and hard, sends a van other than V4 to a partner-brand café (C05, C10, C16).
  • On medium and hard, in the versions where the premium-first habit holds, visits a premium café after a standard one on any route, unless the company policy lets this rule bend and the AI asked the client for an exception first.

If it is valid, quality is…

Lower is better: the total minutes all vans are out, from leaving the kitchen to getting back (waiting for a café to open counts), plus 3 times each minute a café is reached after its deliver_by time. The client's own way (score 0) is the dispatcher's hand plan: he gives each café, in the order they open, to the van that can be there first (or splits the map into slices, whichever plan is cheaper), puts each van's cafés in their best order and sends each van out to reach its first café as it opens (about 515 to 830 minutes in the versions on this page); the reference solution (score 1) is a computer search that keeps moving cafés between routes and picks each van's best departure to the minute (about 445 to 550 minutes), within 3.4% of the proven optimum where that is known.

  1. Valid or not. Breaking any rule above makes the deliverable invalid and the run scores 0. A rule the company policy lets bend counts only if the AI asked the client for an exception.
  2. Quality. The result is placed on a scale where the client's own way without an engineer is 0 and the reference solution is 1. Better than the reference goes above 1; full credit at the policy's cap (1.5).

  4. Several versions. The data stays the same; only the client's exact values (a threshold, a unit, a date) change from one version of the case to another. A difficulty's score is the average over several versions, so memorising one answer does not help.

Case notes (README)

Bakery Delivery Dispatcher — logistics/bakery_delivery_dispatcher

A bakery's central kitchen delivers bread trays to its cafés every morning with four vans. The client asks the agent to plan tomorrow's (Monday) routes "so the cafés get their trays in time and the drivers aren't out longer than they need to be". The deliverable is routes.json: for each van used, a departure time (HH:MM, 05:00 if left out) and the cafés in visiting order. The evaluator drives the plan through the client's travel times and scores it between the dispatcher's own hand plan (0) and the reference (1).

The case has settings: three tiers × rule seeds (DESIGN.md, Settings). The data and the request are the same in every setting; only the client's hidden rules, and so the right plan, change. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md). It is also the framework's test fixture (tests/conftest.py, tests/test_validator.py), so its validation has to stay fast. It is audited (audited: true): see "Ground truth audit" below, GT.md and CLIENT_REVIEW.md.

What an agent must ask

The clarification targets, by tier (locales/<lang>/targets.yaml), one fact each:

  • Every tier: road distance (road_distance), unloading time (service_time), the closed café (closed_cafe), and the tray limit when it is below the racks' 120 (tray_limit; hard always 110).
  • easy and medium: the constant 25 km/h (speed).
  • easy: that any van may serve the partner cafés this week (partner_this_week).
  • medium and hard: the cold chain (cold_chain), whether the premium-first habit holds (premium_first) and what a late minute costs (lateness: 3 van minutes; deliver_by is not a hard deadline).
  • hard: that there is a morning rush (rush_hour), when it starts (rush_start), the end of the shift (shift_end), V2 in the workshop until 06:40 (workshop) and that the C07 manager's demand is not a rule (manager_pressure).

So 5 or 6 targets in easy, 7 or 8 in medium, 12 in hard. Reference and baseline

The reference (_plan) starts from the greedy cheapest-insertion construction (partner cafés first when the cold chain applies, then by tier, biggest first, with retries from each workable order of the partner cafés and from shuffled orders), the tier's fallback without the closed café and the dispatcher's plan; polishes each by local search (relocation, exchanges between routes, reversals within a route); runs ruin and recreate three times (300 rounds each, one with a cooling threshold) with departures on a 5-minute grid; and polishes the best plan again with departures to the minute. It is computed once per world and rule values, and is within 1.2% of the proven optimum in every easy setting, 3.2% in every medium one and 1.6% in every hard one (relative to the reference's objective; GT.md, part 4).

The baseline (baseline_solution) is the dispatcher's own hand plan, which the requirements describe: he draws up the morning three ways and drives the cheapest. Two go through the cafés in the order they open, or in the order of deliver_by (partner cafés first, to V4, when the cold chain applies; premium cafés before standard ones while the habit holds), giving each café to the van that can be there first, at the end of its round, if that van keeps the tray limit and is back in time; a café that fits at the end of no round is squeezed in where it adds the least driving, if need be after moving one café of that round to another, and one that still fits nowhere is given out first. The third splits the map into four slices round the kitchen. He then puts each round in its best order and sends each van out to reach its first café as it opens (V2 in hard not before 06:40). He knows every rule and the travel times. The first version's baseline sent every van out at 05:00 along a slice of the map, 1.2 to 3.8 times the reference: a strawman that left a plan 20% worse than the reference at quality 0.9.
