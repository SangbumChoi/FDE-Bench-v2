At a glance

What the AI has to do

A grocery chain with five stores places its orders from a daily forecast. The AI works out, for each of its 12 products, how much each store should order for every day from Monday 2 to Sunday 15 November 2026, and delivers forecast.csv: one row per store, product and day (840 rows) with the quantity to order, in each product's current sales unit. The stores order exactly that number, so the file is scored as an order plan: by what it is expected to cost, not against one November that happened to turn out a certain way.

What the AI is given

  • The client's request: three sentences asking how much of each product each of the five stores will sell every day for the two weeks from Monday 2 November, because "we place our orders from these numbers". It says nothing about what a wrong number costs, promotions, holidays, stockouts or how the numbers are judged.
  • sales_history.csv: two years of daily sales (4 November 2024 to 1 November 2026): units sold per store, product and trading day, that day's promotion discount (0 = none) and a stockout flag (yes on 7,175 rows, about one in six), plus 1,255 customer-return rows with negative units; 43,687 rows in all. Bread rolls (I07) are counted per roll until 31 May 2026 and per pack of 6 from 1 June, which the file does not say.
  • promo_plan.csv: the 14 promotions planned for the two weeks (store, product, start and end date, 10, 20 or 30% off): 7 on beverages, 3 on bakery, 2 on dairy and 2 on fresh produce.
  • holidays.csv: the 12 public holidays of the past two years (Christmas Day, New Year's Day, Labour Day and a spring, summer and autumn holiday, each twice). None falls in the two weeks, and the one-off regional holiday in November is not listed.
  • stores.csv and items.csv: the five stores (four supermarkets and the small Old Town store, S4) and the 12 products in four categories (fresh produce I01–I03, dairy I04–I06, bakery I07–I09, beverages I10–I12), each with its sales unit, such as kg, bottle or pack of 6.
  • mail/: one e-mail of 28 October from the manager of the Central Plaza store (S1) to the ordering desk: whatever the numbers are, add 20% on top for his store.

What the AI must find out by asking the client

  • What the numbers are used for: each store orders exactly the number in the file, and nobody adds a safety margin. So the client needs the quantity to order, not the expected sales.
  • What a wrong number costs: a unit a customer wanted and did not find costs about 5, 12 or 30 times (depending on the version) what a unit left over at closing costs.
  • What the stockout flag means: on those days the shelf ran empty, sales were capped by the stock and demand was higher, so they are not normal sales. Returns (the rows with negative units) are not demand and are left out.
  • What to do with the promotions in promo_plan.csv. On easy, leave them out: the category team adds them afterwards. On medium and hard they belong in the order (start and end dates both count), with the extra sales learned from past promotions by category and discount; depending on the version, the November promotions are also in the printed flyer and bring 2 or 3 times the extra sales of a past promotion, or there is no flyer.
  • A one-off regional holiday that is not in holidays.csv: it falls on Monday 9, Wednesday 11 or Friday 13 November, depending on the version (the city's anniversary); all stores open, and demand rises as on past holidays, most for bakery and beverages.
  • On hard only: on Friday 30 October, after promo_plan.csv was exported, all promotions of one category were cancelled and will not run: beverages, dairy or bakery, depending on the version.
  • Whether to follow the Central Plaza manager's e-mail: no. It is his own wish, not company policy; his store is ordered like every other.
  • The client's habit for fresh produce and dairy (I01–I06): never order fewer than 4, 6 or 12 units for a day the store is open, depending on the version, so the shelves never look empty.
  • The file: exactly one row per store, product and day, 840 in all, also for the Old Town store's Sundays (it is closed, so 0) and for bread rolls (I07), which are judged on hard only, where their history before 1 June 2026 must be divided by 6.

How it is scored

Scores 0 if the deliverable…

  • Cannot be read: no forecast.csv, no column with the quantity (it is read from order_qty, else from a forecast column, else from the file's one other column of numbers), a store or product that is not in the data, a date outside 2–15 November 2026, or a quantity that is not a number.
  • Misses a row or has one twice: every store, product and day needs exactly one row, 840 in all, bread rolls (I07) included even where they are not judged.
  • Has a negative order quantity.
  • Orders anything but 0 for the Old Town store (S4) on a Sunday (8 and 15 November).
  • Orders a fresh produce or dairy product (I01–I06) below 4, 6 or 12 units (depending on the version) for a day the store is open, unless the company policy lets this rule bend and the AI asked the client for an exception first.

If it is valid, quality is…

Lower is better: for each store, the units expected to be left over plus 5, 12 or 30 times (depending on the version) the units expected to be short, as a share of its expected demand over its products and days, averaged over the five stores. "Expected" means over how demand varies from day to day, which the benchmark knows exactly, so the score is not one lucky or unlucky November and the best possible plan is known. The client's own way (score 0) is the store managers' habit: the average of the last eight weeks' sales of that weekday, days the store ran out left aside, plus a cushion of 10, 20 or 30%, 2% more per point of discount on promotion days and a public holiday ordered like a Saturday (a cost of about 35% to 54% of demand in the versions on this page). The reference solution (score 1) learns each store and product's level, the weekday pattern, the season and the effect of past promotions and holidays from the days the shelf did not run empty, then orders the quantity that balances the two costs (about 28% to 39%). The best possible plan scores about 1.04.

  2. Quality. The result is placed on a scale where the client's own way without an engineer is 0 and the reference solution is 1. Better than the reference goes above 1; full credit at the policy's cap (1.5).

Case notes (README)

Grocery Demand Forecaster — retail/grocery_demand_forecaster

A regional grocery chain with five stores asks the agent to forecast "how much of each product each of our five stores will sell every day for the next two weeks, starting Monday 2 November", because it places its orders from these numbers. What the request does not say is that the stores order exactly the number they are given. So the deliverable, forecast.csv (store_id,item_id,date,order_qty: one row for every store S1–S5 × item I01–I12 × day from 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-15, 840 rows, in each item's current sales unit), is an order plan, and it is scored as one: by what it is expected to cost the client, not against one November that happened.

This is the benchmark's forecasting case, and it follows the rules for such cases (docs/DIRECTION.md, section 5):

  • Expected loss, not one draw. The world knows the demand distribution it draws from, so the scorer computes the plan's expected cost under it, exactly. No realized future, no luck, and the best possible plan is known.
  • The client's decision picks the loss. A unit left over costs one unit's worth; a unit a customer wanted and did not find costs lost_sale_cost times as much. That ratio is a client fact, and it changes the best number.
  • Rules constrain decisions, not forecasts. The display minimum, the closed store and the complete file are rules on the order. No forecast is "invalid" for being low: the model family is the agent's own business.
  • Elicitation is about the target. What the number is used for, demand or recorded sales, the calendar (holiday, promotions, the flyer, a cancellation), and whose word counts (the store manager's e-mail).

The case has settings: three tiers × rule seeds (DESIGN.md, Settings). The data and the request are the same in every setting; only the client's hidden facts, and so the right plan, change. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

  • sales_history (43,687 rows, 2024-11-04 to 2026-11-01, 104 weeks): one sales row per store, item and trading day with units_sold, promo_discount_pct (0 = no promotion) and a stockout flag (7,175 stockout rows, 17% of the sales rows and 10% to 31% by item, where recorded sales are 30–85% of demand), plus 1,255 separate customer-return rows with negative units (−2,097 units against 1,943,585 sold).   • holidays: 12 past public holidays (Christmas, New Year's Day, a spring, summer and autumn holiday, Labour Day, each twice). None falls in the horizon, and the one-off holiday (on the drawn date) is not listed.

The world is a multiplicative model per store × item × day (store scale and trend, category weekday profile and yearly season, promotion lift by category and discount, holiday lift by category) with lognormal day noise (σ = 0.07) and Poisson counts. Stockouts happen with an item's own probability (8% to 30%), plus 20 points on promotion days, independently of the day's demand; on a stockout day the till records 30–85% of demand. The horizon has no realized future: a day's demand in a cell is that same law around the cell's mean, with the holiday on the drawn date and, in medium and hard, the promotions that run (the flyer multiplies their extra demand).

The request names no file format, and the agent is told to infer one, so the header decides nothing: the quantity is read from order_qty, else from a forecast column, else from the file's one other column of numbers, and the key columns may carry the names the client's data gives them in any of the five languages (store_id or 점포코드). Quality (lower is better): per store, Σ (expected units left over + lost_sale_cost × expected units short) ÷ Σ expected demand over its judged items and days, averaged over the five stores. The expectation is exact: the scorer integrates the day noise (16 Gauss-Hermite nodes) and sums the counts of the demand law above. Demand is without the promotions in easy; I07 is judged in hard only.

Reference and baseline

The reference is a model of expected demand per cell, from the history only: stockout days and return rows left out, I07's history before 1 June 2026 divided by 6; a weekday profile per category, pooled over its items, stores and both years; a level per store × item from the last twelve weeks of usable days without promotion or holiday, carried into November by last year's change of the category's level; a promotion lift per category and discount and a holiday lift per category, both against the local level; and an estimate of the day noise. Then the setting: the promotions this tier has (lift × the flyer's multiple on the extra part, none for the cancelled category), the holiday lift on the drawn day, and the order: the lost_sale_cost / (1 + lost_sale_cost) quantile of the demand the model predicts for the cell; S4's Sundays at 0 and the display minimum on core items.

The baseline is the store managers' habit, what the client does without an engineer: for every item the average of what the till recorded on that weekday in the last eight weeks, days the store ran out left aside, plus a cushion of 10, 20 or 30% at a cost ratio of 5, 12 or 30; 2% more per point of discount, times the flyer's multiple, on a promotion day; the holiday ordered like a Saturday; S4's Sundays at 0 and the display minimum. The managers know their business (what is on promotion, the flyer, the holiday, the cancellation), not statistics. The habit is taken at its fairest, so that the scale's zero is not a strawman: over the measured settings of the public world and world seeds 1 and 2 these cushions cost it at most 0.002 more than the flat cushion that costs it least at each ratio, and no other window or cushion lifts it by half the scale (a year's window at most 0.33, ten points more cushion at most 0.16). With a four-week window, as the audit first had it, the average rested on two to four days: the scale was up to 120% wide and a year's window alone scored up to 0.59. The first baseline repeated last week's sales and knew none of the client's facts; an engineer who ignored them could beat it by modelling alone.

The best plan (best_known_solution = objective_bound, a proven optimum) orders every cell's cost-minimizing quantity under the true demand distribution. It scores 1.03 to 1.06 (median 1.040), so quality_cap is 1.05 (the median rounded up to the next 0.01) and the reference is 1.2% to 2.2% above the optimum.

Literal solution

literal_solution is the request done as written: the reference's model fitted to sales as the till recorded them (stockout days in, returns netted), with the promotion plan at the lift past promotions show and the holidays on file (none in the horizon), I07's history as recorded, the store manager's 20% for Central Plaza, and the forecast itself handed in as the number (a forecast column, which the stores order as it is): no cushion and no display minimum. It is the same file in every setting and is invalid in all 9 public settings: 118 to 136 core-item orders fall below the client's display minimum. The column name is not what fails it: a forecast column is read as the order. The next most natural literal readings fail the same way, in all 45 audited settings and the nine of round 2026-11: the e-mail not followed, stockout days left out, bread rolls in packs, the numbers rounded to whole units (each invalid as delivered and 0.00 with the rule relaxed).

Rule seeds

The case moved from one fixed problem to the rule-seed contract (DESIGN.md, Settings): three tiers in case.yaml and five parameters that a rule seed draws, each on its own, from a pool per tier. The data and request.md are byte-identical in every setting and to the case before the conversion; the requirements, targets, rules and the truth the forecast is scored against follow the tier and the drawn values.

  • Tiers. Easy: the base forecast, scored against base till sales (net of returns, stockout days as recorded, without the promotion and holiday effects); the requirements say in the client's words that the category team adds the promotions and the holiday itself, that stockout correction comes in a later round and that bread rolls (I07) are not judged this round (their rows stay in the file). Medium adds the promotion plan (with the flyer's multiple where drawn) and the one-off holiday on its drawn date, still against till sales and without I07. Hard is scored against customer demand (stockout days censored, returns not demand), judges I07 (its unit change) and adds the dated cancellation of 30 October.
  • Parameters. min_display (6, 4, 8: the display floor, rule min_display_stock), holiday_date (none in easy; 2026-11-11, 2026-11-13, 2026-11-09), promo_uplift (1 in easy; 1, 1.5, 2: the flyer's multiple of a past promotion's extra sales), promo_cancelled (none in easy and medium; beverages, dairy, bakery in hard) and short_weight (1, 2, 3: a unit forecast too low counts that many times a unit too high in the store's WAPE). Labels word the dates, multiples and categories in each locale. Public panel: rule seeds 3, 25 and 58, which show every candidate of every pool; seed 3 draws the binding check's start (hard/s3).
  • Truth. build_world keeps the history and the plan exactly as before and then draws, per horizon cell, the day noise, a Poisson quantile, the stockout draw, the share sold on a stockout day and a return draw (common random numbers). truth(world, rules) turns them into the setting's actuals: demand (hard) or till sales net of returns (easy, medium) under that setting's mean (promotions with the flyer and the cancellation, the holiday on its date). Scoring stays the store-mean WAPE against that one realization, with the error weight.
  • Reference and baseline. The reference keeps its weekday-level model, now with promotion lifts per category and discount and holiday lifts per category, fitted to till sales in easy and medium and to demand in hard, and forecasts the weight's quantile of its residual ratios before the floor. The baseline stays the seasonal naive forecast with the drawn floor. Baseline ÷ reference at the case seed: 1.64–1.98 (easy), 1.65–2.47 (medium), 1.95–3.20 (hard).
  • Binding. From hard/s3 every parameter changes the answer: the floor of 8 makes the reference invalid (and a floor of 4 lets the reference improve by 4.9%), another holiday date moves its objective by 28–33%, a flyer by 3–11%, another cancelled category by 6–17%, another error weight by 45–91%. On world seeds 1–20 the strongest alternative of each parameter makes the reference invalid, or moves or beats it by at least 5%.
  • Targets. 5 in easy (base_forecast is new), 7 in medium (promo_flyer new; the holiday target is now one_off_holiday with the drawn date), 11 in hard (promo_revision new; stockout_censoring, returns and unit_change_i07 are hard only). wape_metric states the weight and what the actuals are per tier.
  • Explainer and translations. locales/<lang>/explainer.yaml (zh, ko, ja, es) has the three tiers and the floor rule with {{min_display}}. docs/<lang>/README.md and docs/<lang>/REVIEW.md still describe the case without settings and are to be retranslated.
  • No test hard-codes this case; python -m pytest -q passes unchanged.
  • Check of the conversion (fixed). The plan's 14 promotions are a random sample of store × item pairs, and on some world seeds a category the cancellation can draw has none (no dairy promotion on world seed 6, no beverages on 8). A hard setting that draws that category then cancels nothing: the client's 30 October message is about promotions that do not exist, and the parameter changes nothing there (the validator's binding check still passes, since another category binds). build_world now makes sure every category of the promo_cancelled pool has a promotion in the plan: a plan that lacks one moves one promotion of its most promoted category (same store, dates and discount) to an item of the missing category, with its own random stream. The stored world and world seeds 1–5 already cover every category, so their data and answers are unchanged.

Ground truth audit

The case owner's principles for forecasting cases (docs/DIRECTION.md, section 5) and the audit contract (DESIGN.md, Ground truth audit) were applied together. audit-case now passes with no finding and no waiver; audited: true.

What was wrong
  • The literal forecast was one exception away from a good score. It broke only the display floor, a bendable rule; with the floor relaxed it scored 0.42–0.95 in every public setting, and with the floor kept (the "literal plus one rule" near miss) 0.42–0.86.
  • Open shortcuts. The median forecast with every other fact right scored 0.74–1.00; the literal forecast with a blanket 20% on top 0.00–0.78.
  • A rule on a forecast. The display floor made a forecast invalid for being low, although forecasting models differ and none is "the" answer.
  • One draw. Quality was the weighted WAPE against one realized horizon, so skill and luck were mixed and the best achievable score was unknown (the true generator mean reached 1.07).
  • Facts that changed nothing. Returns (under 0.002), bread rolls' unit change (0.001–0.009), stockout days (0.06–0.07, hard only); wape_metric bundled the error weight with what the actuals were.
  • A baseline that knew nothing. Last week's sales repeated, so a competent model beat it by modelling alone and the client's facts carried little of the scale.

Adversarial check

A second engineer checked the audit by running code against it. What held, what did not, and what changed.

Held.

  • The score is exact. The expected cost of six plans (reference, baseline, literal, best plan, the reference jittered by ±15%, a flat 40 units) in easy/s3, medium/s58 and hard/s25 was recomputed with independent code: the cell means re-derived from the world's constants, the day noise integrated on a 4,001-point grid, and a Monte Carlo of 200,000 draws per cell with the generator's own recipe. score() agrees with the quadrature to 1.4 × 10⁻⁵ or better (the 16 Gauss-Hermite nodes) and with the Monte Carlo within 1.2 standard errors in all 18 comparisons. The 35,257 history rows without a stockout have mean −0.01 and standard deviation 1.00 when standardized by the scoring law, so the law that scores is the law the data was drawn from.
  • The bound is the optimum. Per cell, no order on a 0.25 grid costs less than the bound's (45 settings, the scorer's cost and an independent one on sampled cells), and 900 random valid perturbations of the best plan all cost more.
  • The agent's view is byte-identical over 78 settings (public, round and 20 other rule seeds) in each of the five locales; the five locales' data hold the same numbers, dates and ids; and the data is built before any setting is drawn, so no file can show a drawn value. The days the holiday can fall on look like any other in past years.
  • The never-asks solver is a genuine attempt. Without the first grant (it hands in the expected demand) it earns 0.00 in every tier; without the second (it follows the e-mail) 0.03 / 0.08 / 0.07 (0.19 / 0.24 / 0.26 on the first baseline). Other compromise ratios, display minimums and holiday hedges were tried: over all 27 / 81 / 243 combinations of a tier's candidates the best of them (a ratio of 20 instead of 14) earns 0.113 / 0.181 / 0.117 against its 0.099 / 0.167 / 0.099, and a planner that scores every candidate plan against its own model, by the ratio of the best cost to the plan's cost over all combinations, picks the solver's own ratio and minimum. A plan found with hindsight (the true demand and the true scale of every combination) earned 0.01 to 0.04 more on the first baseline. It was left as it is.
  • The literal solution is not bent to fail. Following the e-mail helps it (Central Plaza gets a cushion); without the e-mail, with stockout days left out, with bread rolls in packs or rounded to whole units it is invalid as delivered and 0.00 with the rule relaxed in all 54 settings tried. Its forecast header is read as the order.
  • The locales agree on every placeholder, marker, code, date and number of two digits or more, line by line; the differences are single digits one language writes as words.

Still open
  • Stockouts are independent of the day's demand, so leaving flagged days out is unbiased (F5). The history's stockout rates (10% to 31% by item) are also the same whatever cushion the client says the managers add.
  • One cost ratio for every product. Fresh produce that is thrown away and water that keeps do not cost the same when left over. With per-category costs a flat cushion would fail; today a flat cushion of the right size on an otherwise right forecast reaches 0.69–0.96.
  • The managers' cushion follows the cost ratio (habit_cushion) and the requirements quote it, so a client asked how the stores order today hints at the cushion's size.
  • Bending never pays for a plan at the cap (F4).
  • The cost-ratio diagnostic reads the cushion, not the ratio: a plan sized for the right ratio with one flat percentage, or with twice the day noise, is called not enforced where it lands on the neighbouring candidate.
  • The complete grid is a hard gate: a plan that leaves out S4's Sunday rows is invalid, although the order there would be 0. The request asks for every store and every day, and the client says the ordering system needs the rows; it stays as it was before the audit.
