What the agent is asked

The client's request, the only text the agent gets up front. It is the same in every setting.

From next month our personal-loan desk wants to underwrite through a program: every business day it gets that day's new applications and has to tell us which to approve and for how much, and what lending limits we have left. Could you build it? Past loans with their outcomes, the bureau data and existing debts of those borrowers (an export of our loan database), our product sheet and one pilot day (what the program receives, what our pilot answered and the e-mails we got that day) are attached.

Deliverable spec (the program interface)

A program/ directory with underwriter.py defining on_day(day). The engine loads it once and calls it once per
business day of the hidden period, in order, in the same process (module-level state survives between calls);
10 s per call, Python standard library only, no network and no access to the data files at run time.
day = {"date", "day_index", "days_in_period", "period_end", "applications": [...], "notices": [...],
"book": {"loans": [...], "referred": [...]}} exactly as in the pilot-day input file.
Return {"decisions": [{"application_id": str, "decision": "approve" | "decline" | "refer",
"amount": int (approved amount; 0 unless approve), "reason_code": str (on every decline; "" otherwise)}],
"limit_table": {"customers": {customer_id: int}, "daily_capacity_remaining": int,
"period_budget_remaining": int or null}} as in the pilot-day output file.
reason_code is one of DELINQUENCY | LOW_SCORE | SPLIT_APPLICATION | LIMIT_EXCEEDED | CREDIT_RISK.

Settings: one dataset, 9 ground truths

The agent sees the same request and data in every setting. The tier (= difficulty) decides which rules apply; the rule seed draws each rule parameter, independently of the others, from its candidates below. Each difficulty is reported as the mean over rule seeds: the public ones below, or private seeds that draw from the same candidates.

Case notes (README)

Loan Underwriting Specialist — finance/loan_underwriting_specialist

A lender's personal-loan desk wants to underwrite day by day through a program. Every business day new applications for its one unsecured product (PL36: 36 months, 11.9% a year, 1,000–50,000 per customer) arrive; the program decides each one (approve / decline / refer, an amount, a reason code) and reports the day's limit table: what each customer may still borrow and what is left of the bank's daily capacity and period budget. The agent delivers program/underwriter.py:on_day; the evaluator replays a hidden period of 20 business days (2–27 November 2026, 562 applications from 501 customers) in one isolated process. The key of the case is keeping every approval within the day's limits and getting each day's limit table right. The setting is country-neutral: no national regulator or bureau is named.

The agent sees 3,000 past funded loans with their default outcomes (an SQL export of the loan database), the product sheet, and one pilot day: an input exactly as the engine sends it (8 applications, one notice of each kind, a branch note), the answer the client's pilot program gave under the pilot's interim rules (format only), and that day's notices and branch note as the e-mails the desk received (see Data). The case has settings on one world: three tiers, and a rule seed that draws the client's ten rule parameters from their candidate pools (see Rule seeds); the public panel is rule seeds 30, 525 and 1043 (nine public settings), and any other int is a private seed. The history, the hidden period, every application, branch note and notice are the same in every setting, and the agent's view (request, data, deliverable spec) is byte-identical (checked by the validator). Only the hidden requirements and the evaluator differ. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

The daily flow

day 0 … 19:   engine builds the day's input  ──►  on_day(day)  ──►  decisions + limit table
              (applications, notices, book of                      │
               earlier approvals and referrals)                    ▼
                                         evaluator: rules on the day's decisions (customer limit, daily cap,
                                         budget, codes, …), the true expected profit of each approval, the
                                         table against the closing limits; approvals update the book

The program keeps its own state (one process, module variables survive); the input's book also lists every approval so far and the applications whose latest decision is refer. Approvals are final and paid out the same day, so an over-limit approval cannot be undone by a later call.

The limit table and the score

Customer limit of customer c on day d after L of our lending to them in the period: min(50,000, ⌊income × income_cap_pct/100⌋, DSR headroom) − L, where the DSR headroom is the largest whole amount X with (annual service of existing debts + 12 × 33.17 × (L + X) / 1,000) / income ≤ the DSR cap (medium+: credit lines as balance/5 + balance × rate, and 33.89 per 1,000 when existing balances + L + X exceed 60,000; the cap is 5 points lower from the regulator's effective date; hard: verified income and repayments). It is 0 for a delinquent customer or one below the score cutoff. A customer's approvals of one day together must fit within the morning's limit (that is the customer_limit rule).

The table is the closing one, after the day's approvals and notices: every customer who has applied in the period so far (today included) with their limit after everything approved to them; the daily cap minus today's approvals; (medium+) the budget in force minus everything approved in the period. An entry is right within 1 + 0.1%; a day scores ½ × the share of customers right + ½ × the share of bank figures right (daily capacity; plus the budget from medium); the table's accuracy is the mean over the 20 days. A failed call scores 0 that day.

Objective (maximize) = 0.7 × expected profit / the reference program's expected profit in the same setting + 0.3 × table accuracy. Expected profit: per approval amount × 10% − true PD × 0.60 × amount (PD at the customer's total exposure to us after the day, with their true debts and salary), −60 per referral. The requirements state the margin, the loss on a default and the referral cost as business facts in the credit policy, so the client can give them when asked. The reference profit is recomputed deterministically in-process (cached), so the reference scores exactly 0.7 × 1 + 0.3 × 1.000 = 1.0. Quality is normalized between the baseline (0) and the reference (1); see Reference numbers. A program that approves nothing but reports a perfect table scores 0.30 in easy (and is invalid from medium, by the quota).

F1 — The top of the scale is flat, and profit can buy back a missing table (high, confirmed)

The reference always lends the most the limit allows, because its own sizing switch is off. Switching it on beats the reference: 1.054/1.009/1.102 in easy, 1.202/1.025/1.060 in medium, and 1.193 (quota broken)/1.016/1.095 in hard (A/B/C). In medium/A the sized program is still valid at 1.052 with an empty customer table. B has little headroom (the oracle gains +1.8% to +5.4%). Reporting the morning table instead of the closing one still scores 0.97–0.98 accuracy, yet the README calls the table the key of the case. On the new scale (baseline 0, reference 1, full credit at 1.5), medium/A sized reaches q ≈ 1.50 and sized with an empty table q ≈ 1.13. So skipping the table still beats the reference.

Status: open — proposal: make the reference close to profit-optimal: sizing on, plus a scorecard with tenure and the score/DSR nonlinearity, so that 1.0 means near-oracle. Cap the profit ratio inside the objective (0.7 · min(1, P/Pref) + 0.3 · accuracy) or multiply the two parts, so the table cannot be traded for profit. Score the table on the entries that change on a day (approved customers, customers a notice affects, the regulation day) rather than on ~500 mostly static entries. Once the reference is re-tuned, set quality_cap in case.yaml from the oracle.

Update (rule seeds): partly addressed. The reference now sizes each loan (the most profitable amount in 250 steps) and its scorecard has the job-tenure term and hinges above a 30% DSR and below a score of 800; near-prime top-ups still lend the maximum. The earlier reference (lending the maximum) scores 0.940–1.012 of it in the public settings, above it only in medium/s30 and hard/s30, where the sized reference leaves part of the cut budget unspent. Capping the profit ratio, scoring the table on the entries that change and quality_cap are still open, and the oracle headroom has not been measured on the rule seeds.

F2 — "refer" is a cheap way out of the reason codes (high, confirmed)

Any application may be referred at a cost of 60, and only declines are checked for a code. A program that refers every LOW_SCORE / LIMIT_EXCEEDED / CREDIT_RISK / SPLIT_APPLICATION decline instead stays valid in all 9 settings, at an objective of 0.942–0.977. With sizing added it reaches ≥ 1.0 in five settings. The reference declines 153–265 applications per setting as CREDIT_RISK and 31–82 as LIMIT_EXCEEDED, so a program can skip the most error-prone arithmetic: the limit left after the day's approvals.

Status: open — proposal: make referrals scarce as a business fact. One way is a daily underwriter capacity, for example at most N discretionary referrals a day on top of the mandated tenure and discrepancy ones, as a contractual rule. Another is a rule that a clear decline (delinquent, below the cutoff, under 1,000 of limit left) must not be referred, with the referral cost raised to an underwriter-hour level (several hundred). Alternatively, require a reason code on referrals too.

F3 — Several rule-set parameters barely bind (high, partly)

Only 0.9% of applications score below 650. Of 482 non-delinquent customers, 3 (A), 13 (B) and 6 (C) fall under their cutoff, and only one of them is profitable. C's 120% income cap binds for no application, since a 45% DSR already caps a debt-free loan at about 1.13 × income. A's 100% cap trims at most 0.49%. The verifier found parts overstated. Using the wrong bureau makes the reference invalid in all 9 settings, and removing A's income cap breaks customer_limit, so both matter. The idle C cap and the thin population under the cutoffs are real.

Status: open — proposal: shift the score distribution (e.g. mean ~760, sd ~90) or raise the cutoffs, so that 10–20% of non-delinquent applicants fall under each cutoff. Let the true PD partly follow Bureau B, so that some customers under one bureau's cutoff are profitable. Set C's income cap below its DSR-implied cap (e.g. 90–100%) or add bonus-heavy incomes, so that each rule set binds in its own way. All of this changes the world and every reference number, so it needs a full re-tune.

Update (rule seeds): resolved by retuning the candidates rather than the world. The cutoff is drawn in Bureau A points, 745 / 760 / 775 (800 / 815 / 830 with Bureau B, by derive_params), and puts 8–19% of the 482 non-delinquent applicants under it (39–89 customers), around the score where a loan stops paying, so it both saves bad loans and costs good ones.
