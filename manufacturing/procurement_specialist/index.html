What the agent is asked

The client's request, the only text the agent gets up front. It is the same in every setting.

We assemble two models of control board and buy about two dozen components from a handful of suppliers. Can you plan what we should order from which supplier over the next two months so production doesn't stop and we don't overspend? I've attached the production plan, the bill of materials, our current stock, open orders, the supplier quotes, the approved-vendor list and the budget. Supply has been shaky lately, so I've also added our suppliers' delivery history for the last six months and the allocation notice we got. The BOM already includes engineering's planned change to the flash memory on both boards. Please also use the attached supplier price email when pricing orders.

Deliverable spec (shown to the extractor that reshapes the agent's files, not to the agent)

orders.csv with the header order_week,component_id,supplier_id,quantity
One row per purchase order line: order_week = the week the order is placed, as a number 1..8 (W1 = week of
2026-11-23); component_id and supplier_id exactly as in the data; quantity = whole units (pieces, not reels).
Rows with the same order_week, component_id and supplier_id are added together into one order line.

Settings: one dataset, 9 ground truths

The agent sees the same request and data in every setting. The tier (= difficulty) decides which rules apply; the rule seed draws each rule parameter, independently of the others, from its candidates below. Each difficulty is reported as the mean over rule seeds: the public ones below, or private seeds that draw from the same candidates.

Case notes (README)

Procurement Specialist — manufacturing/procurement_specialist

A contract manufacturer that assembles two control-board models (B100, and B200 with a display) asks the agent to plan eight weeks of purchase orders (W1 = week of 23 November 2026 to W8) for about two dozen components, "so production doesn't stop and we don't overspend". The deliverable is orders.csv (order_week,component_id,supplier_id,quantity). The plan is simulated over W1–W10 against the production plan. The setting is country-neutral apart from one fact: the budget is in the local currency (KRW) and one supplier quotes in USD, converted at a fixed planning rate.

A setting is a tier and a rule seed: the tier decides which of the client's rules are in force this round, the rule seed draws their parameters (planning rate, safety stock, share cap, quality hold) from candidate pools. The public panel has three rule seeds (42, 3, 78), so nine public ground truths; any other rule seed is a private draw of the same candidates. The agent sees the same request and the same data in every setting (the validator checks that its prompt and workspace are byte-identical); what changes is the hidden requirements, which the simulated client answers from, and the evaluator. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

Data (the same in every setting)

components (24 parts plus the new flash C25, critical flag, standard cost), production_plan (boards per model, W1–W10), bom (with valid_from_week / valid_to_week: C04 in W1–W5, C25 in W6–W10), inventory (resistors in reels of 5,000), open_orders, supplier_quotes.xlsx (currency, unit_price, moq, two all-units price breaks, lead_time_days in business days; a suppliers sheet), approved_vendors, budget (monthly caps), delivery_history (188 past deliveries, promised vs. received date) and allocations (monthly caps for S03/C01, S03/C03, S08/C06) and supplier_price_email.txt (the price notice below). The request mentions the history, the allocation notice, the planned flash change and the price email, so every tier sees them.

Supplier price email

A synthetic supplier notice (supplier_price_email.txt, dated 2026-11-20) approves a 20% increase to all C01/S03 quote tiers for orders placed in W4 or later. Earlier orders keep the quoted price. A further 5% increase is pending and is excluded. The order week determines the price for both purchase cost and monthly budget; open orders are unaffected. This adapts the distinction between approved and pending price changes from the ReflectionAI procurement scenarios.

Derived for the texts (derive_params): hold_supplier (the supplier's name in each locale) and hold_until (the week). admissible() rejects a hold that ends before the supplier's goods could arrive at all (S03 and S05 quote 11–13 business days, so nothing of theirs arrives before W4); every candidate in the pools passes, the hook guards edits to them.

Why the pools look like this (all measured with the validator's binding test: from the first public seed's setting, change one parameter; it binds if that setting's reference breaks a rule, its objective moves by 1%, or the other setting's reference beats it by 1%):

  • The hold week is not a parameter of its own. In hard, moving the end of an S03 hold from W4 to W5 changed nothing (the November budget already pushes S03's orders to W3, arriving W6), so supplier and week are drawn as one value. In hard, quality_hold S03 to W5 → S01 to W5 makes the reference invalid on world seeds 20261123, 1, 2 and 3.
  • The planning rate and the share cap are fixed in hard. With late deliveries, the safety lead time and the allocation, S04 carries 4–9% of the hard reference's purchases, so 1,320 → 1,550 moved it 0.6–1.4% depending on the world, and a 90% cap left the hard reference valid under 70% on two of four worlds. In medium they bind: from the first public seed (1,320), 1,450 and 1,500 break the budget on worlds 20261123, 1 and 3 and move the reference +1.31% and +1.82% on world 2 (1,380 moves it only 0.6–0.7%, and breaks the budget on world 3); from 90%, both 80% and 70% make the reference invalid on all four worlds. 1,500 (a cautious treasury rate) was added because, with the first version's reference, 1,320 → 1,450 moved the medium reference by only 0.85% on the case world.
  • Safety stock binds everywhere: in hard, 1 → 1.5 weeks moves the reference by +8.3% (world 2) to +11.9% (world 20261123).
  • A 60% share cap was tried for hard and left out: the reference's second-source step moves whole lines and cannot reach 60% for C02 and C25 in most combinations.
  • No budget parameter. A finance reserve (0, 5 or 10% of every month's budget kept back) was tried: it moved the hard reference by 0–1.3% (the reference defers to fast suppliers at little extra cost), so it was dropped. The budget is data; it is calibrated over the pools instead (below), so that November binds in every combination.

What an agent must ask

The data never states the parameters, so the client has to be asked, in every tier:

  • the planning rate for S04's dollar quotes (target usd_planning_rate);
  • the safety stock rule for critical parts, in weeks of average demand (safety_stock);
  • the dual-sourcing habit and its cap, which the client lets bend if it saves real money (dual_source_critical);
  • which supplier is on quality hold and up to which week (quality_hold); with S03 on hold (hard), the client adds that the hold covers its allocated MCU and DRAM and the new C25 too.

And, independent of the rule seed: the BOM explosion, units (reels), MOQ per line, the budget month, approved status only, the cost model, and the round's scope (easy and medium) or the lateness, allocation, engineering change and expected-cost scoring (hard), as listed in the tiers table.

Reference and baseline numbers (seed 20261123)

The reference is a time-phased MRP per part: it nets stock and open orders against the exploded demand and orders from the cheapest supplier that still arrives in time, as late as possible, covering two weeks (or one) and rounding up to a price break when the break costs less in total (medium/hard). Critical parts then move whole lines to a second source until the share cap holds. Leftover stock is trimmed, latest line first: a line is cut by what the part holds above its target until its last week, or replaced in the same week by a small-MOQ supplier sized to what is still missing, when that lowers the part's cost. If a month is over budget, whole lines move to the first week of the next month with a supplier that still arrives in time, cheapest extra cost per KRW deferred first, each re-sized to what the part still needs from its new arrival week (so the phased-out C04 deferred to Express Parts Korea (S09) in hard is 1,400, not the 4,100 of the S03 MOQ it replaced); if no line can move in time, a line moves late (the part waits for it); January's excess is pulled into W6 where December has room; and if nothing can move, a line is cut (accepting shortfall or backlog), so that the reference is valid whatever a rule seed draws. In hard it plans one week of safety lead time for every supplier whose smoothed late rate in the history is above 5% and respects the allocation. It keeps the cheapest valid plan, by its own cost view, of fifteen variants: the netting planner with and without the second-source step and with two- or one-week lots, plus a lot-for-lot planner that splits every order of a critical part with a second source at the share cap (the client's quota habit, which beats whole-line second sourcing when the second source is dear), each with the trim before and after the budget step, only after it (trimming first can leave the budget step only dearer deferrals), or not at all. It uses only what the agent can see: lateness estimates from the history, never the true rates.

The baseline (baseline_solution, quality 0 on the normalized scale) is what the client does today without an engineer: the ERP's plain MRP run and the buyer's fixes. Every part is ordered lot for lot (each order covers the first week short of stock or safety stock, at least the MOQ) from the cheapest approved supplier that still delivers in time, as late as its lead time allows, at list prices and on the promised dates (no price breaks, no use of the delivery history). Critical parts follow a quota arrangement: every order is split with the cheapest other supplier so that neither gets more than the share cap. A month over budget moves whole lines to the next month as the reference does, and January's lines are pulled into W6 (cut as the reference cuts when December has no room).

Budget in the data: November 115 M, December 475 M, January 85 M KRW. It is calibrated over every admissible combination of the candidate pools in every tier (225 combinations), on the unconstrained plans: November is 95% of the least any combination spends there, rounded down to 5 M, so it binds in every combination a rule seed can draw; December has 5% headroom over the most any combination spends there plus what it spends in November above the cap; January 5% over the most any combination spends there. Allocation: C01 6,500, C03 9,000, C06 3,500 per month (60% of the peak month of the unconstrained hard plan for the calibration values 1,380 / 1 / 80% / S07 to W3, the old rule set A).

Over all 225 combinations on this world the reference and the baseline are valid, the baseline is worse (ratio 1.045–1.136 in easy, 1.032–1.122 in medium, 1.156–1.410 in hard) and the unconstrained plan overspends November. The reference sometimes has to move a November line late (the part waits a week) and in a few hard combinations December binds too. The same holds on world seeds 1, 2 and 3 for all 225 combinations each: the reference and the baseline are valid and the baseline is worse by at least 3.5% / 2.5% / 12.8% (world 1, easy / medium / hard), 1.2% / 1.0% / 14.9% (world 2) and 1.1% / 1.2% / 16.9% (world 3). The narrowest gaps are combinations with Kowon Passive (S07) on hold to W3 and 0.5 weeks of safety stock, where the reference's best plan is the quota planner with the trim after the budget step (see REVIEW.md, Rule seeds). Validation covers the public panel on world seeds 1–3.

The local-search headroom of the first version (a first-improvement search beat the greedy reference by 3–11%, see REVIEW.md F2) was measured on the old rule sets and the old reference and has not been redone.

How the expectation is computed (hard)

Lateness is at most two weeks, so the net position of a part at the end of week w is certain except for the deliveries promised for weeks w−1 and w. For each week the scorer enumerates the joint outcomes of those deliveries (at most two per supplier quoting the part, so at most 2^8 states) and takes the expected holding, backlog, shortfall and scrap. The result is exact and deterministic; nothing is sampled.

Notes

  • Practice behind the facts. All-units quantity discounts per order line, MOQs set by full reels or trays, business-day lead times and allocation in shortage markets are standard procurement practice. During the 2021–2023 component shortage, suppliers put parts on allocation, shipped only part of what was ordered and stretched lead times (Ultra Librarian, "Electronic Component Allocation & Lead Times", 2022 and 2023 updates; EMSNow, "Eight commonly used terms when buying electronic components"). Engineering changes with date or use-up effectivity, and the excess and obsolete stock they create, are described in Oracle's MRP documentation ("Engineering change planning", use-up date) and in APICS/CPIM material on engineering change management. Stochastic supplier lead times and the trade-off between safety lead time and safety stock follow e.g. Humair, Ruark, Tomlin and Willems, "Incorporating Stochastic Lead Times Into the Guaranteed Service Model of Safety Stock Optimization", Interfaces 43(5), 2013, and Ben-Ammar, Dolgui et al. on multi-period supply planning under stochastic lead times (expected-cost models with discrete, bounded lead-time distributions).
  • Synthetic or unverified. All prices, MOQs, lead times, lateness rates, allocations, budgets and the four planning rates are synthetic. The cost weights (0.5% holding per week, about 26% a year, in the usual 20–30% range for carrying cost; 5× standard cost per piece-week of backlog; 1.5× for shortfall; scrap at standard cost) are design choices, not measured values. The two short weeks assume Christmas and New Year's Day are non-working days, as in most countries that use the Gregorian calendar; other plants may differ.
  • Lateness is a property of the supplier only (not of the part or the season), at most two weeks, and independent across deliveries; real suppliers have correlated delays (a shared shipment, a plant shutdown). The history has 10–28 deliveries per supplier, so some estimates are far from the truth: S05 (10 deliveries) looks late 32% of the time against a true 15%. True late rates at this seed run from 2% (S09, the express broker) to 37% (S04, overseas) and 35% (S03, the allocated semiconductor maker).
  • validate-case checks every public setting: deterministic data in five locales, the same agent view in all nine, the reference at quality 1.0, a valid baseline worse than the reference, one deliverable per rule of the setting that breaks only that rule, and that every parameter binds (on this world and on world seeds 1–3).

Relationship to the source assignment

This is a synthetic adaptation of the Apex Manufacturing take-home, not a reproduction of its data or a submission to that assignment. Both use a production schedule, BOM, shared components, stock, existing purchase orders, supplier eligibility, lead times and sourcing rules. Here the output remains the benchmark's eight-week orders.csv; the original assignment calls for a general-purpose program that writes purchase orders and alerts to scenario databases. The email is a new fictional commercial event, not a document from the assignment.

The source assignment also includes dated management memos, certification, concentration history, approvals, hazardous handling and expedited freight. Those are not modeled by this extension: the existing quality hold and dual-source rule are only simplified analogues. The simulated client does not receive later emails during a run, issue purchase orders, or request approval. The exercise tests incorporating a dated approved price revision into a static plan and budget, rather than autonomous event handling.
