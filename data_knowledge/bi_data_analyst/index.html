At a glance

What the AI has to do

Answer twelve questions from the managers of a Seoul coffee-and-tea subscription shop using its order database export, and send back answers.json with one number per question, or "don't know" where the AI cannot stand behind a figure. The answers go into the CEO's pack for the next board meeting; money is in Korean won.

What the AI is given

  • The client's request: "Our managers sent these questions. Can you answer them from the database export? … please send back one number per question." It gives none of the definitions behind the numbers and does not say what a wrong figure costs.
  • questions.md and data_dictionary.md: the twelve questions (reference date 2026-09-30, money in KRW) and the columns of each table.
  • customers.csv and accounts_meta.csv: 1,407 customers in Korea, Japan and the US, with sign-up time (in UTC), plan and account type (regular, staff or test).
  • orders.csv, order_items.csv, payments.csv and refunds.csv: about 11,000 orders from January 2025 to September 2026 with status, currency (KRW, JPY or USD) and amounts before and after tax, their lines, their payments and their refunds (refund amounts include tax; some were made in October 2026).
  • products.csv and fx_rates.csv: 24 products in six categories, "snacks" and "pantry" among them, and the won rate of the dollar and the yen for every day.

What the AI must find out by asking the client

  • When the fiscal year starts: September, April or July, depending on the version, and that a year is named after the year it starts in, so FY2025 is September 2025–August 2026, April 2025–March 2026 or July 2025–June 2026.
  • That a period still running on the reference date has no figure yet: under a July year the first half of FY2026, and under a September year its first quarter too, must be "don't know", not the figure to date.
  • That a "day" is a calendar day in Korea time (UTC+9), although every timestamp is stored in UTC: an order at 2026-06-30 16:30 UTC falls on 2026-07-01, the sale day.
  • Which orders and customers count: only completed orders (a cancelled or failed order does not, even when a payment was recorded); test accounts are left out of every figure, staff accounts stay in.
  • How money is counted: net revenue is the amount before tax minus refunds without their tax, booked in the period of the order, refunds made in October 2026 included; yen and dollar amounts convert at the rate of the order's day.
  • What an "active customer" is: one with a counted order in the last 30, 60 or 90 days up to 2026-09-30, depending on the version.
  • That "pantry" was called "snacks" until 2025-10-01 and older products still carry the old label, so a pantry question covers both (needed only on hard).
  • How money answers are rounded: to the nearest 100, 1,000 or 10,000 won, depending on the version.

How it is scored

Scores 0 if the deliverable…

  • Leaves out a question from Q01 to Q12, or answers one with anything other than one number or "don't know".
  • Gives a money answer that counts at this difficulty and is not a whole multiple of the rounding unit (100, 1,000 or 10,000 won).
  • Gives, for a count (active customers, sale-day orders, new customers) or the top customer that counts at this difficulty, the figure with the test accounts kept in, where that differs from the right one.
  • Gives a number for a question that counts at this difficulty and whose period is still running on the reference date, unless the company policy lets this rule bend and the AI asked the client for an exception first.

If it is valid, quality is…

Higher is better: each question that counts earns 2 if right, 0 if wrong and 0.5, 1 or 1.5 for "don't know" (depending on the version; the client says which when asked), and the total is divided by 2 per question. So a figure is worth giving only when the AI is more than 25%, 50% or 75% sure of it. Counts and the customer id must match exactly; money must be within 0.5% or half the rounding unit, the September change within 0.05 percentage points, the rest within 0.5%. The client's own way (score 0) is "not available yet" for every question; the reference solution (score 1) is right on every question that counts and says "don't know" only where the period is still running, which is the most a valid answer sheet can earn.

Case notes (README)

BI Data Analyst — data_knowledge/bi_data_analyst

A coffee and tea subscription shop that sells boxes and one-off items to customers in Korea, Japan and the US (head office in Seoul, all reporting in KRW) sends the agent a CSV export of its order database, a short data dictionary and twelve questions from its managers: "Can you answer them from the database export? ... please send back one number per question." The deliverable is answers.json with every question id ({"Q01": ..., ..., "Q12": ...}), each a number or null. null means "don't know": the CEO's board pack shows the figure as "not available yet". The request names none of the definitions that decide the numbers, and it does not say what a wrong figure costs. Both are in the hidden requirements, and the simulated client gives them only when asked.

A setting is a tier and a rule seed. The tier decides which questions the board pack needs this round. The rule seed draws four of the client's rules, each on its own, from a pool of three values: the first month of the fiscal year, the active-customer window, the unit money is rounded to, and what a "don't know" is worth against a right and a wrong answer. That makes 81 combinations per tier. The public panel is rule seeds 4, 12 and 23 (nine settings, stored in dataset/answer/ and checked by the validator); any other seed is a private draw whose answers are published nowhere. The agent sees the same request and the same data in every setting (the validator checks that its prompt and workspace are byte-identical). The simulated client answers from the hidden requirements of the setting, and the evaluator scores under it. So the case measures two things: whether the agent finds the definitions and gets the answers right, and whether it says "don't know" when it should (calibrated abstention). The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

The case is audited: GT.md has the ground truth in five parts with the audit's numbers, and CLIENT_REVIEW.md the passage the client answers each fact from, in every language.

The settings never change the data. Orders run from 2025-01-01, so FY2025 is covered and over under every fiscal year of the pool (it starts in September, April or July 2025). The FY2026 periods are not all over on the reference date: under an April year the first quarter (April to June) and the first half (April to September) are; under a July year the first half runs to December; under a September year the first quarter runs to November and the first half to February 2027. The objective (maximize) is the total reward divided by 2 × the number of scored questions, a number in [0, 1]. An answer or a null to a question the tier does not score has no effect. The metrics are scored, running (the scored questions whose period is still running), answered, right, wrong, dont_know, accuracy_on_answered (right / answered), coverage (answered / scored), the total reward, and the lists of wrong and "don't know" questions.

Abstention thresholds. A sheet that knows the scheme should answer a question only when its chance p of being right beats "don't know": p × 2 > dont_know_reward. On the normalized scale the all-null sheet is 0, so with k right and w wrong answers a sheet scores about (k − λw) / (scored questions with a figure), λ = dont_know_reward / (2 − dont_know_reward): a wrong figure costs a third of a right one, one, or three. The client states the scheme as a business fact: "a wrong figure that reaches the board has to be corrected at the next meeting and costs us credibility, while 'not available yet' only leaves a gap", followed by the three values. When a null is worth 0.5 the client adds that "this time the board would rather see a figure than a gap"; when it is worth 1.5, that after corrections in the last two packs "the board would much rather see a gap than a wrong figure this time".

Periods still running. One kind of question has no figure at all. Under a July year the first half of FY2026 (Q07) is still running on the reference date, under a September year its first quarter (Q02) too. Finance's habit is to show "not available yet" until a period is over, so the right answer is null and the most a valid sheet earns there is the null's reward. A number for such a question breaks running_period, the one bendable rule: the CFO can approve a figure to date, and with that exception the figure is judged against the exact figure to date. Under an April year every period is over and the rule is not in force.

Correctness does not depend on the draw except for the money tolerance. Q03, Q04, Q05 and Q10 must match exactly. Money must be within 0.5% or half the rounding unit, whichever is wider (50, 500 or 5,000 KRW), so the exact figure rounded as the draw requires always counts. Q11 must be within 0.05 percentage points, and Q08 and Q12 within 0.5%. A percentage given as a fraction is accepted. violating_examples asserts, in every setting and on every world the validator builds, that the exact answers rounded to the setting's unit reach the bound with no violation (null where the period is running), and that with the running-period habit set aside the figures to date, rounded the same way, are right for every scored question: following a rule never costs a question.

F1 — The rounding rule made Q07 and Q09 unanswerable (high, partly)

Money answers had to be whole thousands of KRW, but they were scored within 0.5% of the unrounded figure. For the per-order averages this fails: Q07 = 41,618 rounds to 42,000 (+0.92%) and Q09 = 35,780 rounds to 36,000 (+0.62%). Bending report_rounding raised the score: a UTC-day sheet scored 7/12 rounded and 9/12 exact.

Status: fixed in the first change. A money answer is now correct within 0.5% or 500 KRW of the exact figure, whichever is wider, so the exact figure rounded to the nearest thousand always counts. The "How the answers are judged" section and the report_rounding target say so in all five locales. violating_examples now asserts that the exact answers, rounded as finance requires, score 12/12 with no violation on every world the validator builds; they do on seeds 1–20. The reference rises from 9 to 11 (Q07 and Q09 now count), and following the rule no longer costs a question: the UTC-day sheet scores 9/12 rounded or exact. The verifier's objection stands: no general "bending never helps" check was added, since bendable rules elsewhere are meant to cost objective. F2 — No headroom between the reference and the ceiling (high, confirmed)
