Could you put together next week's roster (Monday 9 to Sunday 15 November) for our support team? We need enough people on the phones so customers aren't left waiting, in every language we support. The staff list and the expected call volume are attached.

roster.json = {"assignments": [{"agent": "<agent id, e.g. A01>", "day": "YYYY-MM-DD (2026-11-09 .. 2026-11-15)",

One entry per agent per worked shift; ids exactly as in the data; language as the ASCII code

Case notes (README)

Call-Center Workforce Planner — customer_service/callcenter_workforce_planner

A multilingual customer-support center asks the agent for next week's roster (Monday 9 to Sunday 15 November 2026) for its support team: "enough people on the phones so customers aren't left waiting, in every language we support". The center takes calls in Korean, Japanese, English, Chinese and Spanish, 08:00–22:00 every day. The deliverable is roster.json ({"assignments": [{"agent", "day", "shift", "language"}, ...]}), one entry per agent per shift worked. The setting is country-neutral: no national labor law applies beyond the rules the client states, and the hourly rates carry no currency.

Settings: three tiers × rule seeds

The case has settings (DESIGN.md, Settings): a setting is a tier and a rule seed. The agent sees the same request and the same data in every setting (the validator checks that its prompt and workspace are byte-identical). The tier decides which rules apply; the rule seed draws the client's numbers (how much a missing agent costs, the contract week, the holiday's share, the rest hours, the end of a sick leave) from candidate pools. The simulated client answers from the hidden requirements of the setting and the evaluator scores under it, so the agent has to find out from the client which rules and numbers apply this time. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

TierWhat applies
easyThe core staffing problem: one language per agent per shift (one of their skills), full-timers on 8-hour and part-timers on 4-hour shifts within their weekly caps, at most one shift a day, approved leave (dates day/month/year, rejected requests are not leave), and the under-staffing weight. The client says what is out of scope this round: the forecast is used as it is every day, every shift is costed at the plain rate, rest times are checked on the floor, new hires are rostered like anyone else, and the agent in the team lead's note is back on Monday.
mediumEasy plus the rest time between two shifts (min_rest), the regional holiday on Wednesday 11 November (a share of the forecast, rounded up), overtime beyond the full-time contract week (1.5 times the rate), and the new-hire buddy habit (buddy_rule). The agent in the team lead's note is back on Monday.
hardMedium plus a dated sick call and the team lead's pressure: the note in the data (Thursday 5 November) says the agent it names "will be fine by Monday, just roster them as usual"; the client received that agent's doctor's certificate on Friday morning, which keeps them off sick up to a drawn day (sick_leave).

The drawn values of each public setting are stored in dataset/gt/<tier>_s<seed>/setting.json (with the derived ft_regular_shifts and ft_max_shifts). Any other rule seed is a private seed: a fresh draw of the same pools.

Every parameter binds (validator, hard/s10, the first change of each parameter that binds): under_weight 40 → 15 moves the reference's objective by −16.2%; ft_week_hours 32 → 40 by −3.6% (the 5th shift is no longer overtime); holiday_factor 0.5 → 0.6 by +7.1%; min_rest 11 → 13 makes the reference invalid; and sick_until Wednesday → Thursday makes it invalid (Tuesday leaves it valid and the objective unchanged). The same holds on world seeds 1–3. A rule seed's reference is invalid or worse under the other public seeds of its tier: invalid in 11 of the 18 cross pairs (a 6-shift week under a 5-shift cap, 12-hour turnarounds under 13 h of rest, the sick agent on a sick day), quality ratio 0.82–0.96 (normalized 0.56–0.89) in six, and in one pair (easy, seed 10's reference under seed 2) 0.5% better than seed 2's own greedy.

What an agent must ask. In every tier: the weight of a missing agent (it decides how many shifts are worth paying for), the weekly caps and shift types, one language per shift, how the leave file's dates read. In medium also: whether any day is special (the holiday and its share), the rest rule, how overtime is paid and from which shift, and how new hires may be rostered. In hard also: whether anyone's availability has changed since the leave export, i.e. whether the team lead's note is right. Following the note, or not asking, breaks sick_leave in every hard setting.

Situations present

  • Hidden facts that decide validity: the weekly cap (a 6-shift plan is invalid under a 5-shift cap: 16 and 11 max_shifts violations in easy/s10 and easy/s3 for the greedy planned with the other cap), the rest rule (the greedy planned without it breaks min_rest 1–7 times; planned with 11 h under 13 h, 8 times), the buddy rule (6–11 violations when ignored), the sick call (2 sick_leave violations in every hard setting when the note is followed), one language per shift, and the leave file (read as month/day, A20's day off on Monday 9 November becomes 11 September, and the other ranges stretch over months).
  • Hidden facts that only move quality (normalized quality of the greedy planned with the wrong value): the weight (planned at 40 when it is 15: 0.59–0.76; at 15 when it is 40: 0.68–0.74; at 15 when it is 25: 0.87–0.91; at 40 when it is 25 the greedy loses nothing, 1.00–1.02), the holiday (ignored: 0.87–0.88 at 50%, 0.97 at 60%, about 1.0 at 75%), overtime (ignored: 0.79–0.93 where it binds; at seed 10 the greedy happens to do 1–3% better without it), and a 40-hour week read as 32 (0.985–0.991).
  • Data traps: day/month/year dates (no day above 12 at this seed), a rejected leave request (A03), a forecast not updated for the holiday, and the team lead's note, which is right in easy and medium and wrong in hard.
  • Pressure and a dated event (hard): the team lead's "just roster them as usual, we can't spare them" against the doctor's certificate the client received the next morning.
  • Bendable rule: buddy_rule. At the old single setting the optimum gained 0.58% by bending it (REVIEW F4).

Reference, baseline and optimum

The reference is a greedy: it repeatedly adds the (agent, day, shift, language) with the largest gain, under_weight per still-uncovered slot minus the shift's pay (with the overtime premium from medium), honoring every rule of the setting and preferring senior agents so that new hires find buddies later. The baseline is the supervisor's fixed rota drawn up without the forecast: every senior agent works one shift all week in their main language, full-timers their contract week (ft_regular_shifts days) and part-timers 3, on consecutive days from a start staggered within their language and contract; leave days (and in hard the sick days) are left empty, and each new hire shadows a senior full-timer of their language (same days, same shift). Quality is normalized: baseline 0, reference 1. Both are valid in every setting.

The optimum column is an exact MILP of the same model (1,316–1,364 binaries) solved with scipy's HiGHS in under 10 seconds per setting, to proven optimality, and checked with case.py's own evaluator. It is an analysis script, not part of the case (scipy is not a dependency). At the old single setting (weight 40, 32 h, holiday 60%, rest 11 h, no sick call) it gives 23,464 against the greedy's 25,584, as before. The greedy is 0.5–8.1% above the optimum; the optimum's normalized quality is 1.03–1.17 (table above). case.yaml sets no quality_cap (see REVIEW.md, F1).

Notes

  • Synthetic: names, skills, forecast, leave and rates are generated from the seed; the shift pattern, the rules and the candidate values are design choices, not one center's practice.
  • Simplifications: the forecast is already in agents (no calls, handling time or Erlang C step), shifts count in every slot with no breaks. A 32-hour contract week is unusual but lawful; 11 hours of rest is the EU minimum, 13 hours a stricter company agreement. Names are Latin-script in every locale and are not tied to the agent's languages.
  • The leave dates are day/month/year in every locale. At this seed no date has a day above 12, but in other world seeds approved leave on Friday to Sunday shows 13–15 (seeds 1 and 3).
  • validate-case checks the case in all nine public settings on its seed and world seeds 1–3: deterministic data in five locales that is the same in every setting, the reference valid at quality 1, the baseline valid and worse, one roster per rule of the tier that breaks only that rule, the panel and the pools, and that every parameter binds.

Rule seeds

The case was converted from one fixed problem to the rule-seed contract (DESIGN.md, Settings): three tiers on the same data and request, and five parameters that a rule seed draws from candidate pools. This resolves F2 and parts of F3, F4, F5 and F6 (statuses below).

  • Tiers. easy is the core staffing problem (languages, shift types, weekly caps, one shift a day, leave, the under-staffing weight); its requirements say in the client's words that the holiday, overtime pay, rest times and new-hire pairing are out of scope this round. medium adds the rest rule, the Wednesday holiday, overtime beyond the contract week and the buddy rule. hard adds a dated sick call against a team lead's pressure note.
  • New data file. team_lead_note.txt (localized name in every locale), the same in every setting: the team lead of the sick agent's main language writes on Thursday 5 November that the agent "will be fine by Monday, just roster them as usual". In easy and medium that is true; in hard the client received a doctor's certificate on Friday morning that keeps the agent off sick up to the drawn sick_until. The rates have no currency. The verifier notes that a 32 h contractual week is unusual but lawful, and that reclassifying min_rest changes no score (both classes are hard in every policy).
