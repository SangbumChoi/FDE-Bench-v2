At a glance

What the AI has to do

Build next week's shift roster (Monday 9 to Sunday 15 November 2026) for a call center that answers the phones in Korean, Japanese, English, Chinese and Spanish from 08:00 to 22:00. For each of 30 agents the AI decides which days they work, which shift and in which language, so that every language has enough people at the lowest total cost. The result is roster.json.

What the AI is given

  • The client's request: a few lines asking for next week's roster, with “enough people on the phones so customers aren't left waiting, in every language we support”. It gives no rules and no numbers.
  • agents.xlsx: the 30 agents (20 full-time, 4 of them new hires, and 10 part-time) and the languages each one can take calls in.
  • call_forecast.csv: how many agents each language needs in every half-hour from 08:00 to 22:00, day by day: between 1 and 5, already counted in people, not calls.
  • shifts.csv and labor_cost.csv: four 8-hour full-time shifts (starting 08:00, 10:00, 12:00, 14:00), four 4-hour part-time shifts (08:00, 12:00, 16:00, 18:00), and the hourly rates: 22 full-time, 18 part-time, with no currency.
  • leave.csv: 5 leave requests, dates written day/month/year, one of them rejected.
  • team_lead_note.txt: a message sent on Thursday 5 November saying that an agent off sick with the flu “will be fine by Monday” and should be rostered as usual.

What the AI must find out by asking the client

  • What a missing agent costs: the client's price for one agent short for half an hour in one language, in the units of the hourly rates: 17 or 50, depending on the version. It decides how many shifts are worth paying for.
  • That an agent takes calls in only one language for the whole shift: a bilingual agent never covers two languages at once.
  • The full-time contract week: 32 or 40 hours (4 or 5 shifts), depending on the version. On easy nobody works beyond it. On medium and hard one extra shift is possible, but only with the operations manager's approval and at 1.5 times the rate.
  • The part-time contract week: 12 or 16 hours (3 or 4 four-hour shifts), depending on the version, with no overtime. Those hours are guaranteed: a part-timer is paid the whole contract week even with fewer shifts.
  • Rest between shifts: at least 11 or 13 hours (depending on the version) between the end of one shift and the start of the same agent's next shift.
  • Whether any day is special. One is: a weekday from Monday 9 to Friday 13 November (depending on the version) is a regional public holiday. Plan that day for 25% or 50% of the forecast, rounded up; the file was not updated.
  • How new hires are paid: they are on a training salary, so their whole contract week is paid whether or not they are on the phones, and leaving their shifts off saves nothing.
  • On medium and hard: a new hire is never alone on a language. In every half-hour a new hire works, at least one experienced agent must take calls in the same language.
  • On hard: whether the team lead's note is still right. It is not: a doctor's certificate received on Friday morning keeps that agent off sick up to Wednesday 11, Thursday 12 or Friday 13 November, depending on the version.

How it is scored

Scores 0 if the deliverable…

  • Puts an agent on a language that is not among their skills.
  • Puts a full-timer on a 4-hour shift or a part-timer on an 8-hour one, or gives anyone more shifts in the week than their contract allows (4 or 5 for full-timers on easy, one more on medium and hard; 3 or 4 for part-timers).
  • Leaves an agent less than the 11 or 13 hours of rest between two shifts (two shifts on one day included).
  • Rosters anyone on a day of approved leave.
  • On medium and hard, gives a full-timer a shift beyond the contract week without the operations manager's approval (unless the company policy lets this rule bend and the AI asked the client for an exception first), or leaves a new hire on the phones in a half-hour with no experienced agent on the same language.
  • On hard, rosters the agent from the team lead's note on any day up to the last day of the doctor's certificate.

If it is valid, quality is…

Lower is better: 17 or 50 (depending on the version) for every agent missing in a half-hour and language, plus the pay for all shifts (22 an hour full-time, 18 part-time; a part-timer's and a new hire's whole contract week is paid even with fewer shifts; an approved overtime shift at 1.5 times), all in the units of the hourly rates. The client's own way (score 0) is the supervisor's fixed rota, drawn up without looking at the forecast; the reference solution (score 1) re-plans each agent's week in turn, and then pairs of agents together, until no change lowers the total. The best roster known, an exact optimum, scores a few percent above 1.

  1. Valid or not. Breaking any rule above makes the deliverable invalid and the run scores 0. A rule the company policy lets bend counts only if the AI asked the client for an exception.
  2. Quality. The result is placed on a scale where the client's own way without an engineer is 0 and the reference solution is 1. Better than the reference goes above 1; full credit at the policy's cap (1.5).

Case notes (README)

Call-Center Workforce Planner — customer_service/callcenter_workforce_planner

A multilingual customer-support center asks the agent for next week's roster (Monday 9 to Sunday 15 November 2026) for its support team: "enough people on the phones so customers aren't left waiting, in every language we support". The center takes calls in Korean, Japanese, English, Chinese and Spanish, 08:00–22:00 every day. The deliverable is roster.json ({"assignments": [{"agent", "day", "shift", "language"}, ...]}), one entry per agent per shift worked. The setting is country-neutral: no national labor law applies beyond the rules the client states, and the hourly rates carry no currency.

Settings: three tiers × rule seeds

The case has settings (DESIGN.md, Settings): a setting is a tier and a rule seed. The agent sees the same request and the same data in every setting (the validator checks that its prompt and workspace are byte-identical). The tier decides which rules apply; the rule seed draws the client's numbers (how much a missing agent costs, the full-time and part-time contract weeks, the rest hours, which day is a holiday and how much of the forecast it keeps, the end of a sick leave) from candidate pools. The simulated client answers from the hidden requirements of the setting and the evaluator scores under it, so the agent has to find out from the client which rules and numbers apply this time. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

TierWhat applies
easyThe core staffing problem: one language per agent per shift (one of their skills), full-timers on 8-hour and part-timers on 4-hour shifts within their contract weeks (nobody works beyond them: no overtime this round), the rest time between two shifts of an agent (min_rest), approved leave (dates day/month/year, rejected requests are not leave), a regional public holiday on one weekday (a share of the forecast, rounded up) and the price of a missing agent against the labor cost (by the shift, with the part-timers' guaranteed hours and the new hires' training salary paid in full). New hires are rostered like anyone else, and the agent in the team lead's note is back on Monday.
mediumEasy plus overtime as a business policy (overtime_approval: a full-timer may work one shift beyond the contract week, only with the operations manager's approval and at 1.5 times the rate; an approved exception can bend it) and the new-hire buddy rule (buddy_rule, contractual: a new hire is never alone on a language). The agent in the team lead's note is back on Monday.
hardMedium plus a dated sick call and the team lead's pressure: the note in the data (Thursday 5 November) says the agent it names "will be fine by Monday, just roster them as usual"; the client received that agent's doctor's certificate on Friday morning, which keeps them off sick up to a drawn day (sick_leave).

What an agent must ask. In every tier: the price of a missing agent (it decides how many shifts are worth paying for), both contract weeks and how they are paid (the part-timers' guaranteed hours, the new hires' training salary), the rest rule, one language per shift, and whether any day is special (the holiday, its day and share). Situations present

  • Hidden facts that decide validity: both contract weeks (a 5-shift full-time week under a 32-hour contract, a 4-shift part-time week under a 12-hour one), the rest rule (13 hours forbids the 12-hour turnarounds an 11-hour plan uses), from medium the overtime approval and the buddy rule, in hard the sick call.
  • Hidden facts that move quality: the price of a missing agent (the reference planned at the other price loses up to 0.22 of the scale), the holiday (planning the forecast as it is loses 0.03–0.21), one language per shift (counting a bilingual agent on two languages loses 0.46 to all of it), the new hires' training salary (planning them as paid by the shift loses up to 0.33 at the price 17), the longer contract weeks read as the shorter ones (the part-time week is paid in full either way).
  • Data traps: day/month/year dates (no day above 12 at this seed), a rejected leave request (A03), a forecast not updated for the holiday, and the team lead's note, which is right in easy and medium and wrong in hard.
  • Pressure and a dated event (hard): the team lead's "just roster them as usual, we can't spare them" against the doctor's certificate the client received the next morning.
  • Bendable rule: overtime_approval. With an approved exception a full-timer may work one more shift at 1.5 times the rate. That pays where a missing agent is dear and the contract week short (price 50, 32 hours: the optimum is 2.9–8.2% cheaper, +0.03 to +0.13 of quality), a little at price 50 and 40 hours (0–1.5%), and nothing at price 17 (the optimum takes no overtime shift), where an exception only costs the policy's soft-rule penalty.

Reference, baseline and optimum

The reference (_Planner) re-plans each agent's week exactly given everyone else's: a dynamic program over the seven days (which shift and language each day, within the contract week and the rest time, a new hire only beside an experienced colleague of the same language), agents in turn until no week improves; then every pair of agents who share a language is taken off the roster and re-planned together while that lowers the objective (an experienced agent and a new hire also with the first planning for both). It plans no overtime shift. It is deterministic and takes 1–10 seconds per setting. The baseline is the supervisor's fixed rota drawn up without the forecast: every senior agent works one shift all week in their main language, full-timers and part-timers their contract week, on consecutive days from a start staggered within their language and contract; leave days (and in hard the sick days) are left empty, and each new hire shadows a senior full-timer of their language (same days, same shift). Quality is normalized: baseline 0, reference 1. Both are valid in every setting.

The best known solution is the exact optimum of the same model: a mixed-integer program (one binary per agent, day, shift and language the rules allow; under-coverage, overtime, rest and buddy constraints as in the scorer) solved by scipy's HiGHS to proven optimality within seconds per setting; its dual bound is the case's objective_bound (proven optimal in every audited setting and on world seeds 1–3, at most 90 seconds each). The reference is 0.3–8.1% above the optimum: the planner is weakest from medium, where the new hires' paid weeks need experienced colleagues beside them. The optimum's normalized quality is 1.008–1.114 (median 1.052 over the audit's 45 settings; 1.037, 1.046 and 1.055 on world seeds 1–3), and quality_cap is 1.06.

Literal solution (literal_solution: the request done as written, the same in every setting): a greedy with the readings the agent's view gives: the forecast file covered wherever it can be ("enough people on the phones so customers aren't left waiting", a missing agent weighted above any shift's pay), skills, shift types, one shift a day and the leave file's day/month dates with approved requests only, the usual five-day week for everyone (the view gives no weekly cap), the agent in the team lead's note rostered as usual, and no holiday cut, rest time, overtime rule or new-hire pairing. It is invalid in all 9 public settings, as delivered and with the bendable overtime_approval relaxed: its 10-hour turnarounds break the rest time and its five-shift weeks the part-time contract week in every setting (and the full-time one at 32 hours), and from medium it also breaks the buddy rule (and in hard the sick call). With any one of the rules it breaks kept (shortcuts literal_plus_*), it is still invalid everywhere: the client's facts carry its failure, not one rule.
