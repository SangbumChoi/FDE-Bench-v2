The client's request, the only text the agent gets up front. It is the same in every setting.

Our managers sent these questions. Can you answer them from the database export? The tables, a short data dictionary and the list of questions are attached — please send back one number per question.

Deliverable spec (shown to the extractor that reshapes the agent's files, not to the agent)

answers.json = {"Q01": <number or null>, "Q02": <number or null>, ..., "Q12": <number or null>}
Every question id from the questions file (Q01..Q12) is a key. Its value is one JSON number, or null where the
engineer gave no figure for that question ("don't know", "not available yet"). Money in KRW, percentages in
percent (e.g. 4.25 for 4.25%), customer ids as the numeric customer_id.

Case notes (README)

BI Data Analyst — data_knowledge/bi_data_analyst

A coffee and tea subscription shop that sells boxes and one-off items to customers in Korea, Japan and the US (head office in Seoul, all reporting in KRW) sends the agent a CSV export of its order database, a short data dictionary and twelve questions from its managers: "Can you answer them from the database export? ... please send back one number per question." The deliverable is answers.json with every question id ({"Q01": ..., ..., "Q12": ...}), each a number or null. null means "don't know": the CEO's board pack shows the figure as "not available yet". The request names none of the definitions that decide the numbers, and it does not say what a wrong figure costs. Both are in the hidden requirements, and the simulated client gives them only when asked.

The case has nine ground truths: three tiers × three rule sets. The agent sees the same request and the same data in all nine (the validator checks that its prompt and workspace are byte-identical). What changes is which questions the board pack needs this round, the client's conventions (fiscal year, active window, rounding unit) and what a right, a "don't know" and a wrong answer are worth. The simulated client answers from the hidden requirements of the setting, and the evaluator scores under it. So the case measures two things: whether the agent finds the definitions and gets the answers right, and whether it says "don't know" when it should (calibrated abstention). The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

Answers and score

Every key Q01..Q12 must be present, with a finite number or null. A missing key, or a value that is neither, breaks answer_format (physical). Each question the tier scores earns a reward:

wrongdon't know (null)right
A012
B00.52
C01.52

The objective (maximize) is the total reward divided by 2 × the number of scored questions, a number in [0, 1]. A sheet that is right everywhere scores 1. A sheet that says "don't know" everywhere scores 0.5, 0.25 or 0.75. An answer or a null to a question the tier does not score has no effect. The metrics are scored, answered, right, wrong, dont_know, accuracy_on_answered (right / answered), coverage (answered / scored), the total reward, and the lists of wrong and "don't know" questions.

Abstention thresholds. A sheet that knows the scheme should answer a question only when its chance p of being right beats "don't know": p × 2 > dont_know_reward, i.e. p > 50% under A, p > 25% under B and p > 75% under C. The client states the scheme as a business fact, and it is a clarification target (answer_rewards, kind missing): "a wrong figure that reaches the board has to be corrected at the next meeting and costs us credibility, while 'not available yet' only leaves a gap", followed by the three values.

Correctness is unchanged from the case without settings, except for the money tolerance. Q03, Q04, Q05 and Q10 must match exactly. Money must be within 0.5% or half the rounding unit, whichever is wider (500, 5,000 or 50 KRW), so the exact figure rounded as the rule set requires always counts. Q11 must be within 0.05 percentage points, and Q08 and Q12 within 0.5%. A percentage given as a fraction is accepted. violating_examples asserts that the exact answers, rounded to the setting's unit, score 1.0 with no violation, in every setting and on every world the validator builds.

Situations present

  • Hidden facts: the eleven targets above. The data is built so that each one shows up. A QA load test on test account 10027 (36 orders of ten hand grinders and five 1 kg blends, April 2025 to March 2026) makes it the top customer under every fiscal year when test accounts stay in. Five test orders land in the first minutes of the 2026-07-01 sale (Korea time), which is still 2026-06-30 in UTC. Paid cancellations have payment rows. Four pantry products were launched under the new label, while the four older ones still say snacks. October refunds of September orders move Q11 by about 3 points (2–4 on seeds 1–3).
  • Calibrated abstention: the reward scheme is a hidden fact too. An agent that has not found a definition, or cannot settle a figure, should weigh its chance of being right against the rule set's threshold. Saying "don't know" everywhere earns 0.25–0.75, which is the baseline (quality 0); answering everything at face value earns 0–0.4 at the public seed, less than that.
  • Dated events, pressure: none in the case itself (see REVIEW.md F6). The messy engagement adds the framework's generic events.

Reference and baseline numbers (public seed 20261026)

The reference is a careful analyst who uses the setting's conventions and gets every definition right except the category rename: it answers the "pantry" question from the pantry label alone. It leaves the questions the tier does not score as null. Its abstention policy is principled and deterministic: its pantry figure covers only the products launched since 2025-10, so it gives that answer even odds of being right and keeps it only when ½ × right_reward > dont_know_reward. That holds under B (1 > 0.5), so it answers and is wrong. Under A (1 = 1, a tie abstains) and C (1 < 1.5) it says "don't know". In easy and medium Q06 is not scored, so there the reference is right on every scored question.

The baseline is what finance reports without an analyst: "not available yet" (null) for every question. It earns the rule set's don't-know reward on every scored question (0.5 / 0.25 / 0.75 under A / B / C), so saying "don't know" to everything scores 0 on the normalized scale. The face-value sheet is the export summed with none of the client's definitions: calendar-year periods on the stored UTC dates, every order whatever its status, revenue = order total less the refunds dated in the period, every currency at the 2026-09-30 rate, active = ordered in the last 30 days, "pantry" = the pantry label. It answers every question, keeps the two finance policies the client applies anyway (test accounts out, money rounded to the setting's unit), and scores below the baseline in every setting: its wrong figures earn nothing.

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review — data_knowledge/bi_data_analyst

Findings from the case review, checked again against the code at the public seed (20261026) and on world seeds 1–20. F1, F3, F8 and F9 were fixed when the review was first addressed (the "first change" below). F10 is the case owner's request: three tiers × three rule sets, and answers that can be right, "don't know" or wrong, with rewards that differ by rule set. It is done in the second change, which also moves F2, F4 and F5. The rest are open proposals for the case owner.

F1 — The rounding rule made Q07 and Q09 unanswerable (high, partly)

Money answers had to be whole thousands of KRW, but they were scored within 0.5% of the unrounded figure. For the per-order averages this fails: Q07 = 41,618 rounds to 42,000 (+0.92%) and Q09 = 35,780 rounds to 36,000 (+0.62%). So a sheet that followed the rule could score at most 10/12, and the stored reference scored 9. The rounded exact answers passed on only 35% (Q07) and 45% (Q09) of seeds 1–20. Bending report_rounding raised the score: a UTC-day sheet scored 7/12 rounded and 9/12 exact.

Status: fixed in the first change. A money answer is now correct within 0.5% or 500 KRW of the exact figure, whichever is wider, so the exact figure rounded to the nearest thousand always counts. The "How the answers are judged" section and the report_rounding target say so in all five locales. violating_examples now asserts that the exact answers, rounded as finance requires, score 12/12 with no violation on every world the validator builds; they do on seeds 1–20. The reference rises from 9 to 11 (Q07 and Q09 now count), and following the rule no longer costs a question: the UTC-day sheet scores 9/12 rounded or exact. The verifier's objection stands: no general "bending never helps" check was added, since bendable rules elsewhere are meant to cost objective. Second change: the rounding unit is now a rule-set parameter (1,000 / 10,000 / 100 KRW), the money tolerance is 0.5% or half the unit, and the same assertion (objective 1.0, no violation) runs in all nine settings.

F2 — No headroom between the reference and the ceiling (high, confirmed)

The reference is compute() with the category rename missed. Under the old clipped scale it scored 9, and any sheet with 9 or more correct got full quality. With the F1 fix and the new scale the problem is reversed. The reference scores 11 and the baseline 0, so a perfect sheet has normalized quality 12/11 = 1.09. Under the policy cap of 1.5, a perfect sheet earns only 73% of the quality credit, and the rename (Q06) is the only fact that separates it from the reference.

Status: open, and changed by the second change (F10). The reference still misses only the rename, and Q06 is scored only in hard. So in easy and medium the reference is right on every scored question and is the ceiling: a perfect sheet has normalized quality 1.000 in all six of those settings. Under the policy cap of 1.5 the best possible sheet earns 67% of the quality credit there. In hard the perfect sheet scores 1.091 (A), 1.125 (B) and 1.091 (C) at the public seed: the reference leaves Q06 as "don't know" under A and C and gets it wrong under B. Its objective is 0.958 / 0.917 / 0.979 on every seed checked (1–20), and the baselines move the normalized figure only slightly. The earlier proposal (quality_cap: 1.09) no longer fits. One case-wide cap cannot give full credit to the perfect sheet in all nine settings: a cap of 1.0 would give a reference-level sheet full credit in hard as well, and a cap above 1.0 leaves easy and medium short. So the second change sets no quality_cap. Proposals: (a) a per-tier quality_cap in the framework (1.0 for easy and medium, 1.10 for hard); (b) give the reference one deliberate gap in every tier, e.g. a question it cannot settle in easy and in medium, so that it abstains there too; (c) weight each question by the number of binding targets it exercises, so that every hidden fact costs about the same.

F3 — Refunds dated after the reference date, and a hair-trigger Q11 tolerance (high, partly)

refunds.csv has 23 refunds dated 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-18 (Korea time), worth 853,041 KRW ex tax. The truth counts them against their order's period. The requirements already gave the booking rule but not the export date. Cutting refunds off at the reference date moves Q11 from −2.57% to +0.38%. Q11 was scored within 0.5% of itself, i.e. ±0.013 percentage points, or about ±3,700 KRW on 29 M KRW of monthly revenue.

Status: fixed in the first change, using the text option, which leaves the data and every truth unchanged. The requirements in all five locales now say that the export was taken on 2026-10-31 and holds the orders placed up to the reference date with every refund recorded until then, and that October refunds count against their order's period. The net_revenue target said the same; in the second change that clause is its own target, late_refunds (medium and hard, where Q11 is scored). By construction no refund can fall after 2026-10-29; the latest on seeds 1–20 is 2026-10-26. Q11 is now scored within ±0.05 percentage points, so a change reported to one decimal place counts. The October refunds still cost a sheet that drops them: 1–2 questions, Q11 and sometimes Q07. A tax-inclusive Q11 is off by only 0.015–0.052 points and now usually passes, but that sheet still loses Q01, Q02, Q07 and Q09.

F4 — Some target clauses never change an answer (medium, partly)

Three clauses never change an answer: refunds at the refund-date FX rate (0/12 change), line-matched rather than pro-rata refund allocation (Q06 moves 0.28%), and an 89-day active window. With the F1 tolerance, the refund cut-off and active window variants now cost only their own question (Q11, Q03). Before, Q07 and Q09 also failed by the rounding lottery.

Possible data changes: an FX move between order and refund dates large enough to move a money answer by more than 0.5%; partial-quantity refunds that do not equal a line amount; completed orders on 2026-07-02 and 2026-07-03 from customers with no other order in the window. Then check on seeds 1–3 that each target, and each clause the client is asked about, flips at least one answer.

Second change (partly addressed). The net_revenue target is split by tier. net_revenue keeps the tax and booking clauses, which bind in every tier (tax-inclusive: Q01 in easy, 3–5 questions in medium and hard). The October-refund clause is late_refunds (medium, hard): Q11 in every rule set, plus Q02 under B. The pro-rata clause moved into category_rename (hard). The 89-day point is now a one-day-short window under each rule set: it costs Q03 in every setting at the public seed and on seeds 1–3, except A at the public seed and B at seed 1.

F6 — No dated event and no pressure (medium, confirmed)

The case also had no settings (F5).
