Case notes (README.md)

BI Data Analyst — data_knowledge/bi_data_analyst

A coffee and tea subscription shop that sells boxes and one-off items to customers in Korea, Japan and the US (head office in Seoul, all reporting in KRW) sends the agent a CSV export of its order database, a short data dictionary and twelve questions from its managers: "Can you answer them from the database export? ... please send back one number per question." The deliverable is answers.json, one JSON number per question id ({"Q01": ..., ..., "Q12": ...}). The request names none of the definitions that decide the numbers; they are in the hidden requirements, and the simulated client gives them only when asked.

The case has no settings: it is the same problem at every difficulty. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

Quality is the number of questions answered correctly against the exact (unrounded) figures from the world: Q03, Q04, Q05 and Q10 exactly; money within 0.5% or 500 KRW, whichever is wider (so the exact figure rounded to the nearest thousand always counts); Q11 within 0.05 percentage points; Q08 and Q12 within 0.5%. A percentage given as a fraction (0.0610 for 6.10%) is accepted.

Situations present

  • Hidden facts: the nine targets above. The data is built so that each one shows up: a QA load test on test account 10027 (36 orders of ten hand grinders and five 1 kg blends in FY2025) makes it the top customer when test accounts stay in; five test orders land in the first minutes of the 2026-07-01 sale (Korea time), which is still 2026-06-30 in UTC; paid cancellations have payment rows; four pantry products were launched under the new label while the four older ones still say snacks; October refunds of September orders move Q11 by about 3 points (2–4 on seeds 1–3).
  • Dated events, pressure: none in the case itself (see REVIEW.md F6); the messy engagement adds the framework's generic events.

Notes

  • Synthetic. Prices, tax rates, FX paths, order rhythms and refund rates are invented; the shop, its customers and its accounts are fictional. The fiscal-year, local-day, test-account, net-revenue and rename conventions are the common traps of BI work on raw exports, not facts about a real company.
  • Known simplifications (REVIEW.md F7): a flat 7% US tax on every order, FX rates on weekends, paid cancellations without a reversal row, and sign-ups from 2024-07-01 while the order history starts on 2025-01-01.

Review findings (REVIEW.md)

Review — data_knowledge/bi_data_analyst

Findings from the case review, checked again against the code at the public seed (20261026) and on world seeds 1–20. F1, F3, F8 and F9 are fixed in this change. The rest are open proposals for the case owner.

F1 — The rounding rule made Q07 and Q09 unanswerable (high, partly)

Money answers had to be whole thousands of KRW, but they were scored within 0.5% of the unrounded figure. For the per-order averages this fails: Q07 = 41,618 rounds to 42,000 (+0.92%) and Q09 = 35,780 rounds to 36,000 (+0.62%). So a sheet that followed the rule could score at most 10/12, and the stored reference scored 9. The rounded exact answers passed on only 35% (Q07) and 45% (Q09) of seeds 1–20. Bending report_rounding raised the score: a UTC-day sheet scored 7/12 rounded and 9/12 exact.

Status: fixed in this change. A money answer is now correct within 0.5% or 500 KRW of the exact figure, whichever is wider, so the exact figure rounded to the nearest thousand always counts. The "How the answers are judged" section and the report_rounding target say so in all five locales. violating_examples now asserts that the exact answers, rounded as finance requires, score 12/12 with no violation on every world the validator builds; they do on seeds 1–20. The reference rises from 9 to 11 (Q07 and Q09 now count), and following the rule no longer costs a question: the UTC-day sheet scores 9/12 rounded or exact. The verifier's objection stands: no general "bending never helps" check was added, since bendable rules elsewhere are meant to cost objective.

F2 — No headroom between the reference and the ceiling (high, confirmed)

The reference is compute() with the category rename missed. Under the old clipped scale it scored 9, and any sheet with 9 or more correct got full quality. With the F1 fix and the new scale the problem is reversed. The reference scores 11 and the baseline 0, so a perfect sheet has normalized quality 12/11 = 1.09. Under the policy cap of 1.5, a perfect sheet earns only 73% of the quality credit, and the rename (Q06) is the only fact that separates it from the reference.

Status: open. Proposal: set quality_cap: 1.09 in case.yaml. This is the normalized quality of the known best sheet, and (12 − b) / (11 − b) ≥ 12/11 for any baseline b ≥ 0, so 12/12 gets full credit on every seed. As an alternative, make the reference the full 12 and let the baseline carry the scale. Consider also weighting each question by the number of binding targets it exercises, so that every hidden fact costs about the same.

F3 — Refunds dated after the reference date, and a hair-trigger Q11 tolerance (high, partly)

refunds.csv has 23 refunds dated 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-18 (Korea time), worth 853,041 KRW ex tax. The truth counts them against their order's period. The requirements already gave the booking rule but not the export date. Cutting refunds off at the reference date moves Q11 from −2.57% to +0.38%. Q11 was scored within 0.5% of itself, i.e. ±0.013 percentage points, or about ±3,700 KRW on 29 M KRW of monthly revenue.

Status: fixed in this change, using the text option, which leaves the data and every truth unchanged. The requirements in all five locales now say that the export was taken on 2026-10-31 and holds the orders placed up to the reference date with every refund recorded until then, and that October refunds count against their order's period. The net_revenue target says the same. By construction no refund can fall after 2026-10-29; the latest on seeds 1–20 is 2026-10-26. Q11 is now scored within ±0.05 percentage points, so a change reported to one decimal place counts. The October refunds still cost a sheet that drops them: 1–2 questions, Q11 and sometimes Q07. A tax-inclusive Q11 is off by only 0.015–0.052 points and now usually passes, but that sheet still loses Q01, Q02, Q07 and Q09.

F4 — Some target clauses never change an answer (medium, partly)

The verifier reproduced the numbers. Three clauses never change an answer: refunds at the refund-date FX rate (0/12 change), line-matched rather than pro-rata refund allocation (Q06 moves 0.28%), and an 89-day active window. Test accounts are visible as account_type = test. New data point: at world seed 2 the latest-rate FX shortcut costs no question at all, so fx_order_date binds only on some worlds. With the F1 tolerance, the refund cut-off and active window variants now cost only their own question (Q11, Q03). Before, Q07 and Q09 also failed by the rounding lottery.

Status: open. Proposal: make the sub-clauses bind, or drop them from the target texts. Possible data changes: an FX move between order and refund dates large enough to move a money answer by more than 0.5%; partial-quantity refunds that do not equal a line amount; completed orders on 2026-07-02 and 2026-07-03 from customers with no other order in the window. Then check on seeds 1–3 that each target, and each clause the client is asked about, flips at least one answer.

F5 — No settings and no README (medium, confirmed)

case.yaml declares no tiers or rule sets, so all nine difficulty × rule-set cells score the same problem. The convention parameters are already easy to lift out of compute(): FY start and naming flips 6–8 answers, the FX convention 0–5, and the active window flips Q03. The verifier found that booking refunds in the refund period flips 2 answers (Q08, Q11), not 4 as the finding said.

Status: open. The README is added in this change. Proposal for the settings:

  • Tiers. Easy scores Q01, Q03, Q04, Q10 and Q12, covering counted orders, test and staff accounts, FY and rounding; the other questions are answered with null "this round". Medium adds net revenue, FX and Korea time. Hard adds the rename, the October refunds and the F6 situations.
  • Rule sets vary the FY start (April / July / January), the active window (90 / 60 / 30 days), the rounding unit (1,000 / 10,000 / 100) and the FX convention. Score the rounding unit with the half-unit tolerance from F1.
  • Check that each rule set's sheet loses at least one question under the other two.
F6 — No dated event and no pressure (medium, confirmed)

The case has only hidden facts. The QA load test on account 10027, which makes it the top FY2025 customer when test accounts stay in, is an obvious temptation that nobody voices. After F1, report_rounding costs nothing to follow, so the case no longer has a bendable rule that it pays to bend.

Status: open. Proposal: add a visible pressure note, identical in every setting. For example, a sales director's line in the questions file: "Q04 should be 10027, our big B2B pilot." The right move is to follow the finance policy or escalate. Add a dated event, e.g. a mid-task message that a staff account was reclassified as test from a given date, or a corrected fx_rates.csv for one week. Add a business-policy rule whose bending is reasonable and changes an answer, so that asking to bend it is a real trade-off.

F7 — Seoul head office with a non-Korean working language; implausible data details (low, confirmed)

Every locale describes a Seoul company that reports in KRW. Its labels are nevertheless "in our working language" (Spanish, Japanese, Chinese). The data also has a flat 7% US tax, FX rates on weekends, 391 paid cancellations with no reversal row, and sign-ups from 2024-07-01 while orders start on 2025-01-01.

Status: open. Proposal: make the client the Madrid, Tokyo or Shanghai sales office of a Seoul group that reports group figures in KRW. That keeps one world and one truth. Add reversal rows for paid cancellations, which keeps the trap. Use business-day FX with a "last available rate" rule (a new hidden fact). State in the data dictionary that the order history starts on 2025-01-01.

F8 — Korean wording: two half-years and an ambiguous Q12 (low, confirmed)

The Korean Q05 called the 2026-07-01 sale "상반기 결산 세일" (calendar first half), while Q07 uses "FY2026 상반기" for April–September. That nudges toward a calendar fiscal year. Q12 "몇 개의 상품" admits a distinct-products reading: 1.66 lines per order against the true 2.105 units.

Status: fixed in this change. The Korean questions file now reads "Q05: 여름 세일 당일(2026-07-01)…" and "Q12: FY2025 주문 1건당 평균 상품 수량(낱개 기준)은 얼마인가요?". The ja (中間セール, 点数) and zh (年中大促, 件) wording has no such collision, so it is unchanged. The category label 팬트리 is kept; that point is a matter of taste and was not in the fix list.

F9 — The test-account gate is narrower than its rule text (low, partly)

The gate only checks Q03, Q04, Q05 and Q10 for exact equality with the Korea-time figure that includes test accounts. Money answers that include test accounts pass validity (8/12, no violation), and so does a sheet that excludes only account 10027 (4/12). The requirements already described the gate correctly; case.yaml did not.

Status: fixed in this change. The case.yaml rule now says "a count or customer-id answer (Q03, Q04, Q05, Q10) that equals the figure with test accounts included, where that differs from the figure without them". One point is left for the owner. The comparison always uses Korea-time days, so a sheet that excludes test accounts but uses UTC days can match by coincidence: at world seed 1, its Q10 = 639 equals the test-inclusive figure and is flagged. Proposal: do not flag an answer that equals the test-free figure under either day convention.

Baseline: the export summed at face value (calendar-year periods on UTC dates, every order status, tax-inclusive totals less refunds dated in the period, the 2026-09-30 FX rate, a 30-day active window, the pantry label only), with test accounts excluded and money rounded to thousands so that it is valid. It scores 0/12 against the reference's 11/12 at the public seed, 1, 0 and 0 on world seeds 1–3, and 0–2 on seeds 1–20; the exact answers rounded to thousands score 12.
