# Ground truth: data_knowledge/bi_data_analyst

The ground truth (GT) of a setting in five parts, with the reference as supporting evidence. Generated by `gt_report.py` from `dataset/audit.json` (`fdebench2 audit-case`, public world seed 20261026): 45 settings, easy 15, medium 15, hard 15 (each tier's public panel plus admissible parameter combinations). Damage is 1 − credit, credit = 0 for an invalid sheet, else the quality score clipped to [0, 1]; a target bites in a setting when misreading it alone loses at least 0.10 of the credit (for a drawn parameter: its least harmful wrong candidate). The sheet has twelve answers; 7 / 11 / 12 of them are scored in easy / medium / hard.

## 2. Validity checks (hard gates)

A sheet that breaks any of these is invalid and earns no credit. `answers.json` that cannot be read as an object is fatal.

| rule | class | tiers | why it is a hard gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `answer_format` | physical | easy, medium, hard | the board pack has one slot per question: a missing id or a value that is neither a number nor `null` cannot be placed (mandatory output: all twelve ids, whether the round uses them or not) |
| `exclude_test_accounts` | contractual | easy, medium, hard | finance policy: QA accounts are not customers; the load-test account would be the board's "top customer" |
| `report_rounding` | contractual | easy, medium, hard | the board pack's tables are in the client's unit; a figure in another precision cannot be pasted in (it was the bendable rule before the audit: bending it gained nothing, since a rounded figure always counts as right) |
| `running_period` | business_policy | easy, medium, hard (when fy_start in [7, 9]) | finance's habit: no part-period figure under a full-period heading. The one bendable rule: the CFO can approve a figure to date, which is then judged against the exact figure to date |

**Mandatory outputs.** Every question id Q01 to Q12, each a finite number or `null` (`answer_format`). Nothing else is mandatory: `null` is always an accepted answer and earns the null's reward, so abstaining is priced by the objective, not forbidden. The count gate (`exclude_test_accounts`) does not hold a count against a sheet when another reading of the export without test accounts gives the same count (the stored UTC dates, another fiscal year or window, other orders counted): only a figure that needs the test accounts breaks it.

**The bendable habit.** `running_period` is the one business-policy rule. With an approved exception the rule is switched off and the figure to date is judged like any other answer; the policy then takes its soft-rule penalty off the final score (0.1 under `enterprise_balanced`, 0.05 under `startup_velocity`), in which quality is one component (60% under `enterprise_balanced`, 60% under `startup_velocity` when every component applies). The table gives the reference sheet with its nulls replaced by the exact figures to date: its quality gain over the reference with the rule relaxed (the same for every null reward: k running questions of n scored add k / (n − k)), and what the bend nets in the final score of a sheet below the quality cap under each policy that allows it (the quality share × the gain − the penalty). So the bend pays in some settings and loses in others. A sheet already at the cap (1.0) gains nothing, so for it the bend always loses the penalty; under an April year no period is running and there is nothing to bend.

## 3. Objective (higher is better)

objective = Σ over the scored questions of the answer's reward / (2 × scored questions):

- **Right**: 2. The customer id and the counts (Q03, Q04, Q05, Q10) must match exactly; a money figure must be within 0.5% or half the rounding unit of the exact figure, whichever is wider, so the exact figure rounded as the rule requires always counts; the September change (Q11) within 0.05 percentage points; Q08 and Q12 within 0.5%.
- **`null`** ("not available yet"): the drawn reward, 0.5, 1 or 1.5.
- **Wrong**: 0. A wrong figure in the board pack has to be corrected at the next meeting; a gap only leaves a gap.
- A question the round does not use counts neither way.

**Uncertainty.** The reward for a null prices the analyst's own doubt: a figure is worth giving only when its chance of being right is above null reward / 2 (25%, 50% or 75%). The all-null sheet is the baseline (quality 0), so abstaining everywhere earns nothing and a wrong figure costs null reward / (2 − null reward) of a right one (a third, one, or three). One kind of question has no figure at all: a period that is still running on the reference date (the first half of FY2026 under a July year; its first quarter too under a September year). Finance shows "not available yet" for it, so `null` is the right answer there, and the most a valid sheet earns on it is the null's reward.

## 4. Reference and bounds

- **Reference** (`reference_solution`): a careful analyst who knows every one of the setting's conventions. Each scored question's exact figure, money rounded to the setting's unit; `null` for the questions the round does not use and for a period still running. (Before the audit it missed the category rename on purpose, so that the reward for a null changed its sheet in hard; it now enforces every fact in force.)
- **Baseline** (quality 0, `baseline_solution`): "not available yet" for every question, what finance reports without an analyst.
- **Best known** (`best_known_solution`): the answer key for all twelve questions, whether the round uses them or not: another sheet than the reference in easy and medium, with the same objective.
- **Bound** (`objective_bound`, proven): a scored question earns at most 2, and one whose period is still running at most the null's reward, because a number there breaks `running_period`. The reference reaches it: the gap is 0 in every measured setting. Its scope is every valid sheet, the habit kept: a figure to date makes the sheet invalid, so it cannot beat the bound. A sheet that bends the habit under an approved exception is judged with the rule off and ends above it (quality up to 1 + k / (n − k), the table in part 2); the quality cap of 1.0 stops there, so a bent sheet earns no more quality credit than the reference and pays the penalty, and the reference keeps full credit without an exception.

- **quality_cap** = 1.0: the best known quality is 1.000 over the measured settings (median 1.000), and the bound is 0.0% above the reference, so a sheet as good as the reference earns full credit and no valid sheet scores above it.

## 5. Diagnostic cases

`diagnose()` reports `enforced[target]` and a line of evidence for every target in force, for valid and invalid sheets alike, from the sheet's own figures (a run's scoring and the audit ask for it; the validator does not). The diagnostic cases are the scored questions where only that fact decides the figure: the export is computed under every other reading (a fiscal year from each other month, the other naming, each misreading of a fixed fact listed in case.py `MISREADINGS` and its `VARIANTS`, other active windows), and a question tells a reading from the client's when that reading's figure, exact or rounded to the unit, would be scored wrong. A right answer there counts for the fact (not where only a variant differs: staying clear of US time says little about Korea time). A wrong figure counts against the one fact whose reading gives it as a sheet would write it (rounded to the unit or to two decimals, or within 0.1%: five times tighter than the scorer's tolerance), the nearest when several do; a figure that two facts' readings give alike, or that no single reading explains, tells nothing. A fact is not enforced when more questions carry some misreading's figure than the client's, enforced when some carry the client's, and not determinable otherwise.
