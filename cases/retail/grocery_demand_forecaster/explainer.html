What the agent is asked

The client's request, the only text the agent gets up front. It is the same in every setting.

Can you forecast how much of each product each of our five stores will sell every day for the next two weeks, starting Monday 2 November? We place our orders from these numbers. The sales history and our master data are attached, with a couple of other files.

Deliverable spec (shown to the extractor that reshapes the agent's files, not to the agent)

forecast.csv with ASCII header store_id,item_id,date,forecast
One row per store x item x date in the 14-day horizon (2026-11-02 .. 2026-11-15);
store_id / item_id exactly as in the data (e.g. S1, I01), date as YYYY-MM-DD,
forecast = the forecast quantity in the item's current sales unit (a number, decimals allowed).

Case notes (README)

Grocery Demand Forecaster — retail/grocery_demand_forecaster

A regional grocery chain with five stores asks the agent to forecast "how much of each product each of our five stores will sell every day for the next two weeks, starting Monday 2 November", because it places its orders from these numbers. The deliverable is forecast.csv (store_id,item_id,date,forecast): one row for every store (S1–S5) × item (I01–I12) × day from 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-15, 840 rows, in each item's current sales unit. The forecast is scored against the horizon's actuals, which live only in the world.

The case has settings: three tiers × rule seeds (DESIGN.md, Settings). The data and the request are the same in every setting; only the client's hidden rules, what the forecast is judged against, and so the right forecast change. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

Tiers

TierWhat the forecast must doOut of scope (said in the client's words)
easyThe base forecast: a normal day per store × item, judged against base till sales (net of returns, stockout days as recorded, no promotion and no holiday effect). S4's Sunday closure, the display floor and the error weight applyThe promotions and the one-off holiday ("our category team adds the effect … so leave both out"); stockout correction ("comes in a later round"); bread rolls I07 ("not judged this round … keep their rows in the file")
mediumEasy + the promotion plan (inclusive dates, the lift by category and discount learned from past promotions, times the flyer's multiple when one is drawn) and the one-off regional holiday on its drawn date; still judged against till sales net of returnsStockout correction and I07, as in easy
hardMedium judged against customer demand: stockout days are censored, returns are not demand, I07 is judged (its unit change: pieces until 31 May 2026, packs of 6 since), and a dated revision: on Friday 30 October, after promo_plan.csv was exported, the drawn category's promotions were cancelled—

The tiers change the truth, not only the rules: the world draws one realization per cell (day noise, a Poisson quantile, the stockout and return draws) and each setting evaluates it under its own mean (with or without the promotions, the flyer, the cancellation and the holiday) and as till sales or as demand, so all settings share the same luck.

Data

  • sales_history (43,718 rows, 2024-11-04 to 2026-11-01, 104 weeks): one sales row per store, item and trading day with units_sold, promo_discount_pct (0 = no promotion) and a stockout flag (4,390 stockout rows, where recorded sales are 30–85% of demand), plus 1,286 separate customer-return rows with negative units (−2,104 units against 2,002,817 sold). S4 has no Sunday rows.
  • promo_plan: 14 store × item promotions in the horizon (10/20/30% off, start and end inclusive); at the case seed 7 beverages, 3 bakery, 2 dairy and 2 produce promotions. On every world seed the plan has at least one promotion in each category the 30 October cancellation can draw (beverages, dairy, bakery).
  • holidays: 12 past public holidays (Christmas, New Year's Day, a spring, summer and autumn holiday, Labour Day, each twice). None falls in the horizon, and the one-off holiday (on the drawn date) is not listed.
  • stores (four supermarkets and the Old Town express store S4) and items (12 items in four categories: fresh produce I01–I03, dairy I04–I06, bakery I07–I09, beverages I10–I12, with their sales unit). Bread rolls (I07) are listed in packs of 6; the history records them per piece until 31 May 2026.

The data is unchanged by the conversion (byte-identical to the case without settings). The world is a multiplicative model per store × item × day (store scale and trend, category weekday profile and yearly season, promotion lift by category and discount, holiday lift by category) with lognormal day noise (σ = 0.07) and Poisson sales. Stockouts happen with an item's base probability, plus 0.20 on promotion days, independently of the day's demand. In the horizon, the flyer multiplies a promotion's extra demand; till sales are demand cut to 30–85% on a stockout day (same probabilities) minus a return of 1–2 units on 3% of cells.

Reference and baseline

The reference is a weekday-level model per store × item, from the history only: a weekday profile from the last 52 weeks of usable days without promotion or holiday, a level from the last 8 weeks of such days, a category season factor from last year's level around the horizon, a promotion lift per category and discount and a holiday lift per category, both against the local level, and history before 1 June 2026 divided by 6 for I07. Then the setting: the promotions this tier forecasts (lift × the flyer's multiple on the extra part, none for the cancelled category), the holiday lift on the drawn day, and the short_weight / (1 + short_weight) quantile of the store × item's ratio of actual to fitted value over the last 26 weeks (the median at weight 1); S4's Sundays at 0 and the floor on core items. In easy and medium it fits till sales as recorded (stockout days in, returns netted, as the client describes the actuals); in hard it fits demand (stockout days and return rows out).

The baseline is the seasonal naive forecast a store manager would use without an engineer, the same in every setting: what the till recorded on the same weekday of the last history week (26 October to 1 November), with S4's Sundays at 0 and the drawn floor on core items. It makes no stockout, promotion, holiday or weight adjustment.

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review — retail/grocery_demand_forecaster

Findings of the case review, each with the verifier's verdict and its status after this change. Quality numbers below use the new scale (seasonal-naive baseline 0, reference 1) unless they are marked as ratios.

F1 — No settings: one fixed problem at every difficulty (high, partly)

case.yaml declares no tiers, rule sets or param_labels; the only ground truth is gt/default. So the difficulty column says nothing about this case, and "which rules apply this time" is never tested. Settings-less cases are allowed by DESIGN.md (8 of the 13 built cases have none), so this is a coverage gap, not a contract breach.

Status: open — proposal: convert to 3×3 while keeping the visible data byte-identical. Easy = base demand only (requirements say promotions and the 11 November holiday are handled elsewhere this round); medium adds the promotion plan, the holiday and the I07 unit change; hard adds the parts that need new scored truth (a dated revision, a closure, pressure, asymmetric cost on perishables). Rule-set parameters should be ones the data does not reveal: holiday date (Wed 11 / Fri 13 / Mon 9 Nov), floor (6 on produce + dairy / 4 on dairy only / 8 on produce only), a future closure (none / S2 on 15 Nov / S5 on the holiday). Do not use store-mean vs. volume-weighted WAPE as a rule-set pair (same per-cell minimizer, so the references would not differ by 1%), and do not put informative censoring in hard only (it changes the visible history).

Status (rule seeds): resolved — converted to 3 tiers and 5 parameters drawn by rule seeds; see "Rule seeds" below. The data stays byte-identical. Easy is the base forecast only (the requirements say the category team adds the promotions and the one-off holiday this round) and is scored against base till sales; medium adds the promotion plan and the holiday; hard adds what the owner placed there: stockout censoring and returns (hard is judged against demand, the lower tiers against till sales net of returns), the I07 unit change (I07 is judged in hard only) and a dated promotion revision. The proposal's store-mean vs. volume-weighted WAPE pair was not used; the asymmetric cost became the error weight short_weight in every tier. Rule seeds

The case moved from one fixed problem to the rule-seed contract (DESIGN.md, Settings): three tiers in case.yaml and five parameters that a rule seed draws, each on its own, from a pool per tier. The data and request.md are byte-identical in every setting and to the case before the conversion; the requirements, targets, rules and the truth the forecast is scored against follow the tier and the drawn values.

  • Tiers. Easy: the base forecast, scored against base till sales (net of returns, stockout days as recorded, without the promotion and holiday effects); the requirements say in the client's words that the category team adds the promotions and the holiday itself, that stockout correction comes in a later round and that bread rolls (I07) are not judged this round (their rows stay in the file). Medium adds the promotion plan (with the flyer's multiple where drawn) and the one-off holiday on its drawn date, still against till sales and without I07. Hard is scored against customer demand (stockout days censored, returns not demand), judges I07 (its unit change) and adds the dated cancellation of 30 October.
  • Parameters. min_display (6, 4, 8: the display floor, rule min_display_stock), holiday_date (none in easy; 2026-11-11, 2026-11-13, 2026-11-09), promo_uplift (1 in easy; 1, 1.5, 2: the flyer's multiple of a past promotion's extra sales), promo_cancelled (none in easy and medium; beverages, dairy, bakery in hard) and short_weight (1, 2, 3: a unit forecast too low counts that many times a unit too high in the store's WAPE). Labels word the dates, multiples and categories in each locale. Public panel: rule seeds 3, 25 and 58, which show every candidate of every pool; seed 3 draws the binding check's start (hard/s3).
  • Truth. build_world keeps the history and the plan exactly as before and then draws, per horizon cell, the day noise, a Poisson quantile, the stockout draw, the share sold on a stockout day and a return draw (common random numbers). truth(world, rules) turns them into the setting's actuals: demand (hard) or till sales net of returns (easy, medium) under that setting's mean (promotions with the flyer and the cancellation, the holiday on its date). Scoring stays the store-mean WAPE against that one realization, with the error weight.
  • Reference and baseline. The reference keeps its weekday-level model, now with promotion lifts per category and discount and holiday lifts per category, fitted to till sales in easy and medium and to demand in hard, and forecasts the weight's quantile of its residual ratios before the floor. The baseline stays the seasonal naive forecast with the drawn floor. Baseline ÷ reference at the case seed: 1.64–1.98 (easy), 1.65–2.47 (medium), 1.95–3.20 (hard).
  • Binding. From hard/s3 every parameter changes the answer: the floor of 8 makes the reference invalid (and a floor of 4 lets the reference improve by 4.9%), another holiday date moves its objective by 28–33%, a flyer by 3–11%, another cancelled category by 6–17%, another error weight by 45–91%. On world seeds 1–20 the strongest alternative of each parameter makes the reference invalid, or moves or beats it by at least 5%.
  • Targets. 5 in easy (base_forecast is new), 7 in medium (promo_flyer new; the holiday target is now one_off_holiday with the drawn date), 11 in hard (promo_revision new; stockout_censoring, returns and unit_change_i07 are hard only). wape_metric states the weight and what the actuals are per tier.
  • Explainer and translations. locales/<lang>/explainer.yaml (zh, ko, ja, es) has the three tiers and the floor rule with {{min_display}}. README.<lang>.md and REVIEW.<lang>.md still describe the case without settings and are to be retranslated.
  • No test hard-codes this case; python -m pytest -q passes unchanged.
  • Check of the conversion (fixed). The plan's 14 promotions are a random sample of store × item pairs, and on some world seeds a category the cancellation can draw has none (no dairy promotion on world seed 6, no beverages on 8). A hard setting that draws that category then cancels nothing: the client's 30 October message is about promotions that do not exist, and the parameter changes nothing there (the validator's binding check still passes, since another category binds). build_world now makes sure every category of the promo_cancelled pool has a promotion in the plan: a plan that lacks one moves one promotion of its most promoted category (same store, dates and discount) to an item of the missing category, with its own random stream. The stored world and world seeds 1–5 already cover every category, so their data and answers are unchanged.
