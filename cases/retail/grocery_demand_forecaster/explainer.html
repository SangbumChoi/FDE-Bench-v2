Case notes (README.md)

Grocery Demand Forecaster — retail/grocery_demand_forecaster

A regional grocery chain with five stores asks the agent to forecast "how much of each product each of our five stores will sell every day for the next two weeks, starting Monday 2 November", because it places its orders from these numbers. The deliverable is forecast.csv (store_id,item_id,date,forecast): one row for every store (S1–S5) × item (I01–I12) × day from 2026-11-02 to 2026-11-15, 840 rows, in each item's current sales unit. The forecast is scored against the true customer demand of the horizon, which lives only in the world.

The case has no settings: the same request, data, hidden requirements and evaluator at every difficulty. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

Data

  • sales_history (43,718 rows, 2024-11-04 to 2026-11-01, 104 weeks): one sales row per store, item and trading day with units_sold, promo_discount_pct (0 = no promotion) and a stockout flag (4,390 stockout rows, where recorded sales are 30–85% of demand), plus 1,286 separate customer-return rows with negative units (−2,104 units against 2,002,817 sold). S4 has no Sunday rows.
  • promo_plan: 14 store × item promotions in the horizon (10/20/30% off, start and end inclusive).
  • holidays: 12 past public holidays (Christmas, New Year's Day, a spring, summer and autumn holiday, Labour Day, each twice). None falls in the horizon, and the one-off holiday on Wednesday 11 November is not listed.
  • stores (four supermarkets and the Old Town express store S4) and items (12 items in four categories: fresh produce I01–I03, dairy I04–I06, bakery I07–I09, beverages I10–I12, with their sales unit). Bread rolls (I07) are listed in packs of 6; the history records them per piece until 31 May 2026.

The world is a multiplicative model per store × item × day (store scale and trend, category weekday profile and yearly season, promotion lift by category and discount, holiday lift by category) with lognormal day noise (σ = 0.07) and Poisson sales. Stockouts happen with an item's base probability, plus 0.20 on promotion days, independently of the day's demand.

Rules

Quality is the WAPE per store (Σ |forecast − demand| ÷ Σ demand over its items and days), averaged over the five stores; lower is better. Demand includes what customers could not buy because of a stockout.

Situations present

Only hidden facts. The request says only "forecast what each store will sell"; the nine clarification targets are stockout censoring (forecast demand, not recorded sales), returns (leave out, do not net), S4's Sunday closure, the promotion plan (inclusive dates, lift by category and discount), the 11 November holiday, the I07 unit change, the display floor of 6, the WAPE metric and the file format. There are no case-specific dated events or pressure messages; the engagement axis supplies the only events.

Reference and baseline numbers (seed 20261102)

The reference is a censoring-aware weekday-level model per store × item, from the history only: a weekday profile from the last 52 weeks of clean days (no stockout, promotion or holiday), a level from the last 8 weeks of clean days, a category season factor from last year's level around the horizon, a promotion lift per discount level and one holiday lift, both pooled over all categories, history before 1 June 2026 divided by 6 for I07, returns left out, S4's Sundays at 0 and the floor of 6 on core items.

The baseline is the seasonal naive forecast a store manager would use without an engineer: what the till recorded on the same weekday of the last history week (26 October to 1 November), with S4's Sundays at 0 and the floor of 6 on core items. It makes no stockout, promotion or holiday adjustment.

Review findings (REVIEW.md)

Review — retail/grocery_demand_forecaster

Findings of the case review, each with the verifier's verdict and its status after this change. Quality numbers below use the new scale (seasonal-naive baseline 0, reference 1) unless they are marked as ratios.

F1 — No settings: one fixed problem at every difficulty (high, partly)

case.yaml declares no tiers, rule sets or param_labels; the only ground truth is gt/default. So the difficulty column says nothing about this case, and "which rules apply this time" is never tested. Settings-less cases are allowed by DESIGN.md (8 of the 13 built cases have none), so this is a coverage gap, not a contract breach.

Status: open — proposal: convert to 3×3 while keeping the visible data byte-identical. Easy = base demand only (requirements say promotions and the 11 November holiday are handled elsewhere this round); medium adds the promotion plan, the holiday and the I07 unit change; hard adds the parts that need new scored truth (a dated revision, a closure, pressure, asymmetric cost on perishables). Rule-set parameters should be ones the data does not reveal: holiday date (Wed 11 / Fri 13 / Mon 9 Nov), floor (6 on produce + dairy / 4 on dairy only / 8 on produce only), a future closure (none / S2 on 15 Nov / S5 on the holiday). Do not use store-mean vs. volume-weighted WAPE as a rule-set pair (same per-cell minimizer, so the references would not differ by 1%), and do not put informative censoring in hard only (it changes the visible history).

F2 — Little headroom above the reference (high, confirmed)

The reference WAPE is 0.1470, the oracle (true generator mean plus the floor) 0.1381, the baseline 0.2790. On the new scale the oracle reaches quality 1.067 (1.118 / 1.167 / 1.072 on world seeds 1–3), and per-category promotion and holiday lifts alone give 1.039. Quality is no longer clipped at 1.0, but with the policy cap of 1.5 even a perfect model earns only about 71% of the quality credit, and the gap between a good and an excellent agent is a few hundredths. The cause is a clean multiplicative generator whose Poisson and day noise dominate.

Status: open — proposal: add learnable structure that a plain heuristic misses (informative censoring, post-promotion dips, within-category cannibalization, a store level shift mid-history, an intermittent slow mover, a new item with an analog), then strengthen the reference so the oracle sits about 10% above it. Until then, set quality_cap in case.yaml near what a strong but achievable model reaches (about 1.05 at this seed), so full credit does not require beating the oracle. Add a check that keeps the oracle's quality within a band across world seeds.

F3 — Display floor stated as 2 in case.yaml, 6 everywhere else (high, confirmed)

case.yaml described min_display_stock as "never forecast below 2 units", while MIN_DISPLAY = 6.0 in case.py, all five requirements and the target say 6. The exception classifier sees the case.yaml text next to the requirements, so the client was shown both numbers at the one moment the bendable rule matters, and the explainer printed both. A floor of 2 would not bind (reference 0.13948 vs. 0.13947 without a floor). The validator's number lint missed it because "2" appears in the requirements as a date ("Monday 2 November").

Status: fixed in this change — the rule description now says 6, matching MIN_DISPLAY, the requirements and the target. No {{min_display}} placeholder: the case has no rule sets to fill it from.

F4 — Bending the display floor never pays (medium, confirmed)

The floor binds on 136 reference cells (basil I03 and aged cheese I06, 1.3% of horizon demand). Dropping it gives WAPE 0.1395, quality 1.057 (1.084–1.089 on seeds 1–3). At a quality weight of 0.6 and cap 1.5 that is worth about 0.6 × 0.057 / 1.5 ≈ 0.023 of composite, below both soft-rule penalties (0.05 startup, 0.10 enterprise). WAPE against true demand gives no credit for shelf presence, so "never bend" is always the right answer.

Status: open — proposal: make bending a real trade-off. Either price shelf presence in the objective (a cost for open-day core forecasts below the floor on days the item sells, or score order quantities that include the floor), or make the floor bind on more items and calibrate it so bending gains more than the startup penalty but less than the enterprise one. Document the expected bend / no-bend answer per policy in the README.

F5 — Several data-semantics traps barely bind (medium, partly)

Measured as one change to the reference: netting returns gives quality 1.001 (1,286 return rows, −2,104 units against 2,002,817 sold), ignoring the I07 unit change 0.986, using stockout days as normal 0.960, missing the holiday 0.927, ignoring the promotion plan 0.855. Stockouts depend only on the item and promotion status, not on the day's demand, so dropping flagged days is unbiased. The verifier notes the unit change does bind for long-window models (a 52-week weekday mean falls from ratio 0.945 to 0.476 without it) and that the stockout effect, while small, is half the oracle headroom.

Status: open — proposal: make censoring informative (cap sales at a shelf or inventory level so stockouts fall on high-demand days, which makes dropping them bias low). Make returns material (e.g. occasional bulk returns from a catering account) or drop the returns target. Moving the I07 change into the recent window (e.g. 2026-10-05) would make it bind for every model; all of these change the visible data and the reference numbers.

F6 — Targets that the data already settles count fully in Ask-F1 (medium, partly)

s4_sunday_closed (S4 has no Sunday rows) and unit_change_i07 (items list "pack of 6", the history drops about 6× on 1 June 2026) are labelled missing, and Ask-F1 recall counts all nine targets equally, while the agent is told to ask only about what the data does not settle. The verifier rejects the reviewer's 4/9 figure: stockout censoring and returns are correctly ambiguous (capped sales and exclusion vs. netting cannot be read from the data), and promo_plan also covers inclusive end dates and category lift.

Status: open — proposal: relabel s4_sunday_closed and unit_change_i07 as a confirm kind and count them as covered when NOTES.md states the right inference (or give them half weight). Keep missing for facts with no trace in the data (holiday, floor, metric). This needs a framework notion of target kind in clarify.py.

F7 — No dated events or pressure (medium, confirmed)

Only hidden facts are present: no inbox, no dated notice, no stakeholder pushing for a distorted forecast. The case cannot test re-planning on a dated change or resisting pressure; the generic engagement events are the only dynamics.

Status: open — proposal: add an inbox e-mail dated 30 October that revises the promotion plan (one promotion cancelled, one extended to 15 November, one discount raised), or a refit closure of S3 on 13–14 November, both scored through the world truth. Add a pressure message (a store manager asking to pad every forecast 15% "so we never run out"), which raises WAPE and which the client confirms is not policy. Add both as targets.

F8 — Calendar is Western and partly Spanish (medium, partly)

The "Spring Holiday" falls on Good Fridays (2025-04-18, 2026-04-03) and the autumn one on 12 October; Christmas is in the zh and ja holiday lists, Lunar New Year and Chuseok are absent, and 11 November (Singles' Day in China, Pepero Day in Korea) is called a city anniversary in zh and ko. Holiday uplift lands on the day itself (S1 I07: 207 on 24 Dec vs. 359 on 25 Dec). kg produce is recorded as integers and bananas are returned by the kg. No document claims the world is country-neutral, and Christmas Day is not the record day (15 August 2026 is).

Status: open — proposal: use a fictional calendar with neutral fixed dates, put eve effects where a holiday closes stores, and move the one-off holiday off 11/11 (e.g. Thu 12 Nov, which also suits a rule-set parameter). Record kg items to 0.1 kg and log returns only for packaged items. All of this changes the visible data.

F9 — Korean phrasing (low, partly)

Numbers and parameters match across all five locales. A few Korean phrases were calques or awkward: "6개 미만" uses the piece counter for items sold by the kg, "CSV 파일 하나, forecast.csv이며" follows the English word order, "빼 주세요 … 차감하지도 않습니다" mixes a request and a statement, and the store names 북역점 / 호숫가점 are literal. The verifier notes 개 is the generic counter and 개청 기념일 is attested, so these are polish.

Status: fixed in this change — ko requirements and target: "6(판매단위 기준) 미만", "산출물은 CSV 파일 forecast.csv 하나이며, 헤더는 …", "차감하지도 마세요", "일회성 지역 공휴일인 시민의 날(시 승격 기념일)"; ko store names 북부역점 (S3) and 호수공원점 (S5). The same request/statement mix in ja ("相殺もしません") now reads "相殺もしないでください". A native reviewer should still sign off.

F10 — Documentation details that mislead reviewers (low, confirmed)

The requirements said "one row per store, item and trading day" but then describe separate negative return rows on the same key (1,286 such rows). The explainer labelled the deliverable spec "agent-visible" although only the extractor sees it, and the case had no README with its reference and baseline numbers.

Status: fixed in this change — the data note now reads "one sales row per store, item and trading day, plus separate return rows" in all five locales, and README.md gives the reference, baseline, oracle and ablation numbers. The explainer label is generated by the framework and already reads "shown to the extractor … not to the agent" at this commit.

Baseline: seasonal naive (same weekday of the last history week, S4 Sundays 0, floor of 6 on core items): mean WAPE 0.2790 vs. the reference 0.1470 at the case seed (ratio 1.90); 0.2534 / 0.2521 / 0.2533 vs. 0.1474 / 0.1592 / 0.1417 on world seeds 1–3 (ratios 1.58–1.79).
