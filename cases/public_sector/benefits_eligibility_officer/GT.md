# Ground truth: public_sector/benefits_eligibility_officer

The ground truth (GT) of a setting in five parts, with the reference as supporting evidence. Generated by `gt_report.py` from `dataset/audit.json` (`fdebench2 audit-case`, public world seed 20261026): 45 settings, easy 15, medium 15, hard 15 (each tier's public panel plus admissible parameter combinations). Damage is 1 − credit, credit = 0 for an invalid batch, else the quality score clipped to [0, 1]; a target bites in a setting when misreading it alone loses at least 0.10 of the credit (for a drawn parameter: its least harmful wrong candidate). The batch has 300 applications: 190 new ones and 110 renewals.

## 3. Objective (lower is better)

The client's own measure, in its payment units, over every household:

- **|paid − entitlement|**: the amount paid (0 when refused) against the household's true entitlement under this year's rules of the setting.
- **400 per wrong yes/no**: a household approved that does not qualify, or refused that does.
- **100 per wrong reason**: a household rightly refused with a code other than the first test it fails (residency, assets, income).
- **Renewals** are measured against their entitlement this year too. A valid batch rolls every one over at last year's amount, so what that costs (`practice_cost`: 110 renewals, 12–23 of which no longer qualify) is the same for every valid batch: the price of the practice, which the client states. A renewal written within half a unit of last year's amount (352.0, 351.99) counts as rolled over and is paid last year's amount: before the check, writing each renewal half a unit towards its entitlement stayed valid and beat the bound by 0.5 a renewal. It shifts every valid objective by the same amount and so never moves quality between them; before the audit it was the gap between the reference and the best compliant answer that capped a perfect batch at about 1.03 (REVIEW.md, F2).
- **An exception** to the renewal practice (the rule relaxed) costs 200 for every renewal household once any renewal is not rolled over: the director's exception covers the whole queue (equal treatment), and each re-assessment is half a day of a caseworker.

Quality is normalized between the baseline (0), the client's own worksheet run with the setting's statutory period, asset limit and round, and the reference (1), the answer key.

**What a slip costs.** One household wrongly refused costs 400 plus its amount: with the median award, easy 0.035–0.051, medium 0.048–0.074, hard 0.042–0.065 of quality. A misread fact costs every household it touches (part 1); a careful batch with every fact right and a couple of slips keeps most of its credit (the `two_slips` deliverable of part 5).

**The zero point is the client's own way** (`baseline_solution`, requirements.md "How the counter does it today"): last year's paper worksheet. It keeps the statutory residency period, the asset limit and the round's arrangements, so it is valid, but it has last year's figures on it (table, earnings disregard of 150 and 30%, and from medium the 980 cap table), its disregard box takes only the applicant's earnings (the others' count in full, with those of adults on the form who live elsewhere), it counts every income column (child support too, which easy leaves out), it has no supplement line, it counts residency in calendar months and it decides every application as filed. No one of these carries the scale: copying the worksheet and fixing any one of them reaches at most 0.393 (the `worksheet_*` shortcuts).
