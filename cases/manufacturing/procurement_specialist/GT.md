# Ground truth: manufacturing/procurement_specialist

The ground truth (GT) of a setting in five parts, with the reference as supporting evidence. Generated by `gt_report.py` from `dataset/audit.json` (`fdebench2 audit-case`, public world seed 20261123): 39 settings, easy 15, medium 15, hard 9 (each tier's public panel plus admissible parameter combinations). Damage is 1 − credit, credit = 0 for an invalid plan, else the quality score clipped to [0, 1]; a target bites in a setting when misreading it alone loses at least 0.10 of the credit (for a drawn parameter: its least harmful wrong candidate).

## 2. Validity checks (hard gates)

A plan that breaks any of these is invalid and earns no quality credit. The deliverable is `orders.csv` (`order_week,component_id,supplier_id,quantity`); a row with an unknown part, a supplier without a quote for it, a week outside W1-W8 or a quantity that is not a whole number makes the file unreadable (fatal). Every row is an order line; rows of the same week, part and supplier are added up.

| rule | class | tiers | why it is a hard gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `approved_vendor` | contractual | easy, medium, hard | a part from a supplier that is pending or suspended cannot go on the boards: the quality system rejects it, whatever it costs |
| `moq` | physical | easy, medium, hard | the supplier does not ship an order line below its minimum, so a plan that relies on one is short in reality |
| `budget_cap` | contractual | easy, medium, hard | the parent company's monthly cap is not negotiable: a plan that overspends a month is not approved, however cheap it is overall |
| `quality_hold` | physical | easy, medium, hard | lots from the supplier on hold that arrive while the hold lasts are rejected at incoming inspection and never reach the line |
| `allocation_cap` | physical | hard | the allocated supplier confirms no more than its monthly allocation; anything above it is simply not delivered |
| `dual_source_critical` | business_policy | easy, medium, hard | the company keeps a qualified second source for every critical part (its habit, which the client may waive for a real saving through the exception process: the policy scores a granted bend with the rule switched off and a penalty) |

Mandatory content. The plan has no separate report: what it must contain is enforced by the rules above (every critical part in use is ordered from two suppliers, so an empty or partial plan is invalid) and by the objective (a part left out is a backlog of boards). ## 3. Objective (lower is better, KRW)

- **Purchases**: every order line at its price (medium and hard: the all-units break the line reaches), Nara Display's display module C06 12% dearer from order week W4, the dollar quotes of Pacific Parts at the planning rate. Cash the client pays.
- **Holding**: 0.5% of the standard cost per piece in stock at the end of a week (about 26% a year, the usual carrying cost of capital, storage and obsolescence).
- **Backlog**: 10,000 KRW per board that waits a week for a missing part (late-delivery penalties and overtime), counted per part: a missing piece costs 10,000 KRW divided by the part's pieces per board. A missing capacitor stops a board as surely as a missing display, which the first version's 5x the part's own cost per piece did not reflect (REVIEW.md F4).
- **Safety-stock shortfall**: 1.5x the standard cost per piece of a critical part below its safety stock at the end of a week in which it is used: the client's price for running without the buffer it asked for.
- **Scrap** (hard): C04 left at the end of W5 at its standard cost (the supplier does not take it back).

**Uncertainty.** In hard each delivery is one or two weeks late with the supplier's true probabilities, which the agent only sees as a sample (the delivery history). Holding, backlog, shortfall and scrap are their exact expectation over every combination of late deliveries (enumerated, never sampled); purchases are paid either way. The reference plans on smoothed history estimates, never the true rates. Stock left after W10 is worth nothing.

## 4. Reference and bounds

- **Reference** (`reference_solution`): a time-phased MRP per part (netting stock and open orders against the exploded demand, cheapest timely approved supplier, as late as possible), with whole-line or quota second sourcing, a leftover trim and a budget step that defers, pulls or cuts lines; the cheapest of fifteen such variants by its own cost view is then improved by a local search (lines moved to another week or supplier, resized to a break or by hundreds, removed; every rule kept), which also brings C06 orders forward into W3 before Nara Display's increase. In hard it plans one week of safety lead time for suppliers whose smoothed late rate in the history is above 5%.
- **Baseline** (quality 0): the client's own MRP run, lot for lot from the cheapest timely supplier at list prices, critical parts split at the share cap, the budget fixed afterwards; in hard the buyer adds one week to every lead time.
- **Best known** (`best_known_solution`): the wide local search (also new lines, transfers between lines and longer moves) from the three cheapest variants and from the reference, by the same own cost view.
- **Bound** (`objective_bound`): a linear relaxation solved exactly with HiGHS (no MOQ, every piece at the lowest price its line could reach, the share cap, budget, allocation, hold and lead times kept; in hard the expected net position, a lower bound by Jensen's inequality). A mixed-integer version with the price tiers and MOQs (scipy's HiGHS MILP) was tried on the first panel seed: in easy/s42 it closes the gap to 3.6% after 1,000 nodes (61 s), in medium/s42 and hard/s42 200 nodes took 70-90 s and left 9% and 14%, so the LP bound is kept.
- **quality_cap** = 1.11: the median best-known quality over the measured settings is 1.106 (range 1.026–1.313); the cap is set at or above it, so the best plan known earns full quality credit and a plan as good as the reference earns 1/cap.

## 5. Diagnostic cases

`score()` reports `enforced[target]` for every target in force, for valid and invalid plans alike. For a target with a natural misreading, the plan D is scored under the client's view S and under the misread view S' and compared with the reference R: delta = [g_S(D) − g_S(R)] − [g_S'(D) − g_S'(R)], where g is the objective plus 10^10 per distinct rule broken. The target is enforced when delta ≤ 0.01% of the reference's objective: the client's reading does not make D look worse next to the reference than the misreading does. A rule broken only under the client's reading fails it; one broken only under the misreading passes it. The planning rate and the safety stock compare the same way with every other candidate of their parameter. The quality hold checks that nothing from the held supplier arrives during the hold and that the plan does not avoid a supplier only another candidate holds (where the reference uses it); the share cap checks the cap and that the plan uses the room a stricter candidate would not leave (where the reference does). The audit checks that the reference is enforced everywhere and that every ablation that bites is not.

Limits (second pass, REVIEW.md). The comparison is with the reference R under S', so a plan that merely differs from R can be called a misreading. The client's own MRP, which knows every fact, is 'not enforced' on the lead times in 28 of 39 settings and on the planning rate in 13 of 15 (often R breaks a rule under the misreading that the MRP keeps), and the tolerance (0.01% of the objective, about 0.1% of the scale) lets any other difference decide. Comparing regrets with the reference planner's own plan for the misreading, within 5% of the scale, frees the client's MRP of most of these and still separates every ablation on the four settings checked, but scoring then plans the misreadings and validate-case takes seven times as long; it waits for a framework change (diagnostics only where evaluate() is asked for them). Read the enforced column of a run's funnel for plans far from the reference with this in mind.
