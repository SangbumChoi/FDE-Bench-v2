# Ground truth: legal_compliance/contract_review_counsel

The ground truth (GT) of a setting in five parts, with the reference as supporting evidence. Generated by `gt_report.py` from `dataset/audit.json` (`fdebench2 audit-case`, public world seed 20261026): 45 settings, easy 15, medium 15, hard 15 (each tier's public panel plus admissible parameter combinations). Damage is 1 − credit, credit = 0 for an invalid review, else the quality score clipped to [0, 1]; a target bites in a setting when misreading it alone loses at least 0.10 of the credit (for a drawn parameter: its least harmful wrong candidate). The review covers 44 contracts; 29 / 29 / 44 are scored in easy / medium / hard.

A review that breaks any of these is invalid and earns no credit. A file that cannot be read (not JSON, an unknown contract id, an entry without a decision that is `sign`, `negotiate` or `reject`) is fatal.

|---|---|---|---|

| `known_issue_codes` | physical | easy, medium, hard | the codes go into the legal team's tracker, which knows the seven and nothing else; a made-up code cannot be filed or sent to a supplier (mandatory output) |

The decision is no longer a gate. Before the audit, `decision_consistency` voided a whole review for one decision that did not follow from its own issue list, and that one rule was all that made the literal solution fail. A wrong decision now makes that contract wrong (part 3), which is what it costs the client.

## 3. Objective (higher is better)

The share of the scored contracts whose entry is exactly right:

- **Right**: the issue list is exactly the playbook's list for that contract (no issue missing, none added) and the decision is the playbook's decision. The legal team acts on each contract as a whole: a list with one issue missing gets a defect signed, and one with an issue too many sends the supplier a demand the company does not make. (The first version scored 0.7 × the F1 of the codes + 0.3 × decision accuracy against an empty review, so a review with the wrong thresholds on every point still earned 0.45–0.80; see REVIEW.md, Ground truth audit.)
- **The decision is a judgement of its own**: it needs which issues are critical, how many of them make a reject, and the contract's annual value against the sign-off limit (below it, a contract whose issues are all non-critical is signed with the issues listed). It cannot be read off the issue list.
- **Scored contracts**: easy and medium leave the supplier-paper, amended and e-mailed contracts to procurement; hard scores all of them.
- **The fast track** (hard): a fast-tracked contract is right when it is marked `sign` with no issues, so keeping the client's habit is never penalised. One taken off the fast track breaks the rule and counts only with an approved exception: it is then judged against the playbook and counts twice when the playbook rejects it and the entry is exactly right, and not at all otherwise.

Quality is normalized between the baseline (0), the legal team's own keyword checklist run with the setting's playbook, and the reference (1), the answer key.

**What a slip costs.** One contract wrong (an issue missed, one too many, or a wrong decision) costs one contract's share of the scale: easy 0.071–0.083, medium 0.043–0.059, hard 0.030–0.045 of quality. A misread fact costs every contract it touches (part 1), and a careful review with the facts right and a few slips keeps most of its credit; what makes a review worth nothing is getting no more contracts right than the client's own checklist, which knows every fact.

- **Best known** (`best_known_solution`): the answer key.
- **Bound** (`objective_bound`, proven): 1.0. A review that keeps the fast track gets at most one contract's credit per scored contract, and the answer key gets all of them. A review that bends the fast track under an approved exception is outside the bound's scope and can end above it (below).

## 5. Diagnostic cases

`score()` returns `enforced` and `evidence` per target (`case.py diagnostic`). For each fact it takes the scored contracts where the client's reading and another reading (another candidate of a drawn figure, the misreading of a fixed fact, or one of the less natural readings of `_other_readings`: the minimum itself flagged, the forum checked in place of the law, data protection not critical, a reject only above the count, critical issues signed below the limit) give different entries, looks only at the aspect of the entry that tells the two apart, and counts which one the review follows. Not enforced: the review sides with some other reading more often than with the client's. Enforced: it sides with the client's more often than with every other. None: no contract tells, or the review follows neither. The three decision facts are read from the decision against the review's own issue list, so a review that misreads clauses but decides by the client's rule is still called enforced on them.
