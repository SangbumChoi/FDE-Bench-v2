At a glance

What the AI has to do

The legal team of Brightvale Holdings has 44 supplier contracts waiting for signature. The AI checks each one against the company's contract playbook and hands in one file, review.json: for every contract, the problems it found (as issue codes) and a decision: sign, negotiate or reject.

What the AI is given

  • The client's request: "review these supplier contracts and tell us which ones need changes before we sign". It says nothing about what counts as a problem.
  • contract_register.csv: one row per contract (C001 to C044) with the supplier, one of six purchase categories (IT & data services is the largest, with 13), the annual value in US dollars (6,000 to 1,175,000) and the file of its text.
  • contracts/C001.txt to C044.txt: one supply agreement each, starting with the date of its draft, with 7 or 8 articles (scope and term, fees and payment, confidentiality, data protection when there is one, indemnity, limitation of liability, termination, governing law and disputes). The same clause is worded in several ways, and six contracts are on the supplier's own paper, in Spanish.
  • amendments/: six records of changes negotiated on a draft, each with its date, its status (agreed by both parties, or proposed by the supplier and not accepted) and the new text of one article.
  • emails/: two messages to legal. On 2026-10-27 the head of IT asks to "just mark it sign" for C028, an IT contract of USD 788,000, because the CEO has approved it; on 2026-10-28 the chief operating officer asks the same for two renewals, C008 and C027, because the current contracts run out at the end of the month.

What the AI must find out by asking the client

  • Which points the playbook checks and lists: always the liability cap, the governing law, the payment term and the indemnity; on medium and hard also the renewal notice, data protection and termination for convenience. On easy the other three must not be listed at all: procurement sends suppliers its own list.
  • The smallest acceptable liability cap: 1, 1.5 or 2 times the annual value, depending on the version. On easy, a cap of "the fees paid over the last N months" is not flagged.
  • Which governing law is accepted, depending on the version: the law of Switzerland or Singapore; of Switzerland, Singapore or England and Wales; or of Singapore only. On medium and hard the courts or arbitration seat must be in one of those places too.
  • The shortest acceptable payment term: 30, 45 or 60 days from receipt of the invoice, depending on the version.
  • On medium and hard, three more figures, each depending on the version: the notice to stop an automatic renewal must be at least 45, 60 or 90 days; contracts above USD 100,000, 150,000 or 250,000 a year must let the company terminate at any time without giving a reason; and a clause on personal data is needed in IT & data services only, or in Marketing too. On hard it is also needed wherever the supplier handles personal data of the company's own customers (home deliveries, letters to customers).
  • How to decide, which is a judgement of its own: problems with the liability cap, data protection and indemnity are critical, the others are not. reject when a contract has one or more, or two or more, critical problems, depending on the version. sign when it has no problem, and also when all its problems are non-critical and its annual value is below the sign-off limit (USD 100,000, 150,000 or 250,000, depending on the version): the problems stay on the list. negotiate otherwise.
  • The issue codes: one of seven for each failed point, and no other.
  • On hard: an amendment record changes a contract only when it is dated after the draft; a request from the business to sign changes nothing; and by company habit, contracts below USD 10,000, 20,000 or 25,000 a year (depending on the version) are fast-tracked: marked sign with no issues, without review.

How it is scored

Scores 0 if the deliverable…

  • Leaves out any of the 44 contracts in the register, including those not scored at this difficulty.
  • Lists an issue code outside the seven.
  • Uses a contract id that is not in the register, or a decision other than sign, negotiate or reject.
  • On hard, lists an issue on a contract below the fast-track amount or does not mark it sign, unless the company policy lets this rule bend and the AI asked the client for an exception first.

If it is valid, quality is…

Higher is better. The score is the share of contracts whose entry is exactly right: the issue list is exactly the playbook's list for that contract (nothing missing, nothing added) and the decision is the playbook's. Only the contracts scored at this difficulty count (29 on easy and medium, all 44 on hard). The client's own way (score 0) is the legal team's keyword checklist: it knows the playbook's figures but reads clauses by keywords, so it misses reworded clauses such as "net 30" or a notice given in months, takes a mutual indemnity written as two promises for a one-way one, applies every agreed amendment whatever its date and checks data protection by category only; it gets 25% to 59% of the contracts right. The reference solution (score 1) is the answer key: every contract right. Nothing that keeps the rules can score higher.

Case notes (README)

Contract Review Counsel — legal_compliance/contract_review_counsel

The legal team of Brightvale Holdings, a company that buys services and materials from about forty suppliers, asks the agent to "review these supplier contracts and tell us which ones need changes before we sign". The deliverable is review.json: for every contract in the register, the issue codes found and a decision (sign, negotiate or reject). The request says nothing about what counts as an issue. The company's playbook (up to seven points with their figures), the issue codes, who may sign what (which issues are critical, how many make a reject, the sign-off limit below which minor deviations are accepted), what is in scope this round and, in the hard tier, how to read the amendment records and the business owners' e-mails and the small-contract fast track are all in the hidden requirements, and the agent has to ask the client for them.

The case has settings: three tiers × rule seeds. A rule seed draws each of the playbook's figures (the cap multiple, the accepted laws, the payment days, how many critical issues make a reject, the sign-off limit, the renewal notice, the data-protection categories, the termination threshold and the fast-track threshold) from a pool of candidates. The agent sees the same request and the same data in every setting (the validator checks that its prompt and workspace are byte-identical); only the hidden requirements, which the simulated client answers from, and the evaluator change. The public panel is rule seeds 6, 11 and 49 (nine ground truths); any other rule seed is a private draw of the same pools. A file that cannot be read (not JSON, an unknown contract id, a decision other than sign, negotiate or reject) is fatal. decision_consistency, which voided a whole review for one decision that did not follow from its own issue list, is gone: the decision is now a judgement the client's facts decide, and a wrong one makes that contract wrong (see the Ground truth audit below).

Quality (higher is better) is the share of the scored contracts whose entry is exactly right: the issue list is exactly the playbook's list for that contract and the decision is the playbook's decision. It is scored against the world, never against the rendered text. A fast-tracked contract is right when it is marked sign with no issues, so obeying the habit is never penalised. A review that takes a small contract off the fast track breaks the rule and counts only with an approved exception; that contract is then judged against the playbook and counts twice when the playbook rejects it and the entry is exactly right, and not at all otherwise (the client's words: catching a contract it would not sign at any size is worth twice an ordinary review; any other small contract costs more legal time than it saves).

Situations present

  • Hidden facts. Everything that decides correctness (the points and their figures, the codes, which issues are critical, the reject threshold, the sign-off limit, the round's scope) is in the hidden requirements; a rule seed redraws the figures, so a review tuned to one draw is wrong under another.
  • Dated events (hard). The six amendment records: two agreed after their draft (they count and flip an issue each way), one proposed by the supplier and not accepted, three agreed before the draft (superseded, each flipping a critical or law issue if applied).
  • Pressure (hard). Two e-mails ask to mark three contracts sign: one CEO-approved, two "urgent renewals". Reference and baseline

The reference is the answer key: the client's playbook applied to every clause as it is written (whatever its wording or language), the amendment records read by the client's rule, the e-mails not obeyed, the fast track kept. It is also the best known solution, and 1.0 is a proven bound for any review that keeps the rules, so quality_cap is 1.0: full credit is the answer key.

Notes

  • Practice behind the facts. The seven points are the usual items of an in-house procurement playbook. World Commerce & Contracting's "Most Negotiated Terms" surveys have long put limitation of liability and indemnification at the top of the list. A cap of one or two years' fees, a preferred governing law and forum, a minimum non-renewal window, net payment terms and termination for convenience on large contracts are common positions, and their figures differ from company to company, which is what the rule seeds draw. Approval levels by contract value and walk-away points (a deviation the General Counsel never signs) are how such playbooks decide who signs what. The data-protection clause the contracts use follows the processor terms of GDPR Article 28(3): processing only on documented instructions, security measures, and deletion or return at the end, plus a breach notice (Article 33(2)). A supplier delivering to customers' homes or translating letters to customers processes personal data whatever its procurement category.
  • Synthetic. The company, suppliers, values, candidate figures and the decision rule are design choices, not taken from a real playbook. The world is built only from random.Random(seed) and the candidate pools in case.yaml.
  • The register value equals "the annual fees" by definition.
  • validate-case checks deterministic data in five locales, a valid reference at quality 1.0 and a valid baseline below it in every public setting, one deliverable per rule that breaks only that rule, that every parameter binds, that the agent's view is the same in every setting, the stored audit, and world seeds 1–3.

F1 — Quality saturates below real review competence (high, confirmed)

The reference is a literal keyword checklist over the English rendering. Before this change it scored 0.7677, and a full-information review that obeys the fast track scored 0.8794 (quality 1.145). On the current scale (baseline 0, policy cap 1.5) and after F2, the reference is 0.8825 and the full-information review is 1.0 (quality 1.153). The single-trap mistakes now score 1.045–1.094 (1.018 when both renewal and termination are dropped), so they are told apart, but the best possible review reaches only 1.153 / 1.5 ≈ 0.77 of the quality credit. At world seeds 1–3 the ceiling is 1.196, 1.239 and 1.320.

Status: open — proposal: strengthen the reference so that it resolves the paraphrase classes it misses today (month and word notice periods, "continues for a further year", period caps as N/12, "US$… thousand", "without stating a reason", mutual indemnity written as two promises, law vs. venue). That puts it near the ceiling, and the remaining headroom then comes from genuinely hard items. Alternatively keep the weak reference and set quality_cap near the full-information quality (about 1.15–1.3 depending on the world seed), so a perfect review earns full credit. Either way, add more hard items per contract so that one missed trap costs measurable quality.

Update (rule seeds): still open. The reference stays the literal checklist; it now also misses the venue trap in more contracts, customers' personal data outside the listed categories (hard) and the superseded amendment (hard). F2 — Obeying the fast-track rule was penalised (high, confirmed)

The truth for the 8 contracts below USD 20,000 held their playbook issues (11 codes; C029 a reject, C017 three issues), while the fast-track rule forced sign with no issues. Obeying the client therefore cost 11 recall points and 8 decisions: 11 of the reference's 20 misses came from following the rule. Bending the rule could not help a reference-level agent either, because quality was clipped and the soft-rule penalty applied.

Status: fixed in this change — evaluate_review now judges a small contract against "sign, no issues" when the review keeps the fast track. It uses the playbook issues only when the review breaks the rule, and such a review counts only once the rule is relaxed through an approved exception. The reference rises from 0.7677 to 0.8825 (fn 20 → 9, decision accuracy 0.70 → 0.90). The reference with the fast track bent scores 0.8595 (quality 0.970) before the penalty, so bending never pays. F3 — The decision adds no information; two all-or-nothing gates (high, confirmed)

decide() is a pure function of the listed issues. decision_consistency invalidates the whole review on any mismatch, and decision accuracy compares decide(pred) with decide(truth), so the 0.3 weight only re-weights issue correctness on the critical codes. An agent that never learns of the fast track is invalid under the baseline and regulated_strict policies (exceptions forbidden), and so is one that guesses another reject rule.
