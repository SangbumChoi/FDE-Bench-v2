Hi, procurement has lined up about forty supplier contracts that are waiting for signature. Please review these supplier contracts and tell us which ones need changes before we sign. The contract register and the contract texts are attached.

Case notes (README)

Contract Review Counsel — legal_compliance/contract_review_counsel

The legal team of Brightvale Holdings, a company that buys services and materials from about forty suppliers, asks the agent to "review these supplier contracts and tell us which ones need changes before we sign". The deliverable is review.json: for every contract in the register, the issue codes found and a decision (sign, negotiate or reject). The request says nothing about what counts as an issue. The company's playbook (seven points with their thresholds), the issue codes, the decision rule and the small-contract fast track are all in the hidden requirements, and the agent has to ask the client for them.

The case has no settings: one world, one ground truth, the same problem at every difficulty. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

Data

  • contract_register.csv (localized name, e.g. 계약대장.csv): contract_id, supplier, category (six procurement categories; IT & data services is the largest with 12 of 40), annual_value_usd and contract_file. The requirements say the register value is "the annual fees" that the contracts refer to.
  • contracts/C001.txt … C040.txt: one supply agreement per file, 7 or 8 articles (scope and term, fees and payment, confidentiality, data protection when present, indemnity, limitation of liability, termination, governing law and disputes). Contracts are written in the client's language. Six of them (C012, C021, C024, C028, C032, C036) are on the supplier's paper in another language: Spanish for an English client, English for the other four locales.
  • Values at seed 20261026: 8 contracts below USD 20,000 (7,500 to 19,000), 14 between 26,000 and 131,500 and 18 between 268,000 and 1,176,500.

Rules

RuleClassWhat it says
all_contracts_reviewedphysicalEvery contract in the register has an entry.
decision_consistencycontractualThe decision follows from the listed issues: reject with two or more critical issues (LIABILITY_CAP, DATA_PROTECTION, INDEMNITY), negotiate with any issue, else sign.
small_contract_fast_trackbusiness policy (bendable)Contracts below USD 20,000 are marked sign with no issues, without review.

Quality (higher is better) is 0.7 × the micro F1 of the issue codes over every contract × code pair plus 0.3 × the share of contracts whose decision matches the playbook decision for their true issues. It is scored against the world, never against the rendered text.

The playbook and the traps in each clause

CodeRuleHow the contracts phrase it, and the trap
LIABILITY_CAPcap ≥ 1 × annual valueA multiple in digits or words ("one half of", "twice"); a fixed amount in digits or paraphrased ("US$20 thousand in aggregate", 万/만 in zh/ko/ja, "mil dólares" in es); or "the fees paid in the six months preceding the claim", which is worth N/12 × the value. A cap exactly equal to the value passes (8 contracts above the fast track sit exactly at 1× at this seed), and so does an uncapped contract.
GOVERNING_LAWSwiss or Singapore law"Swiss law" or "the laws of Switzerland". In 14 of 40 contracts at this seed the courts or arbitration seat are in another jurisdiction than the law; only the law counts, so Hong Kong law with a Singapore seat is an issue.
AUTO_RENEWALnon-renewal notice ≥ 60 days"Renews automatically … unless notice" or "continues for a further year … unless a party objects"; notice in days or in months (a month is 30 days: "two months" passes, "forty-five days" fails). A contract that simply expires passes.
PAYMENT_TERMS≥ 45 days from receipt of invoiceDigits, words ("forty-five days") or "net 30".
DATA_PROTECTIONIT & data contracts need a personal-data clauseTitled "Data Protection" or "Personal Information" with different wording; only the register category decides whether one is needed.
TERMINATION_CONVENIENCEabove USD 150,000, we can terminate without cause"For convenience" or "bring this Agreement to an end at any time and without stating a reason"; termination for breach does not count. Strictly above 150,000.
INDEMNITYmutual"Each party shall indemnify the other", or the same thing as two promises ("the Supplier shall indemnify the Customer … and the Customer shall indemnify the Supplier on the same terms"); one-way means only we indemnify the supplier.

Truth at seed 20261026: 69 issue codes on 33 of the 40 contracts, 8 of them reject. Above the fast track (32 contracts) there are 58 codes; the decisions there are 7 reject, 18 negotiate and 7 sign.

Situations present

  • Hidden facts. Everything that decides correctness (the seven points and their thresholds, the codes, the decision rule, the fast track) is in the hidden requirements. Nothing in the data states them.
  • A company habit (the fast track). The client signs contracts below USD 20,000 without legal review. The rule is a bendable business policy. While it holds, the right answer for a small contract is sign with no issues, and a review that obeys it gets those contracts right. If the agent breaks it, the review counts only with an approved exception. The small contracts are then judged against the playbook like the others, and the policy's soft-rule penalty applies. At this seed all 8 small contracts carry playbook issues (11 codes; C029 would be a reject), so a reviewer who never learns of the fast track and reviews them is invalid without an exception. Bending does not pay in score: the reference with the fast track set aside scores 0.8595 against 0.8825 when it obeys, before the penalty.
  • Language. Six contracts are written in a second language, and every clause has two or three phrasings.
  • Not present: dated events (amendments, side letters, an already-renewed contract), inbox messages, conflicting sources or pressure to sign a bad contract. See REVIEW.md F5.

Reference and baseline

The reference is a literal keyword checklist that reads the English rendering of each contract (whatever the locale) and obeys the fast track. It handles digits, number words for payment days and "one half of", and misses the paraphrases. Baseline: signs every contract as it stands, with no issues and decision sign (what the client does without a reviewer). Both are valid. Normalized quality is (objective − baseline) / (reference − baseline).

Notes

  • Practice behind the facts. The seven points are the usual items of an in-house procurement playbook. World Commerce & Contracting's "Most Negotiated Terms" surveys have long put limitation of liability and indemnification at the top of the list. A cap of one year's fees, a preferred governing law, a minimum non-renewal window, net payment terms and termination for convenience on large contracts are common positions. The data-protection clause the contracts use follows the processor terms of GDPR Article 28(3): processing only on documented instructions, security measures, and deletion or return at the end, plus a breach notice (Article 33(2)).
  • Synthetic. The company, suppliers, values, thresholds (60 days, 45 days, USD 150,000, USD 20,000) and the decision rule are design choices, not taken from a real playbook. The world is built only from random.Random(seed).
  • The register value equals "the annual fees" by definition. The contract scope always matches the register category, so the data-protection point is a category lookup plus a clause check.
  • validate-case checks deterministic data in five locales, a valid reference at quality 1.0, a valid baseline below it, one deliverable per rule that breaks only that rule, and world seeds 1–3.

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review — legal_compliance/contract_review_counsel

Findings of the case review (F1–F9) and what this change did about each. Numbers are at world seed 20261026 unless stated. The review was written before the F2 fix (reference 0.7677) and against a 1.0 quality cap; the current figures are given where they differ. Comments welcome on the open proposals.

F1 — Quality saturates below real review competence (high, confirmed)

The reference is a literal keyword checklist over the English rendering. Before this change it scored 0.7677, and a full-information review that obeys the fast track scored 0.8794 (quality 1.145). Under the old 1.0 cap, every single-trap mistake (no TERMINATION_CONVENIENCE, no renewal threshold, venue read as the law, equal caps flagged, foreign paper left empty) still earned full credit. On the current scale (baseline 0, policy cap 1.5) and after F2, the reference is 0.8825 and the full-information review is 1.0 (quality 1.153). The single-trap mistakes now score 1.045–1.094 (1.018 when both renewal and termination are dropped), so they are told apart, but the best possible review reaches only 1.153 / 1.5 ≈ 0.77 of the quality credit. At world seeds 1–3 the ceiling is 1.196, 1.239 and 1.320.

Status: open — proposal: strengthen the reference so that it resolves the paraphrase classes it misses today (month and word notice periods, "continues for a further year", period caps as N/12, "US$… thousand", "without stating a reason", mutual indemnity written as two promises, law vs. venue). That puts it near the ceiling, and the remaining headroom then comes from genuinely hard items. Alternatively keep the weak reference and set quality_cap near the full-information quality (about 1.15–1.3 depending on the world seed), so a perfect review earns full credit. Either way, add more hard items per contract so that one missed trap costs measurable quality.

F2 — Obeying the fast-track rule was penalised (high, confirmed)

The truth for the 8 contracts below USD 20,000 held their playbook issues (11 codes; C029 a reject, C017 three issues), while the fast-track rule forced sign with no issues. Obeying the client therefore cost 11 recall points and 8 decisions: 11 of the reference's 20 misses came from following the rule. Bending the rule could not help a reference-level agent either, because quality was clipped and the soft-rule penalty applied.

Status: fixed in this change — evaluate_review now judges a small contract against "sign, no issues" when the review keeps the fast track. It uses the playbook issues only when the review breaks the rule, and such a review counts only once the rule is relaxed through an approved exception. The reference rises from 0.7677 to 0.8825 (fn 20 → 9, decision accuracy 0.70 → 0.90). The reference with the fast track bent scores 0.8595 (quality 0.970) before the penalty, so bending never pays. The quality sentence in requirements.md says this in all five locales. Still open: a scored reason to bend (e.g. a small IT contract that processes personal data with no data-protection clause, where an approved exception plus flagging it beats obeying). Also open: the catalog row in cases/README.md ("Accept vendor paper under a value threshold"), which lies outside this case's directory.

F3 — The decision adds no information; two all-or-nothing gates (high, confirmed)

decide() is a pure function of the listed issues. decision_consistency invalidates the whole review on any mismatch, and decision accuracy compares decide(pred) with decide(truth), so the 0.3 weight only re-weights issue correctness on the critical codes. An agent that never learns of the fast track is invalid under the baseline and regulated_strict policies (exceptions forbidden), and so is one that guesses another reject rule.

Status: open — proposal: give the decision a fact of its own that the client holds. Examples: approval levels by value, supplier criticality, or which issues are walk-away points and which are negotiable. Score it separately. Consider grading decision_consistency per contract instead of zeroing the review. If asking about the fast track is meant as the key test, say so in the README.

F4 — No settings, and the world hides the threshold gaps (high, confirmed)

There is one ground truth (setting.json is {}). Values come from three disjoint bands (6,000–19,500, 25,000–150,000, 160,000–1,200,000), and the observed values jump from 19,000 to 26,000 and from 131,500 to 268,000. Any fast-track cut in (19k, 26k] or termination cut in [131.5k, 268k) therefore gives the same truth. Notice and payment days come only from {30, 45, 60, 90}, and the gaps hint at where the thresholds lie.

Status: open — proposal: three tiers. Easy covers the four points that read straight off the clause (caps, law, payment, indemnity), with renewal, data protection and termination "checked by procurement this round". Medium adds month conversion, period caps, data protection by category, termination by value and the venue trap. Hard adds the foreign paper, the fast track and the F5 situations. Three rule sets over law, notice days, payment days, termination threshold, cap multiple, fast-track threshold and which codes are critical, for example A = CH/SG, 60 d, 45 d, >150k, ≥1×, <20k; B = + England & Wales, 90 d, 60 d, >500k, ≥1.5×, <10k; C = SG only, 45 d, 30 d, >100k, ≥2× for IT, <25k. Redraw values, notice days, payment days and cap multiples so they straddle every candidate threshold, and check that each rule set's reference is invalid or at least 1% worse under the others.

F5 — Apart from the playbook, no situations (medium, confirmed)

The data is clean and self-consistent. write_data writes only the contracts and the register: no inbox, amendments, order forms or dates. The scope sentence is fixed per category, so the data-protection point is a register lookup. The register value is "the annual fees" by definition. The only pressure is the fast-track habit, and it pushes toward less work rather than toward a tempting violation.

Status: open — proposal: add scored situations. Hidden facts: a marketing or professional-services contract whose scope says it processes our customer list, so it needs a data-protection clause once the client confirms; a register value that is stale against an attached order form. Dated events: an amendment or side letter dated after the contract that changes the law or the renewal notice; a contract that already auto-renewed. Pressure: a sponsor's e-mail saying "C0xx is CEO-approved, just mark it sign" on a reject-worthy contract, scored on the true decision.

F6 — Contracts are short identical templates (medium, confirmed)

Every contract has the same 7 or 8 articles in the same order. Confidentiality and breach text never change, there are two or three phrasings per clause, and "supplier's paper" is our template rendered in another language. 33 of 40 contracts need changes and 8 are reject; 13 have a one-way indemnity, 13 caps below 1× and 13 a law other than Swiss or Singapore law. That density is implausible for contracts said to be mostly on our own template.

Status: open — proposal: frame the deviations as supplier redlines of our template. Give real supplier paper its own structure: definitions, an order-of-precedence clause, schedules, cross-references ("subject to clause 9.3") and carve-outs that defeat the cap. Add 4–6 phrasings per clause, including negations and exceptions, and bring the share of contracts with issues down toward 40–50%.

F7 — Mixed-language difficulty and a few unnatural strings (medium, confirmed)

Foreign paper is English in zh, ko, ja and es and Spanish only in en, so four locales get the easiest second language. Paraphrased fixed caps divided by 10,000 even below 10,000: C023 (cap USD 5,000) read "미화 0.5만 달러", "0.5万米ドル" and "0.5万美元". The Korean digits payment variant says "세금계산서" (a Korean VAT invoice) while the other variants and the playbook say 청구서. ko requirements.md and targets.yaml have the doubled "검토 없이 이슈 없이". Numbers and thresholds agree across the five locales.

Status: fixed in this change (amounts only) — paraphrased caps below 10,000 now read as natives write them: "五千美元", "미화 5천 달러", "5,000米ドル" (C023 here). From 10,000 up, ko and ja split an amount that is not a whole thousand ("99만 5,500달러", "99万5,500米ドル", seen at world seed 5); round amounts keep the 万/만 form ("2.5만 달러"). Only the zh/ko/ja contract texts change; the truth and the reference do not. Still open — proposal: use a third language that is never English or the client's (German or French) for foreign paper in every locale. Use 청구서 in all Korean payment wording. Rephrase the Korean fast-track line as "법무 검토를 거치지 않고 이슈 없음(빈 목록), sign으로 처리합니다". Get a native-speaker pass over all five locales.

F8 — One clarification target bundled three facts (medium, partly)

decision_and_scoring combined the issue codes, the decision rule and the "0.7 × issue F1 + 0.3 × decision accuracy" weights. The client may not state the weights, so that part could never be covered. The verifier corrected two side claims. First, deliverable_spec reaches only the extractor, never the agent. Second, the extractor runs only when no review.json exists, so invented codes in an agent's own review.json stay fatal.

Status: fixed in this change — the target is split into issue_codes and decision_rule in all five locales (same order), and the scoring clause is dropped. The explainer already labels the deliverable spec as shown to the extractor, not to the agent. Still open — proposal: add separate targets for the real edge rules ("a cap equal to the value is fine", "an uncapped contract is fine", "the venue does not matter"), which today sit inside the liability_cap and governing_law targets.

F9 — No README; reviewer surprises (low, confirmed)

The case had no README with realism notes, baselines or the ceiling. The docstring said keyword matching misses things while the keyword matcher defined quality 1.0. The reference lost 11 misses to the client's own rule, and the catalog row did not match the shipped bendable rule.

Status: fixed in this change — README.md covers the client and data, the rules, the traps per clause, the situations present, the role of the fast track, the reference's errors, the baseline and headroom numbers, and the practice behind the playbook. The docstring now says the reference is the literal checklist. The 11 misses are gone with F2. The catalog row in cases/README.md is outside this case's directory and stays open (see F2).

Baseline: signs every contract as it stands (no issues, decision sign), objective 0.1125 against the reference's 0.8825 at world seed 20261026. At world seeds 1–3 it scores 0.0750 / 0.1200 / 0.0900 against 0.8485 / 0.8301 / 0.7793. It is valid everywhere: it obeys the fast track and gets right only the contracts whose right answer is sign (15 of 40 at the stored seed).
