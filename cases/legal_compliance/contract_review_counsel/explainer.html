What the agent is asked

The client's request, the only text the agent gets up front. It is the same in every setting.

Hi, procurement has lined up about forty supplier contracts that are waiting for signature. Please review these supplier contracts and tell us which ones need changes before we sign. The contract register and the contract texts are attached.

Deliverable spec (shown to the extractor that reshapes the agent's files, not to the agent)

review.json = {"C001": {"issues": ["LIABILITY_CAP", ...], "decision": "sign" | "negotiate" | "reject"}, ...}
One entry per contract in the register, keyed by contract id exactly as in the data.
Issue codes (ASCII, exactly these): LIABILITY_CAP, GOVERNING_LAW, AUTO_RENEWAL, PAYMENT_TERMS,
DATA_PROTECTION, TERMINATION_CONVENIENCE, INDEMNITY. An empty list means no issue found.

Settings: one dataset, 9 ground truths

The agent sees the same request and data in every setting. The tier (= difficulty) decides which rules apply; the rule seed draws each rule parameter, independently of the others, from its candidates below. Each difficulty is reported as the mean over rule seeds: the public ones below, or private seeds that draw from the same candidates.

Tiers

TierWhat is in force
easyFour playbook points that read straight off the clause: the liability cap (multiples and fixed amounts; caps written as the fees of N months are not flagged this round), the governing law (the law only, not the forum), the payment term and a mutual indemnity. Renewal, data protection and termination are left to procurement, and the contracts on supplier paper, with an amendment record or named in the e-mail are not scored. No fast track: every other contract is reviewed, small ones included.
mediumAll seven points: easy plus the renewal notice (months converted at 30 days), caps written as the fees of N months (N/12 of the annual value), data protection by category, termination for convenience above a value and the dispute forum (courts or arbitration seat in an allowed jurisdiction). Supplier paper, amended contracts and the e-mailed contract are still not scored; no fast track.
hardMedium plus the uncertain and adversarial parts: the six contracts on the supplier's own paper, the amendment records (only a change agreed by both parties after the draft counts), personal data of our own customers in contracts outside the listed categories, a business owner's e-mail asking to sign a contract the playbook rejects, and the small-contract fast track (a bendable policy).

Case notes (README)

Contract Review Counsel — legal_compliance/contract_review_counsel

The legal team of Brightvale Holdings, a company that buys services and materials from about forty suppliers, asks the agent to "review these supplier contracts and tell us which ones need changes before we sign". The deliverable is review.json: for every contract in the register, the issue codes found and a decision (sign, negotiate or reject). The request says nothing about what counts as an issue. The company's playbook (up to seven points with their figures), the issue codes, the decision rule, what is in scope this round and, in the hard tier, how to read the amendment records and the business owner's e-mail and the small-contract fast track are all in the hidden requirements, and the agent has to ask the client for them.

The case has settings: three tiers × rule seeds. A rule seed draws each of the playbook's figures (the cap multiple, the accepted laws, the payment days, how many critical issues make a reject, the renewal notice, the data-protection categories, the termination threshold and the fast-track threshold) from a pool of candidates. The agent sees the same request and the same data in every setting (the validator checks that its prompt and workspace are byte-identical); only the hidden requirements, which the simulated client answers from, and the evaluator change. The public panel is rule seeds 6, 11 and 49 (nine ground truths); any other rule seed is a private draw of the same pools. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

Data (the same in every setting)

  • contract_register.csv (localized name, e.g. 계약대장.csv): contract_id, supplier, category (six procurement categories; IT & data services is the largest with 12 of 40), annual_value_usd and contract_file. The requirements say the register value is "the annual fees" that the contracts refer to.
  • contracts/C001.txt … C040.txt: one supply agreement per file, headed by the date of its draft (2026-09-01 to 2026-10-19 at the stored seed), with 7 or 8 articles (scope and term, fees and payment, confidentiality, data protection when present, indemnity, limitation of liability, termination, governing law and disputes). Contracts are written in the client's language. Six of them (C003, C014, C023, C026, C029, C032 at the stored seed) are on the supplier's paper in another language: Spanish for an English client, English for the other four locales.
  • amendments/C0xx.txt (localized folder, e.g. 수정기록/): four records of changes negotiated on a draft, each with its date, its status and the replacement text of one article. At the stored seed: C019 (agreed after the draft: Singapore law and seat become New York law and courts), C015 (agreed after the draft: payment within 15 days becomes 60 days), C013 (proposed by the supplier, not accepted: a one-way indemnity) and C036 (agreed, but dated six days before the draft, which still caps liability at half the fees: superseded).
  • emails/2026-10-27.txt (localized folder): the business owner of C040 (an IT contract of USD 1,040,000) writes that it is CEO-approved and asks legal to "just mark it sign". C040 caps liability at half the fees and has a one-way indemnity, a reject under every candidate playbook.
  • Values at the stored seed (USD): 3 below 10,000; exactly 10,000; 3 between 17,000 and 19,000; exactly 20,000; 2 at 21,000; exactly 25,000; 7 between 50,000 and 89,000; exactly 100,000; 4 between 122,000 and 145,000; exactly 150,000; 4 between 157,000 and 214,000; exactly 250,000; 11 between 383,000 and 1,175,000. The generator reads the candidate thresholds from case.yaml and always puts contracts strictly between consecutive candidates and exactly on each one, so every fast-track and termination candidate changes answers (the thresholds are strict: a contract exactly at a termination candidate never has a termination right).
  • Three logistics or professional-services contracts say in their scope that the supplier handles personal data of the client's own customers (home deliveries to customers, translating letters to customers): C006, C023 and C026 at the stored seed; C023 and C026 have no data-protection clause.

Rules

RuleClassTiersWhat it says
all_contracts_reviewedphysicalallEvery contract in the register has an entry.
decision_consistencycontractualallThe decision follows from the listed issues: reject with reject_at or more critical issues, negotiate with any issue, else sign. In easy only LIABILITY_CAP and INDEMNITY are critical, and codes outside the round are ignored: the decision may follow the listed issues with them or without them.

Quality (higher is better) is 0.7 × the micro F1 of the issue codes over every scored contract × in-scope code pair plus 0.3 × the share of scored contracts whose decision matches the playbook decision for their true issues. It is scored against the world, never against the rendered text. The fast-track scoring fix of F2 stands: while a review keeps the fast track, a small contract is right when marked sign with no issues; a review that breaks it counts only with an approved exception, and the small contracts are then judged against the playbook like the others.

The playbook and the traps in each clause

CodeRule (parameter)How the contracts phrase it, and the trap
LIABILITY_CAPcap ≥ cap_multiple × annual valueA multiple in digits or words ("one half of", "twice"); a fixed amount in digits or paraphrased ("US$37.5 thousand in aggregate", 万/만 in zh/ko/ja, "mil dólares" in es); or "the fees paid in the six months preceding the claim", worth N/12 × the value (6, 12, 18 or 24 months). Fixed caps sit exactly on 1×, 1.5× and 2× of the value and strictly between them, so a cap at the minimum passes and one just below fails. An uncapped contract passes. Easy does not flag the N-month caps.
GOVERNING_LAWlaw in governing_law; from medium also the forum"Swiss law" or "the laws of Switzerland". In 13 of 40 contracts at the stored seed the courts or seat are elsewhere than the law. In easy only the law counts (Hong Kong law with a Singapore seat is an issue); from medium a Singapore-law contract with New York courts is an issue too.
AUTO_RENEWAL (medium, hard)non-renewal notice ≥ renewal_notice_days"Renews automatically … unless notice" or "continues for a further year … unless a party objects"; notice in days or months (a month is 30 days). A contract that simply expires passes.
PAYMENT_TERMS≥ payment_days from receipt of invoiceDigits, words ("forty-five days") or "net 30"; the Korean and Japanese word forms are months ("두 달", "2か月").

Situations present

  • Hidden facts. Everything that decides correctness (the points, their figures, the codes, the decision rule, the round's scope) is in the hidden requirements; a rule seed redraws the figures, so a review tuned to one draw is wrong under another.
  • Dated events (hard). The four amendment records: two agreed after their draft (they count and flip an issue each way), one proposed by the supplier and not accepted, one agreed but dated before the draft (superseded).
  • Pressure (hard). The business owner's e-mail asks to mark a reject-worthy contract sign; the true decision is scored. In easy and medium that contract is out of scope.
  • The hardest hidden fact (hard). Personal data of the client's own customers in a logistics or professional-services contract needs the clause although the category does not.
  • A company habit (hard). The fast track is a bendable business policy. Bending it never pays: a perfect review scores the same before the 0.1 soft-rule penalty, and the reference bent scores 0.8427 against 0.8343 at hard/s6, less than the penalty.
  • Language. Six contracts are written in a second language (scored in hard only), and every clause has two or three phrasings.

Reference and baseline

The reference is a literal keyword checklist that reads the English rendering of each contract (whatever the locale), applies the setting's figures and obeys the fast track. It handles digits, number words for payment days and "one half of"/"100% of", checks the dispute sentence for an accepted city from medium on, applies every amendment record marked agreed (whatever its date) and decides data protection by category only. It misses the paraphrases. Baseline: signs every contract as it stands, with no issues and decision sign (what the client does without a reviewer). Both are valid in every setting. Normalized quality is (objective − baseline) / (reference − baseline).

At hard/s6 (stored seed) the reference scores issue F1 0.828 (tp 72, fp 8, fn 22) and decision accuracy 0.85:

The baseline gets no codes (F1 0); its decision accuracy is the share of scored contracts whose right answer is sign (0 to 10 of 40 at the stored seed: under the strict draws nearly every contract needs changes).

Notes

  • Practice behind the facts. The seven points are the usual items of an in-house procurement playbook. World Commerce & Contracting's "Most Negotiated Terms" surveys have long put limitation of liability and indemnification at the top of the list. A cap of one or two years' fees, a preferred governing law and forum, a minimum non-renewal window, net payment terms and termination for convenience on large contracts are common positions, and their figures differ from company to company, which is what the rule seeds draw. The data-protection clause the contracts use follows the processor terms of GDPR Article 28(3): processing only on documented instructions, security measures, and deletion or return at the end, plus a breach notice (Article 33(2)). A supplier delivering to customers' homes or translating letters to customers processes personal data whatever its procurement category.
  • Synthetic. The company, suppliers, values, candidate figures and the decision rule are design choices, not taken from a real playbook. The world is built only from random.Random(seed) and the candidate pools in case.yaml.
  • The register value equals "the annual fees" by definition.
  • validate-case checks deterministic data in five locales, a valid reference at quality 1.0 and a valid baseline below it in every public setting, one deliverable per rule that breaks only that rule, that every parameter binds, that the agent's view is the same in every setting, and world seeds 1–3.

Findings of the case review (F1–F9) and what this change did about each. Numbers are at world seed 20261026 unless stated. The rule-seed conversion (last section) updates several statuses; each update follows the status it amends, and the older figures are kept as they were measured. F1 — Quality saturates below real review competence (high, confirmed)

The reference is a literal keyword checklist over the English rendering. Before this change it scored 0.7677, and a full-information review that obeys the fast track scored 0.8794 (quality 1.145). Under the old 1.0 cap, every single-trap mistake (no TERMINATION_CONVENIENCE, no renewal threshold, venue read as the law, equal caps flagged, foreign paper left empty) still earned full credit. On the current scale (baseline 0, policy cap 1.5) and after F2, the reference is 0.8825 and the full-information review is 1.0 (quality 1.153). The single-trap mistakes now score 1.045–1.094 (1.018 when both renewal and termination are dropped), so they are told apart, but the best possible review reaches only 1.153 / 1.5 ≈ 0.77 of the quality credit. At world seeds 1–3 the ceiling is 1.196, 1.239 and 1.320.

Status: open — proposal: strengthen the reference so that it resolves the paraphrase classes it misses today (month and word notice periods, "continues for a further year", period caps as N/12, "US$… thousand", "without stating a reason", mutual indemnity written as two promises, law vs. venue). That puts it near the ceiling, and the remaining headroom then comes from genuinely hard items. Alternatively keep the weak reference and set quality_cap near the full-information quality (about 1.15–1.3 depending on the world seed), so a perfect review earns full credit. Either way, add more hard items per contract so that one missed trap costs measurable quality.

Update (rule seeds): still open. The reference stays the literal checklist; it now also misses the venue trap in more contracts, customers' personal data outside the listed categories (hard) and the superseded amendment (hard). On the public panel the full-information review reaches quality 1.12–1.24 (1.121 / 1.195 / 1.161 easy, 1.169 / 1.179 / 1.154 medium, 1.218 / 1.235 / 1.172 hard at the stored seed), so the best review earns about 0.75–0.82 of the quality credit under the 1.5 cap.

F2 — Obeying the fast-track rule was penalised (high, confirmed)

The truth for the 8 contracts below USD 20,000 held their playbook issues (11 codes; C029 a reject, C017 three issues), while the fast-track rule forced sign with no issues. Obeying the client therefore cost 11 recall points and 8 decisions: 11 of the reference's 20 misses came from following the rule. Bending the rule could not help a reference-level agent either, because quality was clipped and the soft-rule penalty applied.

Status: fixed in this change — evaluate_review now judges a small contract against "sign, no issues" when the review keeps the fast track. It uses the playbook issues only when the review breaks the rule, and such a review counts only once the rule is relaxed through an approved exception. The reference rises from 0.7677 to 0.8825 (fn 20 → 9, decision accuracy 0.70 → 0.90). The reference with the fast track bent scores 0.8595 (quality 0.970) before the penalty, so bending never pays. The quality sentence in requirements.md says this in all five locales. Still open: a scored reason to bend (e.g. a small IT contract that processes personal data with no data-protection clause, where an approved exception plus flagging it beats obeying). Also open: the catalog row in cases/README.md ("Accept vendor paper under a value threshold"), which lies outside this case's directory.

Update (rule seeds): the fix is kept unchanged; the fast track now applies in the hard tier only, with its threshold drawn from 10,000 / 20,000 / 25,000, and the requirements say "every other contract is reviewed this round, the small ones included" in easy and medium. Bending still never pays: at hard/s6 a perfect review scores the same before the 0.1 soft-rule penalty, and the reference bent scores 0.8427 against 0.8343.

F3 — The decision adds no information; two all-or-nothing gates (high, confirmed)

decide() is a pure function of the listed issues. decision_consistency invalidates the whole review on any mismatch, and decision accuracy compares decide(pred) with decide(truth), so the 0.3 weight only re-weights issue correctness on the critical codes. An agent that never learns of the fast track is invalid under the baseline and regulated_strict policies (exceptions forbidden), and so is one that guesses another reject rule.

Status: open — proposal: give the decision a fact of its own that the client holds. Examples: approval levels by value, supplier criticality, or which issues are walk-away points and which are negotiable. Score it separately. Consider grading decision_consistency per contract instead of zeroing the review. If asking about the fast track is meant as the key test, say so in the README.

Update (rule seeds): partly. The decision rule now holds a drawn fact the client must give: reject_at (a reject with two or more, or one or more, critical issues), and which codes are critical depends on the tier (in easy only LIABILITY_CAP and INDEMNITY). The decision is still a function of the listed issues, and decision_consistency still voids the whole review; grading it per contract stays open.

F4 — No settings, and the world hides the threshold gaps (high, confirmed)

Notice and payment days come only from {30, 45, 60, 90}, and the gaps hint at where the thresholds lie.
