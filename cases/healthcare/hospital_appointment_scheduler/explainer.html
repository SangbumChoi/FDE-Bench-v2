English

From Monday 9 November our outpatient booking office wants to work through a program: every working day it gets that day's new appointment requests, cancellations and notices together with our current bookings, books patients into the doctors' free slots and tells us where each department stands. Above all we want to stop losing doctor time to empty slots. Could you build it? Attached are an export of our booking system (doctors, clinic calendar, four weeks of requests and appointments with what happened to them, cancellations, attendance and the waitlist), the e-mails our booking office received (absences, notes, the outpatient office's instructions) and one pilot day (what the program receives and what our pilot answered).

Deliverable spec (the program interface)

A program/ directory with scheduler.py defining on_day(day). The engine loads it once and calls it once per
working day of the hidden period, in order, in the same process (module-level state survives between calls);
10 s per call, Python standard library only, no network and no access to the data files at run time.
day = {"date", "day_index", "days_in_period", "period_end", "booking_window": {"first", "last"}, "doctors",
"sessions", "new_requests", "notices", "attendance", "book", "waitlist"} exactly as in the pilot-day input file.
Return {"actions": [{"type": "book" | "move", "request_id", "doctor_id", "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
"start": "HH:MM", "test_date": "YYYY-MM-DD" or null} | {"type": "release", "request_id"}],
"status_table": {department_code: {"free_slots": int, "held_back": int, "waitlist": int}}} as in the pilot-day
output file. Actions are applied in order; a request not booked stays on the waitlist.

Settings: one dataset, 9 ground truths

The agent sees the same request and data in every setting. The tier (= difficulty) decides which rules apply; the rule set gives their parameters. Each difficulty is reported as the mean over rule sets A, B and C.

Tiers

TierWhat is in force
easyThe daily booking core: new patient = two consecutive slots, follow-up = one; department; never before the earliest date; the urgent window; the urgent reserve (the first two morning slots of a session are held for urgent requests until the day before); one clinic closed on the City Day holiday although the calendar lists it; in-person interpreters per day for new patients; the notice period for moving an appointment; the waitlist priority order; follow-ups with their previous doctor (bendable); the daily status table. Absence and interpreter notices, referral dates, blood tests, no-shows and duplicate requests are out of scope.
mediumEasy plus referral letters that expire, a fasting blood test at least one working day before the visit within a daily lab cap, doctor absences (the absent doctor's appointments must be moved, within the notice period) and an interpreter's absence (one fewer interpreter that day).
hardMedium plus no-shows (each patient's hidden rate rising with lead time; the value counts expected patients seen), overbooking of follow-ups (two to a slot, a cap per doctor session, expected collision cost), duplicate requests (one appointment per patient and department; one need on the waitlist) and extra trips of patients booked in several departments.

  • contractual bends only if the policy allows contractual exceptions
  • business_policy may bend through the policy's exception process

Case notes (README.md)

Hospital Appointment Scheduler — healthcare/hospital_appointment_scheduler

A mid-size outpatient hospital wants its booking office to run day by day through a program. Every working day the program gets the day's new appointment requests, the notices of the day (patient cancellations, a doctor off sick or at a conference, an interpreter away, staff notes), today's attendance and the current booking book and waitlist (the effect of its own earlier decisions). It books requests into the doctors' free 20-minute slots of the next ten working days, re-books appointments a notice affects, fills freed slots from the waitlist and reports a daily status table per department. The agent delivers program/scheduler.py:on_day; the evaluator replays a hidden period of 20 working days (9 November – 4 December 2026: 1,184 new requests, 45 waiting and 359 booked at the start) in one isolated process. The core of the case is slot utilization: as many patients seen as possible in the doctors' time, without breaking a rule. The setting is country-neutral: no national insurer or payer rules.

The agent sees an export of the booking system as a plain-text SQL dump (14 doctors in five departments with languages and weekly sessions, the calendar, four weeks of requests and appointments with their outcome — 1,074 appointments, 22% of the attended-or-missed ones missed — cancellations, attendance, the waitlist's history and the 359 appointments already booked from 9 November), the booking office's e-mails (absences, an interpreter's absence, staff notes, a director's-acquaintance request and the outpatient office's instructions: City Day on 23 November, the calendar not updated for it) and one pilot day: an input exactly as the engine sends it (one notice of each kind, an acquaintance note) and the answer of the client's pilot program under its interim rules (format only). The case has nine settings (3 tiers × 3 rule sets) on one world: the history, the hidden period, every request, cancellation intent, notice and note are the same in all nine, and the agent's view (request, data, deliverable spec) is byte-identical (checked by the validator). Only the hidden requirements and the evaluator differ. The engagement knobs apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

The daily flow

day 0 … 19 (end of the working day):
  engine: today's appointments happen (attendance) ─► patients' cancellations free slots ─► day input
          (new requests, notices, sessions of the next 10 working days, book, waitlist)
                                   │
                                   ▼
                           on_day(day) ─► actions (book / move / release) + status table
                                   │
                                   ▼
  evaluator: applies the actions in order; checks every rule on the changed appointments and on the
             upcoming book; scores the table against the true one; the book carries over to tomorrow
end:       operations value over the clinic days 10 Nov – 7 Dec (days 1–20, final after the last call)

Program state survives between calls (one process), but the input repeats everything that matters: the book (every upcoming appointment with its request) and the waitlist (requests without an appointment). A patient's cancellation arrives as a notice with the slot already free (the request closes); a patient cancels k working days before the appointment (hidden per-request intent, 135 of the requests), so how many cancellations happen depends on what the program booked.

The status table and the score

Status table (per department, after the day's actions, over the booking window d+1…d+10):

  • free_slots: calendar slots of the department's doctors with no appointment that anyone may be booked into — not held back, not in the closed clinic on City Day, (medium+) not on a notified absence day;
  • held_back: empty urgent-reserve slots (09:00 and 09:20 of a morning session) on d+2 or later (same exclusions);
  • waitlist: waiting requests of the department; medium+ without new patients whose referral has expired (valid until before d+1); hard counts patients (a patient's duplicate requests once) and not patients who hold an upcoming appointment there or had one take place in the period.

An entry is right only when exact; a day scores the share of its 15 entries; accuracy = mean over 20 days. A failed call scores 0 that day.

Operations value (the utilization objective):

TermValue
Patient-slots seen on clinic days 1–20 (new patient 2, follow-up 1)+10 each (hard: × probability the patient comes)
Waiting− 0.4 × urgency weight (U 3, S 2, R 1) per working day from the first possible day (≥ day 1) to the appointment, or to day 21 if never booked
Requests never booked (that could come before 18 Dec)− 10 each
Changes the hospital makes to an appointment (move or release)− 4 each
hard: overbooked slot− 12 × P(both patients come)
hard: extra hospital day of a patient booked in several departments− 3 each
hard: duplicatesa patient's requests to one department are one need

In the reference runs the seen term is 15,400 of easy's 11,800 operations value (utilization 98.2%), against 2,900–3,700 of waiting cost: utilization is the largest weight by far. Utilization = seen patient-slots / the bookable slots of days 1–20 (easy counts the absent doctors' slots: a locum covers them).

Objective (maximize) = 0.8 × operations value / the reference program's operations value in the same setting + 0.2 × table accuracy. The reference value is recomputed deterministically in-process (cached), so the reference scores exactly 0.8 + 0.2 × 1.000 = 1.0 and quality = objective. A program that books nothing but reports a perfect table is invalid (urgent requests unbooked).

Notes

  • One process per replay: casekit.program_session loads scheduler.py once and makes the 20 calls in the same isolated process. The case also replays the reference in-process (JSON round-tripped) for the normalizer and for the validator's examples; the subprocess run gives the same numbers.
  • Validator examples are the reference with a few actions appended on one day (found from a snapshot of the reference's book that day and checked by replaying the rest of the period in-process): an unknown request; a follow-up moved onto an occupied slot (hard: onto a new patient's slot) or to 12:20; a new patient moved to another department's doctor at the same time; a follow-up moved into the closed clinic on City Day, before its earliest date, into the reserve, at short notice, to another doctor, or into an absent doctor's day; two new-patient visits swapped to overfill a day's interpreters; test days moved to overfill the lab or onto the visit day; a new patient moved past its referral; an urgent appointment moved past its window; overbooked follow-ups beyond the cap; a waiting duplicate booked; a lower-priority request booked into a released slot. validate-case (9 settings, ~250 subprocess replays of ~0.3 s) takes about two minutes (with the dataset check).
  • Rates, capacities and patients are synthetic. A review of no-show models found that predictive overbooking can make some patient groups wait longer (see docs/specs/healthcare.md); the data carries no protected attributes and the hard tier rewards overbooking only through expected idle and collision costs.
