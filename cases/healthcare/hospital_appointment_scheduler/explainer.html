What the agent is asked

The client's request, the only text the agent gets up front. It is the same in every setting.

From Monday 9 November our outpatient booking office wants to work through a program: every working day it gets that day's new appointment requests, cancellations and notices together with our current bookings, books patients into the doctors' free slots and tells us where each department stands. Above all we want to stop losing doctor time to empty slots. Could you build it? Attached are an export of our booking system (doctors, clinic calendar, four weeks of requests and appointments with what happened to them, cancellations, attendance and the waitlist), the e-mails our booking office received (absences, notes, the outpatient office's instructions) and one pilot day (what the program receives and what our pilot answered).

Deliverable spec (the program interface)

A program/ directory with scheduler.py defining on_day(day). The engine loads it once and calls it once per
working day of the hidden period, in order, in the same process (module-level state survives between calls);
10 s per call, Python standard library only, no network and no access to the data files at run time.
day = {"date", "day_index", "days_in_period", "period_end", "booking_window": {"first", "last"}, "doctors",
"sessions", "new_requests", "notices", "attendance", "book", "waitlist"} exactly as in the pilot-day input file.
Return {"actions": [{"type": "book" | "move", "request_id", "doctor_id", "date": "YYYY-MM-DD",
"start": "HH:MM", "test_date": "YYYY-MM-DD" or null} | {"type": "release", "request_id"}],
"status_table": {department_code: {"free_slots": int, "held_back": int, "waitlist": int}}} as in the pilot-day
output file. Actions are applied in order; a request not booked stays on the waitlist.

Case notes (README)

Hospital Appointment Scheduler — healthcare/hospital_appointment_scheduler

A mid-size outpatient hospital wants its booking office to run day by day through a program. Every working day the program gets the day's new appointment requests, the notices of the day (patient cancellations, a doctor off sick or at a conference, an interpreter away, staff notes), today's attendance and the current booking book and waitlist (the effect of its own earlier decisions). It books requests into the doctors' free 20-minute slots of the next ten working days, re-books appointments a notice affects, fills freed slots from the waitlist and reports a daily status table per department. The agent delivers program/scheduler.py:on_day; the evaluator replays a hidden period of 20 working days (9 November – 4 December 2026: 1,184 new requests, 45 waiting and 359 booked at the start) in one isolated process. The core of the case is slot utilization: as many patients seen as possible in the doctors' time, without breaking a rule. The setting is country-neutral: no national insurer or payer rules.

The agent sees an export of the booking system as a plain-text SQL dump (14 doctors in five departments with languages and weekly sessions, the calendar, four weeks of requests and appointments with their outcome — 1,074 appointments, 22% of the attended-or-missed ones missed — cancellations, attendance, the waitlist's history and the 359 appointments already booked from 9 November), the booking office's e-mails (absences, an interpreter's absence, staff notes, a director's-acquaintance request and the outpatient office's instructions: City Day on 23 November, the calendar not updated for it) and one pilot day: an input exactly as the engine sends it (one notice of each kind, an acquaintance note) and the answer of the client's pilot program under its interim rules (format only). The case has nine settings (3 tiers × 3 rule sets) on one world: the history, the hidden period, every request, cancellation intent, notice and note are the same in all nine, and the agent's view (request, data, deliverable spec) is byte-identical (checked by the validator). Only the hidden requirements and the evaluator differ. The engagement knobs apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

The daily flow

day 0 … 19 (end of the working day):
  engine: today's appointments happen (attendance) ─► patients' cancellations free slots ─► day input
          (new requests, notices, sessions of the next 10 working days, book, waitlist)
                                   │
                                   ▼
                           on_day(day) ─► actions (book / move / release) + status table
                                   │
                                   ▼
  evaluator: applies the actions in order; checks every rule on the changed appointments and on the
             upcoming book; scores the table against the true one; the book carries over to tomorrow
end:       operations value over the clinic days 10 Nov – 7 Dec (days 1–20, final after the last call)

Program state survives between calls (one process), but the input repeats everything that matters: the book (every upcoming appointment with its request) and the waitlist (requests without an appointment). A patient's cancellation arrives as a notice with the slot already free (the request closes); a patient cancels k working days before the appointment (hidden per-request intent, 135 of the requests), so how many cancellations happen depends on what the program booked.

The status table and the score

Status table (per department, after the day's actions, over the booking window d+1…d+10):

  • free_slots: calendar slots of the department's doctors with no appointment that anyone may be booked into — not held back, not in the closed clinic on City Day, (medium+) not on a notified absence day;
  • held_back: empty urgent-reserve slots (09:00 and 09:20 of a morning session) on d+2 or later (same exclusions);
  • waitlist: waiting requests of the department; medium+ without new patients whose referral has expired (valid until before d+1); hard counts patients (a patient's duplicate requests once) and not patients who hold an upcoming appointment there or had one take place in the period.

An entry is right only when exact; a day scores the share of its 15 entries; accuracy = mean over 20 days. A failed call scores 0 that day.

Operations value (the utilization objective):

TermValue
Patient-slots seen on clinic days 1–20 (new patient 2, follow-up 1)+10 each (hard: × probability the patient comes)
Waiting− 0.4 × urgency weight (U 3, S 2, R 1) per working day from the first possible day (≥ day 1) to the appointment, or to day 21 if never booked
Requests never booked (that could come before 18 Dec)− 10 each
Changes the hospital makes to an appointment (move or release)− 4 each
hard: overbooked slot− 12 × P(both patients come)
hard: extra hospital day of a patient booked in several departments− 3 each
hard: duplicatesa patient's requests to one department are one need

In the reference runs the seen term is 15,400 of easy's 11,800 operations value (utilization 98.2%), against 2,900–3,700 of waiting cost: utilization is the largest weight by far. Utilization = seen patient-slots / the bookable slots of days 1–20 (easy counts the absent doctors' slots: a locum covers them).

Objective (maximize) = 0.8 × operations value / the reference program's operations value in the same setting + 0.2 × table accuracy. The reference value is recomputed deterministically in-process (cached), so the reference scores exactly 0.8 + 0.2 × 1.000 = 1.0. Quality is normalized between the baseline (0) and the reference (1): q = (objective − baseline) / (1 − baseline). A program that books nothing but reports a perfect table is invalid (urgent requests unbooked).

Baseline (baseline_solution): the booking office's practice without an engineer: the same program (earliest feasible slot in the priority order, every rule kept, the same exact status table), but the urgent reserve stays with urgent patients and is never released for tomorrow's clinics, and nothing is ever double-booked. The client itself names the released reserve as one of the two places it loses most doctor time today. It is valid in all nine settings (and on world seeds 1–3) and scores 0.79–0.81 in easy and medium, 0.69–0.70 in hard.

Stored dataset (dataset/)

Written by python -m fdebench2.cli export-dataset cases/healthcare/hospital_appointment_scheduler (checked by validate-case; about 11 MB, 193 files):

  • agent_view/<lang>/: the agent's data above, per locale.
  • hidden/days/<date>.json: the 20 calls of the hidden period. 9 November is the complete input; for the other days the fields that depend on the program's earlier decisions (attendance, book, waitlist, patients' cancellation notices) are left out — the rest (calendar, new requests, the absence / interpreter / staff notices, notes) is exactly what the engine sends.
  • hidden/mail/<lang>/*.eml: the period's e-mails behind those notices and notes (D10's keep-free note 11 Nov, D13 off sick 13 Nov, D05's conference 20 Nov, the interpreter 24 Nov, four director's-acquaintance requests).
  • hidden/requests.csv: every request of the start and the period with its hidden facts (no-show rate at zero lead, the working days before an appointment the patient cancels, the request it duplicates).
  • gt/<tier>_<ruleset>/: per setting, setting.json, the reference program/ and its score.json, and from the reference's replay actions.csv (every action per call), status_tables.csv (the expected table, 20 days × 5 departments), cancellations.csv (the patients' cancellations it met), final_book.csv (every appointment held in the period, with attendance where reported, and the upcoming ones) and final_waitlist.csv.

Notes

  • One process per replay: casekit.program_session loads scheduler.py once and makes the 20 calls in the same isolated process. The case also replays the reference in-process (JSON round-tripped) for the normalizer and for the validator's examples; the subprocess run gives the same numbers.
  • Validator examples are the reference with a few actions appended on one day (found from a snapshot of the reference's book that day and checked by replaying the rest of the period in-process): an unknown request; a follow-up moved onto an occupied slot (hard: onto a new patient's slot) or to 12:20; a new patient moved to another department's doctor at the same time; a follow-up moved into the closed clinic on City Day, before its earliest date, into the reserve, at short notice, to another doctor, or into an absent doctor's day; (rule sets A and C) a follow-up released, which the reference books again the next day inside the notice period counted from the release; two new-patient visits swapped to overfill a day's interpreters; test days moved to overfill the lab or onto the visit day; a new patient moved past its referral; an urgent appointment moved past its window; overbooked follow-ups beyond the cap; a waiting duplicate booked; a lower-priority request booked into a released slot. validate-case (9 settings, ~250 subprocess replays of ~0.3 s) takes about two minutes (with the dataset check).
  • Rates, capacities and patients are synthetic. A review of no-show models found that predictive overbooking can make some patient groups wait longer (see docs/specs/healthcare.md); the data carries no protected attributes and the hard tier rewards overbooking only through expected idle and collision costs.

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review of healthcare/hospital_appointment_scheduler

Findings of the case review (F1–F9), with the verifier's verdicts and what this change did about each. The numbers are for seed 20261116 unless they say otherwise; "q" is normalized quality (baseline 0, reference 1).

F1 — Utilization sits at the ceiling, so packing earns little (high, partly)

Capacity is about 79 slots a day and demand about 83–86, so the reference already fills 98% of the slots in easy and medium. The best valid variants we know (packing; in hard also a lower overbooking threshold and the true no-show rates) add only +0.1% to +1.5% to the objective, which is q = 1.005–1.056 against the new baseline. Valid programs still spread from about 0.79 to 1.0, but through a few coarse levers (releasing the reserve, the D10 note, overbooking at all) rather than through packing. The original "clipped at 1.0" point is gone with the new scale, but the policy cap of 1.5 is now out of reach: no known valid program earns more than about 70% of the quality credit.

Status: open — proposal: decide first whether better optimization should be measurable here. If yes, make placement harder rather than lowering demand (more follow-ups tied to one doctor, interpreter- and test-heavy days, mixed visit lengths) and add a stronger reference or an upper bound, so packing and look-ahead move the score by several percent. If not, set quality_cap in case.yaml to about 1.1, so that a program at the best known level gets full credit.

F2 — Overbooking does not need the per-patient no-show estimate (high, confirmed)

Overbooking every eligible follow-up slot up to the cap (threshold 0) scores 0.999 / 1.005 / 1.000 in hard A/B/C (q 0.997 / 1.015 / 1.000), all valid; a threshold of 0.10 scores slightly above the reference and no overbooking 0.943–0.952. So whether to overbook matters (about 5%), but the risk selection the hard tier is meant to test does not. Until this change the 10-against-12 trade-off was stated only as a score weight, which the simulated client may not reveal.

Status: open — proposal: make risk selection decide the score: raise the collision cost (or add a doctor overtime cost) so blanket overbooking is clearly net negative, and widen the spread of no-show rates so that selective overbooking clearly beats it. Add a validate-case check that the threshold-0 program scores at least 3% below the reference. (This change, under F6, already states the trade-off as a business fact in the hard requirements: when both patients come the doctor runs late, which costs a little more than an empty slot.)

F3 — Rule-set numbers are inert or one-sided (high, confirmed)

urgent_days = 1, 3 or 10 gives objective 1.0000 with no violation in all nine settings: urgent requests are always local-language, test-free, due tomorrow and generated only when tomorrow's reserve is untouched. Before this change the most conservative value of every number (2 interpreters, lab 5, overbooking 1, notice 4, urgent 1) was valid in 8 of 9 settings at 0.98–1.00, so only waitlist_order (and weakly closed_dept) separated the rule sets. After the F8 fix that envelope is invalid in 8 of 9 (waitlist_priority: unused interpreters and lab places no longer excuse a skipped patient), but notice 4 + overbooking 1 + urgent 1 alone is still valid in 6 of 9 at 0.988–1.000.

Status: open — proposal: make the remaining numbers two-sided, so the most cautious value is also wrong (for example a maximum rebooking delay next to the notice period, or an urgent window with a triage earliest day so that "tomorrow" is not always right). Generate some urgent requests that need an interpreter or a test, or that arrive when tomorrow's reserve is already used, so urgent_days binds. Add the envelope program to validate-case's distinctness check and require it to be invalid or more than 3% worse.

F4 — Bending the follow-up habit rewards the blanket bend, not the targeted one (high, confirmed)

Booking every follow-up with whichever colleague is earliest (287–332 exceptions) raises the objective by 2.0–5.8%; bending only for patients of an absent doctor (43–51 exceptions) by 0.0–1.3%. With the new scale the blanket bend is worth q 1.11–1.29, i.e. +0.04 to +0.115 on the final score before the penalty, so it now pays under startup_velocity (penalty 0.05) in 5 of 9 settings, while the clinically sensible targeted bend (+0.000 to +0.024) never pays. One approval covers all the exceptions.

Status: open — proposal: give some of the sick pediatrician's follow-ups a clinical deadline inside the absence, so that keeping the habit leaves them unseen and only a targeted, approved exception recovers the value. Add a small operations cost per follow-up seen by a doctor other than the previous one, so the blanket bend scores below the targeted one. Report bending in final-score terms in the README.

F5 — The visible history contradicts the hospital's rules (medium, confirmed)

In the export, 106 of the 151 history appointments at 09:00/09:20 are S/R requests booked two or more working days ahead, which the reserve rule forbids; interpreted new-patient visits reach 10 a day (cap 2–4) and fasting tests 10 a day (cap 5–7). _history fills slots at 92% with no reserve, interpreter or lab logic, while the initial book is rule-consistent. The pilot answer uses waitlist order received (rule set B's) and no reserve.

Status: open — proposal: generate the history under the rules all three rule sets share (reserve kept, at most 2 interpreted new-patient visits and 5 tests a day), or have an outpatient-office mail say that the reserve, interpreter rota and lab cap start on 9 November. Reword the lab cap as "fasting draw slots in the outpatient phlebotomy room" to make the small number plausible, and give the pilot an interim order that matches none of the rule sets (or say in the README that it matches B).

F6 — Scoring targets the client is told never to reveal (medium, partly)

scoring and scoring_hard were score formulas and weights, and status_table ("20% of quality") and several_departments ("costs 3") carried weights too, while the client prompt forbids describing scores or weights; Ask-F1 recall could not reach 1. The "already visible" part did not hold: in the default interact_req condition the agent sees only request.md and the data, so interface, visit_length and the period end still have to be asked.

Status: fixed in this change — the four targets are rewritten in all five locales as business facts (empty doctor time is the biggest loss; waiting, unbooked requests and changes cost; what counts with no-shows is the patients who come; a double-booked slot where both come costs a little more than an empty one; extra trips are a cost) with no weights. The hard requirements gain the one sentence the last fact needs; scoring is unchanged. interface, visit_length and evaluation_period are kept.

F7 — Lower-tier scope written as test instructions (medium, partly)

Easy and medium said "each request is its own visit, even when the same patient asks twice" and "tests out of scope", which reads like an instruction to the program rather than a fact of the hospital; deduplicating there is in fact invalid (waitlist_priority, 187–342 cases). The notes guidance (director's acquaintance, D10's afternoons) is stated only in hard, although following D10's note costs 6.7–7.5% in easy and medium.

Status: fixed in this change — the easy and medium scope bullets now say why, as the client would, in all five locales: the referral desk and the lab handle referral letters and fasting tests themselves this round; no-shows are not planned for; a second request gets its own appointment because the office cannot tell from a request whether it is the same complaint. Scoring is unchanged, and the matching targets are reworded. Left as a proposal: move the notes paragraph and the staff_notes target into all tiers, since the notes are in every tier's data.

F8 — Loose waitlist-priority excuses and a notice loophole (low, confirmed)

Reproduced with the old checker: a program that never books an interpreted new-patient visit was valid in 9 of 9 settings (0.950–0.988), one that books test-needing requests last was valid in 6 of 6 medium/hard settings (0.998–1.006), and a follow-up released on one day and re-booked the next day for the day after broke no rule in rule sets A and C (notice 3 and 4; with notice 2 it cannot happen).

Status: fixed in this change — a waiting interpreter-needing new patient is excused only when that day's in-person interpreters are all taken at the end of the call; a test-needing one only when no working day before the slot (from tomorrow, not before its earliest date) has lab room, the booked request's own test place counting as room; move_notice now also covers a released request booked again later (notice from the release day; the released date and time again need none), and the matching release excuse counts from the release day. The requirements (five locales), the move-notice target, case.yaml and the README say so. The reference now remembers its own releases; its results are unchanged in all nine settings. In A and C the validator's move_notice example is now the release path. A move to the same date and time with another doctor no longer asks for notice, as the text says (it was reachable only for IM on Friday afternoons, where D03 and D04 overlap). Not changed (owner's call): a follow-up booked with another doctor is still compared only with its previous doctor's queue.

F9 — Korean term choices (low, confirmed)

earliest_date was rendered "희망시작일" (desired start date) although the rule is a hard "never before"; S was "조기"; doctors signed as "교수" and the secretariat as "의국", which point to a university teaching hospital.

Status: fixed in this change — "진료 가능 시작일(earliest_date)" in the Korean requirements, targets and rule-set C's order label; S = "준응급"; doctors "{name} 과장" and the secretariat "{dept} 사무실" in the Korean mails (with the particle after "과장" adjusted). The Japanese mails still sign as "医局", which a native reviewer may want to check too.

Baseline: the booking office's practice without an engineer: the reference program, but the urgent reserve is never released for tomorrow's clinics and (hard) nothing is double-booked. Objective 0.806–0.812 in easy, 0.787–0.799 in medium and 0.689–0.701 in hard, against the reference's 1.0 in every setting (valid in all nine settings and on world seeds 1–3).
