# Ground truth: healthcare/hospital_appointment_scheduler

The ground truth (GT) of a setting in five parts, with the reference as supporting evidence. Generated by `gt_report.py` from `dataset/audit.json` (`fdebench2 audit-case`, public world seed 20261116): 45 settings, easy 15, medium 15, hard 15 (each tier's public panel plus admissible parameter combinations). Damage is 1 − credit, credit = 0 for an invalid program, else the quality score clipped to [0, 1]; a target bites in a setting when misreading it alone loses at least 0.10 of the credit (for a drawn parameter: its least harmful wrong candidate). The program runs the 20 hidden working days once per evaluation; the diagnostic reads the trace of that run.

**Mandatory outputs.** Every call returns its actions and the status table with a row for every department code and its three whole numbers (`status_table`). Every entry of a valid table that is not exact costs as much as two empty slots in the operations value (part 3).

**The bendable habit.** `follow_up_same_doctor` is the one business-policy rule. With an approved exception the rule is switched off; a follow-up seen by a colleague then counts a tenth of a slot less (−1 a visit: the colleague needs longer), and the policy takes its soft-rule penalty off the final score (0.1 under `enterprise_balanced`, 0.05 under `startup_velocity`), in which quality is one component (60% under `enterprise_balanced`, 60% under `startup_velocity` when every component applies, earning full credit at the quality cap 1.02). The table bends the reference three ways, scored with the rule relaxed: its quality gain over the reference (and the exceptions taken), and what the best of the three nets in the final score of a program below the cap (the quality share / the cap × the gain − the penalty). So the bend pays in some settings and loses in others. Before the audit a follow-up with a colleague cost nothing, and bending every follow-up gained 0.09–0.27 of quality in every setting; easy has no absence to bend around (a locum covers it).

## 3. Objective (higher is better)

objective = operations value / the reference's operations value (same setting). The operations value over the clinic days 10 November – 7 December:

- +10 per patient-slot seen (a new patient two slots); in hard × the chance the patient comes (their own rate plus 1 point per working day of lead, at most 90% away);
- −0.4 × urgency weight (U 3, S 2, R 1) per working day a request (hard: a patient's need in a department) waits, from its first possible day to its appointment, or to 8 December;
- −10 per request never booked (that could come before 18 December); a patient's cancellation closes the request;
- −4 per change the hospital makes to an appointment (move or release);
- −1 per follow-up seen by another doctor than the patient's own (only under the exception);
- −20 per entry of the daily status table that is not exact (15 entries a day; as much as two empty slots: the departments staff their clinics from the table);
- hard: −30 × the chance both patients of an overbooked slot come (three empty slots; was 12), and −3 per extra hospital day of a patient booked in several departments.

**Uncertainty.** The hard tier is expected-value scoring over the patients' no-shows. About one patient in six misses most appointments (rates around 0.6), the rest rarely do (around 0.06), and only the patient's record shows which; the history's attendance at known lead times shows the lead effect. A second follow-up in a slot is worth its own chance of coming and the waiting and unbooked cost it saves, less 30 × both chances: a pair pays when one of the two is likely to stay away, and loses when both are likely to come. Before the audit (12 for a collision, and a narrower spread of rates) overbooking every eligible slot scored as well as choosing (F2).

## 4. Reference and bounds

- **Reference** (`reference_solution`): `reference/scheduler.py` with the setting's `config.json`. Each day it moves (medium+) the absent doctors' appointments and the interpreter's excess, then books the waitlist and the new requests in the drawn priority order into the earliest feasible slot (the reserve only for U, or for tomorrow, every cap and date rule); in hard a waiting follow-up is doubled up next to a booked follow-up of its doctor where that is worth more than its free slot or than waiting (the chances estimated from the patient's record shrunk to the history's rate, and the lead), within the cap; it reports the exact table.
- **Baseline** (quality 0, `baseline_solution`): the office's practice without an engineer: the same program, but the urgent reserve is never released for tomorrow and nothing is double-booked.
- **Best known** (`best_known_solution`): the reference tuned on the hidden period itself (hindsight): coordinate search over packing (prefer slots next to occupied ones), in hard the patients' true no-show rates and the value of a free slot and of a booked request in the overbooking rule, keeping a move while the run stays valid and its objective grows.
- **Bound** (`objective_bound`, proven): a linear relaxation with perfect information. Every request (hard: need) gets at most one appointment on a day from its first possible one (medium+ a day later with a fasting test, not after its referral; an urgent one within its window), with its own doctor or any of its department; the book's appointments stay or move for 4 from day 3 on; a doctor-day holds at most its calendar slots (none in the closed clinic or on an absence); hard a session also holds its overbooking cap of second follow-ups, worth their visit less collisions of at least 30 × (p1 + p2 − 1) with each chance at its lowest. Order, reserve, interpreters, lab, slot positions and arrival times are relaxed, which leaves it loose (the gaps in the table below; hard counts every visit at a day's lead). Its scope is every valid program, the habit kept.

- **quality_cap** = 1.02: the best known quality is 1.000–1.138 over the measured settings (easy 1.000–1.038, medium 1.000–1.049, hard 1.038–1.138; median 1.019), and the bound is 0.150–0.670 above the reference.

## 5. Diagnostic cases

`score()` records a trace of the program's own run (per call: the rule breaches with what each one concerns, the appointments placed and released, the doubled-up slots, the reported table, the true table and the table each misreading would give; at the end the overbooked pairs' chances of coming), and `diagnose()` reads it, so the program runs once per evaluation. A priority breach is given to one reading: of those that explain every request it passed over (an interpreter, a blood test, a referral date, a second request, a release, a note on the booked request, another candidate order), the one that explains the most breaches; a breach nothing explains is the order's.
