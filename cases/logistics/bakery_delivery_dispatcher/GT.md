# Ground truth — logistics/bakery_delivery_dispatcher

The ground truth of every setting in five parts: the client facts the agent must learn, the validity checks, the objective, the reference with its bounds, and the diagnostic cases. Every table is generated by `make_gt.py` from `dataset/audit.json` (world seed 20261025; per tier the public panel and up to 12 admissible combinations of the tier's candidates: easy 9, medium 15, hard 15 settings; easy has only 9 combinations), except the bending, cheapest-insertion, guessed-travel and lucky never-asks numbers, which it computes from case.py on the same settings. Credit is 0 for an invalid plan, else quality clipped to [0, 1]; damage = 1 − credit. A fact bites in a setting when misreading it loses at least 0.1 of credit (for a parameter: the least harmful other candidate, an agent that guesses wrong but sensibly).

## 1. Client facts

One row per target (`locales/en/targets.yaml`). *Ablated as*: a parameter is read as each other candidate of its tier (the reference of that candidate, scored here); any other fact as the reference planner's plan with that one fact read the most natural wrong way the agent's view allows. *Damage*: min–max over the settings where it is in force (best guess for a parameter).

A parameter with an ordered pool bites only where the client's value is the strict end of it: a smaller tray limit or an earlier shift end than the client's costs little (a plan that keeps the stricter rule is valid), so `tray_limit` bites only where the limit is 100 (or in hard, 110 against the racks' 120) and `shift_end` only where the shift ends at 09:00 and the later shifts' plans come back after it.

Facts the client states that are not targets (they stay in requirements.md as context, and the client still answers them when asked):

- **One visit per café**: the deliverable has no tray count per stop, so a split delivery cannot be written; a café listed twice is already a `serve_each_once` violation. The fact changes no plan.
- **The lateness cost in easy** (a late minute costs as much as 3 van minutes): the first version drew it from 2, 3 and 5, but the reference is on time or nearly so in almost every setting, so no other weight changes its plan by a tenth of the scale (the validator found no binding in easy). It is now a fixed fact of every tier, and a target (`lateness`) in medium and hard only, where reading deliver_by as a firm deadline costs a tenth of the scale or more in some settings; in easy it never does.
- **The shift end in easy and medium** (10:00): no good plan comes back after 09:00 there, so it was drawn only in hard; in easy and medium it is a fixed rule that never binds the reference.
- **Vans may leave from 05:00** and **waiting until a café opens**: every plan worth handing in leaves after 05:00 and does not plan on dropping trays before opening; the scorer makes a van wait anyway.
- **How the dispatcher plans today**: context for the client's own way, the zero of the quality scale (part 3).

## 2. Validity checks

Every rule of case.yaml is a hard gate (`physical` and `contractual` never bend; `business_policy` bends only through the company policy's exception path). A file that cannot be read (no `routes.json`, an unknown van or café, two routes for one van, a departure not written HH:MM) is fatal.

| rule | class | tiers | why it is a hard gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| `van_capacity` | physical | easy, medium, hard | the crates do not fit: a van cannot carry more trays than this week's limit |
| `shift_window` | physical | easy, medium, hard | the drivers' shift: no van leaves before 05:00 or comes back after the shift end |
| `van_ready` | physical | hard | V2 is in the workshop until 06:40 on Monday (hard): it cannot leave before then |
| `closed_cafe` | physical | easy, medium, hard | nobody is there to take the trays at the café closed for renovation |
| `serve_each_once` | contractual | easy, medium, hard | the cafés' contract: every open café gets all its trays, in one visit |
| `cold_chain` | contractual | medium, hard | the partner's chilled products may only travel in the refrigerated van (medium, hard) |
| `premium_first` | business_policy | medium, hard | the client's habit (business policy): a hard gate under the baseline policy, bendable with an approved exception under the others |

## 3. Objective

objective = the minutes all vans are out (leaving the kitchen to coming back, waiting at a café that is not open yet included) + 3 × the minutes cafés are reached after their deliver_by; minimize.

- **Travel times.** Road distance is 1.3 times the straight line; vans drive 25 km/h all morning in easy and medium, and in hard 30 km/h before the rush and 20 km/h from it, also on a leg under way. Unloading takes 4 minutes plus 0.2 a tray. None of this is in the data: the request gives positions only.
- **The zero and the one.** Quality 0 is the dispatcher's hand plan (`baseline_solution`, described to the agent in the requirements): he gives out the cafés by opening time (or by deliver_by, or splits the map into four slices: he draws up the three and drives the cheapest), each to the van that can be there first, puts each round in its best order and sends each van out to reach its first café as it opens. It knows every rule and the travel times. The first version's zero point sent every van out at 05:00 along a slice of the map, 1.2 to 3.8 times the reference: a strawman that made a plan 20% worse than the reference still score about 0.9. Quality 1 is the reference (part 4).

## 4. Reference and bounds

- **Reference** (`reference_solution`): starts from the greedy cheapest-insertion construction, the tier's fallback plan and the dispatcher's plan; local search (relocation, exchanges between routes, reversals within one) and three ruin-and-recreate runs of 300 rounds (one with a cooling threshold), departures on a 5-minute grid; the best plan polished again with departures to the minute.
- **Best known** (`best_known_solution`, stored by `make_bounds.py` in `reference/best_known.json`): the best of a long ruin-and-recreate search (4 runs of 3,000 rounds), the mixed-integer model's best routes below, given to the vans in the cheapest valid way and driven with departures to the minute, and a search from them.
- **Bound** (`objective_bound`, proven): the dual bound of a mixed-integer model (scipy's HiGHS, 300 s per setting) of a relaxation: every café visited once by at most four routes, the tray limit, back by the shift end, the cold chain (the partner cafés on one route), no premium café right after a standard one where the habit holds, waiting and lateness priced as in the objective, and the client's travel times with the morning rush priced exactly (every leg at the speed before the rush plus the extra minutes the rush adds to it, a binary per leg for the side of the rush it starts on). Relaxed: departures free to the second, waiting allowed anywhere, and V2's workshop morning as "at most three routes leave before 06:40". Every valid plan maps onto a solution of the model that costs no more, so its dual bound is a lower bound. The first version drove every leg at 30 km/h in hard, 11% to 28% below the reference; now HiGHS solves the model to optimality in every stored setting (`mip` in `reference/best_known.json`), in 10 to 180 s each.

## 5. Diagnostic cases

`diagnose()` returns `enforced` and `evidence` per target in force, for valid and invalid plans alike (see case.py, diagnostics). A rule that only one fact states is read off the plan; every other fact by regret: a plan that loses at least 0.05 of the scale against the reference, and whose loss one of the fact's misreadings explains (its ablation's, the other candidates of its parameter, and a small grid round the client's value in both directions, `_other_way`; under the misread rules it is at least half its loss, 60% for the grid, closer to the reference planner's own plan for them), or that breaks a rule of the client's that the misreading would keep, is not enforced on that fact. *Separates*: the reference is enforced in every measured setting and every ablation that bites is not.

**A part of the work, not a shortcut.** Cheapest insertion (`insertion_plan`: the cafés in the dispatcher's order, each where it adds the least on any round, each van at its best departure) needs every fact the client knows: the rules and the travel times. Its quality per tier: easy 0.00–0.42 (above 0.5 in 0 of 9); medium 0.00–0.39 (above 0.5 in 0 of 15); hard 0.00–0.49 (above 0.5 in 0 of 15). It stays at or below 0.5 on this world but not on every world (REVIEW.md, Ground truth audit), so it is reported here and not as a shortcut: with every fact right, a good construction heuristic does a large part of the work, and the reference's search the rest.
