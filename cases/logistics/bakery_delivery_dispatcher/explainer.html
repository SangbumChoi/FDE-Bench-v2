What the agent is asked

The client's request, the only text the agent gets up front. It is the same in every setting.

Tomorrow (Monday) morning our central kitchen has to get the bread out to all our cafés with our four vans. Can you plan who drives where, so the cafés get their trays in time and the drivers aren't out longer than they need to be? The café list and the van list are attached.

Deliverable spec (shown to the extractor that reshapes the agent's files, not to the agent)

routes.json = {"routes": [{"van": "<van id, e.g. V1>", "depart": "HH:MM (optional, default 05:00)",
                           "stops": ["<cafe id in visiting order, e.g. C03>", ...]}, ...]}
One entry per van that is used; ids exactly as in the data.

Case notes (README)

Bakery Delivery Dispatcher — logistics/bakery_delivery_dispatcher

A bakery's central kitchen delivers bread trays to its cafés every morning with four vans. The client asks the agent to plan tomorrow's (Monday) routes "so the cafés get their trays in time and the drivers aren't out longer than they need to be". The deliverable is routes.json: for each van used, a departure time (HH:MM, 05:00 if left out) and the cafés in visiting order. The evaluator drives the plan through the setting's travel model and scores it against the reference.

The case has settings: three tiers × rule seeds (DESIGN.md, Settings). The data and the request are the same in every setting; only the client's hidden rules, and so the right plan, change. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md). It is also the framework's test fixture (tests/conftest.py, tests/test_validator.py), so its validation has to stay fast.

  • late_cost: the hard/s5 reference is 56 minutes late in all; its objective moves by 7.9% (weight 2) and 15.8% (weight 5).

What an agent must ask

The clarification targets, by tier (locales/<lang>/targets.yaml):

  • Every tier: road distance (1.3 ×), service time, the shift end, the closed café (which one), one visit per café, the premium-first habit (on, or suspended this week), the lateness cost (its weight), and the tray limit when it is below the racks' 120 (tray_limit, drawn 100 or 110; hard always 110).
  • easy and medium: the constant 25 km/h (speed).
  • easy: that any van may serve the partner cafés this week (partner_this_week).
  • medium and hard: the cold chain (partner cafés by V4 only).
  • hard: the rush hour and its start (rush_hour), V2 in the workshop until 06:40 (workshop) and that the C07 manager's demand is not a rule (manager_pressure).

So 9 or 10 targets in easy and medium, 12 in hard.

Data

cafes (18 cafés C01–C18: x_km / y_km relative to the kitchen at (0, 0), trays 8–30, tier premium/standard, opens, deliver_by 30, 45 or 60 minutes after opening, partner_brand) and vans (V1–V4, max_trays 120, only V4 refrigerated). File names, headers and labels are localized (매장목록.csv with 매장등급 = 프리미엄, cafeterias.csv with categoria = premium); IDs and numbers are not, and a café's name follows its ID (C11 is always South Hill café). The closed café is an ordinary row: only the client knows it is closed.

At the case seed the 18 cafés order 332 trays (309 to 318 with the closed café out, against 400 to 480 trays of fleet capacity). The partner cafés are Harbour café (C05, standard), Clock Tower café (C10, premium) and Stadium café (C16, premium); the other premium cafés are C01, C04, C07 and C13. C07 opens at 07:00 (deliver by 08:00), so the manager's "before 06:30" would mean a van waiting half an hour at her door.

Which worlds are used. build_world keeps drawing from the seed's stream until a world is usable for every setting a rule seed can draw (public or private):

  1. For each tier, the reference solver serves all 18 cafés under the tier's strictest combination (100 trays, back by 09:00, in hard the 06:30 rush and V2 from 06:40, the habit and the cold chain where the tier has them). That plan is the tier's fallback: with the closed café dropped it keeps every setting's rules (a later shift end, more trays, a later rush, no habit and one stop fewer never bring a van back later), so every setting has a valid plan.
  2. The parameters can change the answer: under each tier's headline, the reference brings a van back after the earliest shift end (hard), loads one above the smallest tray limit (medium), is late for at least 1% of its objective, and costs at least 1% more than without the premium-first habit (hard).
  3. Every rule has a plan that breaks only it in hard with 110 trays and the 09:00 shift end.

About 9% of draws pass; a world takes 0.8 s on average to build (at most 3.3 s on world seeds 1–30).

Quality (lower is better): the total time all vans are out, from departure to return (waiting at cafés that are not open yet included), plus late_cost × every minute a café is reached after its deliver_by.

Situations present

  • Hidden facts: which café is closed; this week's tray limit below max_trays; the shift end; the lateness weight; whether the premium-first habit holds; in hard, when the rush starts.
  • Data semantics: partner_brand = yes means chilled goods, so the refrigerated van only (medium, hard), but not this week in easy.
  • Habit that may bend: premium first, where drawn. It costs 27–28% of the objective at the headline settings, so bending it through the exception process pays where business policy is soft (REVIEW.md, F3).
  • Dated event (hard): V2 in the workshop until 06:40 on Monday.
  • Pressure (hard): the C07 manager's demand to be the first stop before 06:30. The client says it is not a rule; following it means waiting at a café that opens at 07:00 (case seed) and reshuffling a route.
  • Trade-off: leaving later avoids waiting at cafés that are not open yet, but in hard legs after the rush start are slower, and late arrivals cost late_cost times as much as van time.

Reference and baseline

The reference (_plan) builds the routes by cheapest feasible insertion (partner cafés first when the cold chain applies, then by tier, biggest first), retrying with V4 seeded by each workable order of the partner cafés and then with shuffled insertion orders when the greedy construction corners itself; it also starts from the tier's fallback without the closed café. Each start is improved by relocating single stops, the cheaper one by 300 rounds of ruin and recreate (2–4 neighbouring or random cafés taken out and put back by cheapest insertion), and each route gets its best departure on a 5-minute grid. It is computed once per world and rule values.

The baseline (baseline_solution) is the dispatcher's own plan without an engineer: when the cold chain applies, V4 takes the partner cafés (premium first, in the order that brings it back soonest); the other cafés are taken in bearing order round the kitchen, starting due west, and loaded onto V1, V2, V3 and then V4 until the next one no longer fits (this week's tray limit, back by the shift end); each van serves its premium cafés first and then the rest in order of opening; every van leaves at 05:00 (in hard V2 at 06:40), when the shift starts, and deliver_by is ignored. If a café is left over, the sweep starts from the next café round; if no start works, the tier's fallback routes are driven with every van leaving as early as it may (always valid; not needed so far).

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review of logistics/bakery_delivery_dispatcher

Findings of the case review (F1–F9), with the verifier's verdicts and what this change did about each. The numbers are for the case seed 20261025 unless they say otherwise; "q" is normalized quality (baseline 0, reference 1). The headroom numbers come from a simulated-annealing search from the reference (relocate, swap and 2-opt moves, departures to the minute), which reproduced the review's 601.51 and 521.98.

F1 — The closed café was called by another café's name (high, confirmed)

All five locales said "Clock Tower café (C11)" is closed. In the data Clock Tower is C10, a premium partner café, and C11 is South Hill (남산점, 南山店, Cafetería Colina Sur). An agent that followed the name, serving C11 and skipping C10, handed in an invalid plan: the reference edited that way breaks closed_cafe, serve_each_once (C10 not served) and shift_window (V4 back at 10:08). Validity depended on trusting IDs over names, not on asking the right question.

Status: fixed in this change — requirements.md and targets.yaml in all five locales now name the café as the data does: South Hill café (C11), 남산점(C11), 南山店（C11） (ja, zh), Cafetería Colina Sur (C11). C10 stays open. The validator's name (ID) lint, which flagged the mismatch in all five locales, is now silent.

Rule seeds: the closed café is now a parameter (C11, C03 or C14). Each candidate's wording in every locale comes from params.closed_cafe.labels in case.yaml and is the name the data gives that ID (a café's name follows its ID in every world), so the fix holds for every drawn café; the pressure café of the hard tier, C07, is named as in the data too. The name (ID) lint is silent in every public setting.

F2 — Little headroom above the reference (high, confirmed)

The search reaches 601.51 under all rules, 3.8% below the reference's 625.55. Quality is no longer clipped at 1.0: against the new baseline (963.09) that plan scores q = 1.07 and earns 0.71 of the quality credit at the policy cap of 1.5, against 0.67 for the reference. The review's naive angle-sweep plan with tuned departures (686.39) scores q = 0.82. So naive and expert plans now separate (0, 0.82, 1.0, 1.07), but no known compliant plan gets near the cap.

Status: open — proposal: either strengthen the reference (swap and 2-opt moves, departures to the minute: about 601.5 on this world) or keep it and set quality_cap: 1.1 in case.yaml, so that a plan at the best known level earns full credit. Tightening the instance (F3, F4) would spread plans further.

F3 — Bending premium-first pays only marginally, and not where the habit hurts (high, partly)
