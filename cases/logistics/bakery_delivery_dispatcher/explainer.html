Case notes (README)

Bakery Delivery Dispatcher — logistics/bakery_delivery_dispatcher

A bakery's central kitchen delivers bread trays to its cafés every morning with four vans. The client asks the agent to plan tomorrow's (Monday) routes "so the cafés get their trays in time and the drivers aren't out longer than they need to be". The deliverable is routes.json: for each van used, a departure time (HH:MM, 05:00 if left out) and the cafés in visiting order. The evaluator drives the plan through the world's travel model and scores it against the reference.

The case has no settings: it is one fixed problem at every difficulty (no tiers, no rule sets). The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md). It is also the framework's test fixture (tests/conftest.py), so its validation has to stay fast.

Data

cafes (18 cafés C01–C18: x_km / y_km relative to the kitchen at (0, 0), trays 8–30, tier premium/standard, opens, deliver_by, partner_brand) and vans (V1–V4, max_trays 120, only V4 refrigerated). File names, headers and labels are localized (매장목록.csv with 매장등급 = 프리미엄, cafeterias.csv with categoria = premium); IDs and numbers are not. The closed café is an ordinary row: only the client knows it is closed.

At the case seed (20261025) 17 cafés are open with 356 trays against 480 trays of fleet capacity. The partner cafés are Harbour café (C05, standard), Clock Tower café (C10, premium) and Stadium café (C16, premium); the other premium cafés are C01, C04, C07 and C13.

What the client knows (hidden requirements)

  • Road distance is 1.3 times the straight-line distance. Speed depends on when a leg starts: 30 km/h before 07:00, 20 km/h from 07:00 (rush hour). Unloading takes 4 minutes plus 0.2 minutes per tray.
  • A van that arrives before opens waits. Arriving after deliver_by is accepted, but each late minute costs the bakery as much as three minutes of van time (complaints, credit notes).
  • Vans leave from 05:00 and are back by 09:30 (end of the drivers' shift); each carries at most max_trays.
  • South Hill café (C11) is closed this week for renovation.
  • partner_brand cafés sell the partner's chilled products, so only the refrigerated van V4 may serve them.
  • Each café gets all its trays in one visit from one van.
  • The company habit: on every route, premium cafés come before standard cafés.

There are 9 clarification targets: road distance, speeds, service time, shift, closed café, cold chain, one visit, premium first and the lateness cost.

Rules

RuleClassMeaning
van_capacityphysicalA van carries at most 120 trays.
shift_windowphysicalVans leave at 05:00 or later and are back at the kitchen by 09:30.
closed_cafephysicalNo delivery to the closed café (C11).
serve_each_oncecontractualEvery open café is served exactly once.
cold_chaincontractualPartner-brand cafés are served only by the refrigerated van.
premium_firstbusiness policyOn every route, premium cafés come before standard cafés (may bend through the exception process under policies that make business policy soft).

Quality (lower is better): the total time all vans are out, from departure to return (waiting at closed cafés included), plus 3 × every minute a café is reached after its deliver_by.

Situations present

  • Hidden fact: C11 is closed this week; nothing in the data says so.
  • Data semantics: partner_brand = yes means chilled goods, so the refrigerated van only.
  • Habit that may bend: premium first. At this seed it makes V4 serve C16 and C10 before C05, so C05 is reached about 10 minutes after its 07:30 deliver_by in every compliant plan.
  • Trade-off: leaving later avoids waiting at cafés that are not open yet, but legs that start from 07:00 are slower and late arrivals cost three times as much.
  • No dated event and no stakeholder pressure (see REVIEW.md, F8).

Reference and baseline

The reference (_solve) builds the routes by cheapest feasible insertion (partner cafés first, then by tier, biggest first), retrying with V4 seeded by each workable order of the partner cafés and then with shuffled insertion orders when the greedy construction corners itself. It then relocates single stops while the objective drops, and gives each route its best departure on a 5-minute grid.

The baseline (baseline_solution) is the dispatcher's own plan without an engineer: V4 takes the partner cafés (premium first, in the order that brings it back soonest); the other cafés are taken in bearing order round the kitchen, starting due west, and loaded onto V1, V2, V3 and then V4 until the next one no longer fits (capacity, back by 09:30); each van serves its premium cafés first and then the rest in order of opening; every van leaves at 05:00, when the shift starts, and deliver_by is ignored. If a café is left over, the sweep starts from the next café round (if no start works, the reference routes are driven with every van leaving at 05:00, which is always valid; no world built so far needs this).

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review of logistics/bakery_delivery_dispatcher

Findings of the case review (F1–F9), with the verifier's verdicts and what this change did about each. The numbers are for the case seed 20261025 unless they say otherwise; "q" is normalized quality (baseline 0, reference 1). The headroom numbers come from a simulated-annealing search from the reference (relocate, swap and 2-opt moves, departures to the minute), which reproduced the review's 601.51 and 521.98.

F1 — The closed café was called by another café's name (high, confirmed)

All five locales said "Clock Tower café (C11)" is closed. In the data Clock Tower is C10, a premium partner café, and C11 is South Hill (남산점, 南山店, Cafetería Colina Sur). An agent that followed the name, serving C11 and skipping C10, handed in an invalid plan: the reference edited that way breaks closed_cafe, serve_each_once (C10 not served) and shift_window (V4 back at 10:08). Validity depended on trusting IDs over names, not on asking the right question.

Status: fixed in this change — requirements.md and targets.yaml in all five locales now name the café as the data does: South Hill café (C11), 남산점(C11), 南山店（C11） (ja, zh), Cafetería Colina Sur (C11). C10 stays open. The validator's name (ID) lint, which flagged the mismatch in all five locales, is now silent.

F2 — Little headroom above the reference (high, confirmed)

The search reaches 601.51 under all rules, 3.8% below the reference's 625.55. Quality is no longer clipped at 1.0: against the new baseline (963.09) that plan scores q = 1.07 and earns 0.71 of the quality credit at the policy cap of 1.5, against 0.67 for the reference. The review's naive angle-sweep plan with tuned departures (686.39) scores q = 0.82. So naive and expert plans now separate (0, 0.82, 1.0, 1.07), but no known compliant plan gets near the cap.

Status: open — proposal: either strengthen the reference (swap and 2-opt moves, departures to the minute: about 601.5 on this world) or keep it and set quality_cap: 1.1 in case.yaml, so that a plan at the best known level earns full credit. Tightening the instance (F3, F4) would spread plans further.

F3 — Bending premium-first pays only marginally, and not where the habit hurts (high, partly)

With premium_first relaxed the search reaches 521.98 with no late minutes (q = 1.31), 13.2% below the best compliant plan. Every compliant plan reaches C05 about 10 minutes late, because V4 has to serve C16 and C10 first (reference V4: C16 06:19, C10 07:00, C05 07:40). The old "clipped at 1.0" argument is gone with the new scale. With all four score components counted (quality weight 0.6), bending gains 0.094 of the final score before the penalty against the best compliant plan. That nets −0.006 under enterprise_balanced (penalty 0.1) and +0.044 under startup_velocity (0.05). Against the reference it nets +0.023 under enterprise_balanced. The best bent plan bends three of the four routes, not only V4.

Status: open — proposal: decide whether a well-argued exception should pay here. If yes, make the habit's cost concrete and local: for example a partner SLA ("partner cafés charge us for every late minute"), so the compliant V4 route costs clearly more than a bent one and the business reason is the one the agent should give. Keep the README's with-and-without numbers (601.51 against 521.98) up to date. If no, remove the conflict (for example a later deliver_by for C05) or make the habit contractual, so that bending never nets a gain.

F4 — Capacity, the shift and the one-visit target do not decide the best plan (medium, partly)

In the best compliant plan the loads are 89/85/115/67 trays against 120, and the latest return is 09:21 against 09:30. Open demand is 356 trays against 480 of fleet capacity. The deliverable has no per-stop quantity, so the one_visit target cannot change a plan (a café listed twice is already a serve_each_once violation). Both rules do bind on weaker plans: the baseline's sweep moves to the next van exactly when capacity or the shift cuts it off. van_capacity has no clarification target (max_trays is in the data).

Status: open — proposal: if the physical rules should matter for strong plans, make one bind at the optimum: a shift end of 09:00, or demand near 90% of the fleet. A smaller V4 would not do it, since the compliant V4 route carries 67 trays. Either drop the one_visit target or allow split deliveries with per-stop tray counts, so that the fact changes the deliverable.

F5 — The lateness cost was stated only as a scoring rule (medium, confirmed)

The 3× weight on late minutes appeared only under "How the plan is judged". The client prompt forbids describing weights, and the data notes only said deliver_by is "the latest arrival the café is happy with". Whether the agent learned the trade-off, and whether the lateness target could be covered, depended on the simulated client breaking its own instructions.

Status: fixed in this change — the data notes in all five locales now state the cost as a business fact, right after the deliver_by line: "A late delivery is accepted but not free: every minute a café is reached after its deliver_by costs us as much as three minutes of van time (complaints and credit notes to the café)." The "How the plan is judged" section is unchanged; reducing it to the manager's voice is left to the owner.

F6 — Some world seeds crashed the generator (medium, confirmed)

At review time the reference solver raised on 5 of world seeds 1–30, and 3 of those worlds had no valid plan, because partner and tier roles are fixed by index.

Status: not reproduced — the earlier commit "Bakery dispatcher: work on any world seed" (adb576f) makes build_world keep drawing from the seed's stream until the reference finds a valid plan and every rule has a plan that breaks only it, and the construction now retries from each workable partner order and from shuffled orders. World seeds 1–50 all build, and validate-case --world-seeds 1,...,50 passes: reference, violating examples and the new baseline. The roles are still fixed by index; the redraw does the work.

F7 — Delivery during opening hours, the speed step and district names (medium, partly)

Vans wait until a café opens, and deliver_by is 45–90 minutes after opening, whereas real bakery drops usually land before opening. Speed depends only on when a leg starts: a leg starting at 06:59 runs entirely at 30 km/h, one starting at 07:00 takes 50% longer. District names ignore the coordinates (North Station café is at y = −10.27, South Hill café at y = +6.05), though the data never says +y is north. The verifier finds these to be realism judgments rather than defects: the rules are stated and apply to every agent alike.

Status: open — proposal: if the case is reworked (F8), turn opens into a receiving window ("staff on site from") with deliver_by at or before opening, use a speed that changes mid-leg (or ramps over 06:45–07:15), and assign district names from the coordinates. Each of these changes the data and the reference, so they belong with the F8 redesign rather than in a patch.

F8 — No settings, and only hidden facts (medium, confirmed)

The case has no tiers or rule sets, so every difficulty column scores the same problem. Of the three situation kinds it has only hidden facts (C11 closed, the cold-chain meaning, the premium-first habit): no dated event and no stakeholder pressure. It is also the framework's test fixture (tests/conftest.py), which makes changes to it costly.

Status: open — proposal: first move the fixture tests to a small dedicated case. Then convert to 3 tiers × 3 rule sets with byte-identical data. Easy: the travel model, shift, closed café and lateness cost, with the cold chain and premium-first stated as out of scope this week. Medium: adds the cold chain, rush-hour speeds and premium-first (bendable). Hard: adds a dated event (V2 in the workshop until 06:40, or a doubled order that makes capacity bind) and a pressure message (a premium manager demanding to be first). Rule sets could vary which café is closed, the shift end, the lateness weight and the rush start. A hidden V4 capacity would contradict the visible max_trays, so it should be framed as a hidden fact ("V4's chiller shelf is broken this week").

F9 — Korean wording of the 07:00 boundary and one column label (low, confirmed)

07:00 이전 / 07:00 이후 both include 07:00 in standard Korean usage, so a leg starting exactly at 07:00 fell in both speed bands; minute-level departures make that case reachable. The column label 최종도착 reads as "final arrival" rather than a deadline. Numbers agree across the five locales.

Status: fixed in this change — ko/requirements.md and ko/targets.yaml now say "07:00 전에 출발하는 구간은 시속 30km, 07:00부터 출발하는 구간은 시속 20km". The Korean column is renamed 납품마감 in the data (case.py) and in every Korean text that names it.

Baseline: the dispatcher's own plan (V4 takes the partner cafés; the rest are split by bearing round the kitchen and loaded onto V1, V2, V3, then V4; premium first, then by opening; every van leaves at 05:00): 963.09 against the reference's 625.55 at seed 20261025 (×1.54). On world seeds 1, 2 and 3: 1,292.28 against 568.06, 1,192.03 against 779.02 and 1,402.46 against 560.91. Over seeds 1–50 it is valid in every world, at ×1.37 to ×4.16 the reference (median ×2.19).
