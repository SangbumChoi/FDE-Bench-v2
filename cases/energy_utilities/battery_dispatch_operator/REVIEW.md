# Review — `energy_utilities/battery_dispatch_operator`

Findings of the case review (F1–F9) and what this change did about each; numbers are at world seed 20270322
unless stated. Comments welcome on the open proposals.

### F1 — Reference far below a plain LP; the task is convex, not combinatorial (high, confirmed)

The reference is a greedy two-cycles-a-day heuristic at 18,084.35 EUR. A linear program on the forecast prices
(2,004 variables, about 3 s with HiGHS) reaches 21,343.2 EUR at actual prices (q = 1.180), and a perfect-foresight
LP 23,099.9 (q = 1.277), so the reference sits at 85% of what a careful agent can reach. The review was written
against a 1.0 quality cap, under which every valid LP saturated; on the new scale (baseline 0, cap 1.5) the
forecast LP earns 1.180 / 1.5 = 0.79 of the quality credit, so the problem is now the opposite: the cap is out of
reach for any plan made from the forecast. The LP never charges and discharges in one quarter, so the either/or
rule never binds and `task_type: Combinatorial Optimization` does not fit.

**Status:** open — proposal: keep the heuristic as the reference and set `quality_cap` in case.yaml to the forecast
LP's normalized quality (1.18 at the default seed, 1.11–1.16 at seeds 1–3; 1.15 would be a fair single value), so
the best attainable plan earns full credit. The alternative, making the forecast LP the reference, needs an LP
solver in case.py (scipy is not a dependency today). Relabel `task_type` as LP / convex optimization unless
non-convex rules are added (minimum run blocks, a daily cycle cap with start counts).

**Update (rule seeds):** still open, with new numbers. The lower export caps make clipped solar the main source of
value, so the reference got two refinements: the night fill leaves room for the cheaper daylight refill, and the
reserve is sold once its hours are over. Across the nine public settings and world seeds 20270322 and 1–3, the
forecast LP reaches 1.03–1.30 of the reference, and perfect foresight 1.11–1.38. A single `quality_cap` of about
1.2 would still fit most settings; the 25 EUR/MWh degradation settings (seed 334) sit at 1.11–1.30. `task_type`
is unchanged.

### F2 — Last week's log reveals most hidden rules (high, partly)

`dispatch_log_last_week.csv` is generated by the reference heuristic with the hidden constants, and its
`soc_pct` column shows them: maximum 90.0%, minimum 20.0%, start and end 50.0%, at least 20% in 17:00–21:00, and
SOC steps that give 0.92 per direction exactly. The data also shows the clock change (668 solar rows, 23 price
hours on 28 March), and ignoring degradation costs only 0.1% (21,320 vs. 21,343 EUR). The verifier found the
"one question is enough" claim overstated: the grid-charging window cannot be read from the log, and a plan
without it or without the end-SOC rule is invalid.

**Status:** open — proposal: make the log stale evidence to check with the client, generated under last week's
different parameters (e.g. a 15% floor, a different reserve window, an older efficiency), or drop or coarsen
`soc_pct`. Then decide explicitly which targets are "confirm what the data suggests" and which are "only the
client knows", and make degradation large enough to change the plan (see F3).

**Update (rule seeds):** partly resolved. The log is unchanged (byte-identical data). It still shows last week's
values because last week's planner made it with last week's conventions (`LOG_RULES` in case.py). Every value it
reveals is now a drawn parameter, though, and under every public setting at least one of them is wrong (always the
25 MW cap). So the log is stale evidence, and the requirements say so in the client's words ("planned with last
week's settings, and not all of them are the same this week"). Degradation is a parameter (5/15/25 EUR/MWh) and
changes the plan; 38–84% of the reference's objective at hard/s141. The proposal to coarsen `soc_pct` stays open.

### F3 — No settings; several parameters are natural rule-set knobs (high, confirmed)

The case has one ground truth (`setting.json` is `{}`), no tiers or rule sets, and until this change no README.
All parameters are constants in case.py. Degradation at EUR 5/MWh barely matters (an LP that ignores it cycles 886 instead
of 808 MWh and loses only 0.1% of profit), while EUR 15–25/MWh cuts throughput to 523–433 MWh and removes most of the thin
morning cycle.

**Status:** open — proposal: easy covers the SOC window, power, per-direction efficiency, end SOC, the 25 MW cap and
the clock change, with the requirements saying that the evening reserve and the import window do not apply this
week; medium adds the import window, the reserve, a binding degradation cost and a warranty cycle cap (e.g. 1.5
equivalent cycles a day); hard adds dated events and pressure (F4). Rule sets A/B/C could vary the export cap
(25/22/27 MW), reserve (20% 17–21 h, 30% 18–22 h, 15% 16–20 h), import window (00–06, 01–05, 23–07), degradation
(5/15/25 EUR/MWh), end SOC (50/40/60%) and warranty window (10–90, 15–85, 5–95%), checked so each rule set's
reference is invalid or at least 1% worse under the others.

**Status (rule seeds):** fixed in this change: three tiers and a parameter pool per hidden value, drawn per rule
seed (see "Rule seeds" below). This follows the proposal, with five differences. The export cap pool is 18/15/12 MW,
not 25/22/27, because a cap at or above the battery's 20 MW rarely binds. The import windows are 00-06/01-05/02-08,
so no window nests in another. The end SOC is 50/40/30%, since a target above the 50% start would make the idle
baseline invalid. There is no warranty cycle cap. Hard's dated notices are those of F4.

### F4 — The bendable reserve gains nothing; no pressure or dated events (medium, confirmed)

`reserve_20pct` is the only business-policy rule. Relaxing it gives the forecast LP 22,156.6 vs. 21,343.2 EUR
(q 1.225 vs. 1.180, +0.03 of quality credit at cap 1.5) and the reference 19,733.9 vs. 18,084.35 (+9.1%), less
than the soft-rule penalty (0.1 in enterprise_balanced, 0.05 in startup_velocity), so a competent agent should
never bend it. Nothing pushes the agent towards the reserve, and the only dated fact (the 1 March–30 June
amendment) covers the whole week.

**Status:** open — proposal: add a pressure item that pays only through `request_exception`, e.g. a trader email
in `inbox/` ("Thursday's 19:00 spike is 300 EUR, just use the reserve"). Add dated events that change the optimum:
substation works ending Friday 26 March 00:00 (cap back to 30 MW), a DSO curtailment notice for Saturday 11–15 h
(export at most 10 MW), or a capacity-market test on Wednesday 19:00–20:00 that needs 20 MW for an hour.

**Update (rule seeds):** partly resolved. Hard adds three dated notices: the works end early (`works_end`, the
agreement's 30 MW applies again), a DSO export limit on one evening 18:00–21:00 (`dso_day`, `dso_mw`), and inverter
maintenance with the battery offline 08:00–20:00 on one day (`outage_day`). The DSO limit is in the evening, not at
midday as proposed: a midday curtailment barely moves the optimum, since the battery charges at midday anyway. The
capacity-market test was left out because it forces a discharge, which the idle baseline cannot meet. The trading desk's push to sell the reserve is
in hard's requirements, in the client's words, since the data must be the same in every setting; an inbox email
would need an engagement event. With the reserve a parameter (15/20/30%, three windows), bending it is now worth
+0% to +13% to a forecast LP and +4% to +19% to the reference at world seed 20270322. It pays most under a 30%
reserve: +13% is about +0.09 of quality credit at cap 1.5, close to the 0.05–0.1 soft-rule penalty.

### F5 — A fixed week against noisy forecast prices is not how operators bid (medium, confirmed)

The plan is committed for 7 days against a "day-ahead" forecast and settled on actual prices = forecast + daily
bias N(0, 4) + hourly N(0, 7). The noise is zero-mean and the objective is linear in price for a fixed plan, so
planning on the forecast is already optimal and the uncertainty tests nothing. Forecast prices are floored at
3 EUR (13 hours sit at exactly 3.0) although 67 quarters have solar above 25 MW, and the catalog row says
"against tomorrow's prices".

**Status:** open — proposal: convert to a program deliverable driven day by day (`casekit.program_session`: each
day's cleared day-ahead prices, the current SOC and any notices), so re-planning, carrying SOC across days and
dated events are what gets scored. If it stays a batch file, provide last week's forecast and actual prices (or
quantiles) so robust planning becomes learnable, and allow negative prices so curtailment, solar charging and the
import window interact.

### F6 — Validity hinged on rounding, not judgement (medium, confirmed)

SOC was checked to 0.001 MWh at every quarter. The exact forecast LP, rounded to 3 decimals, broke `soc_limits`
and `end_soc` (minimum 9.997%); rounded to 2 decimals it also broke `reserve_20pct`; the same held at seeds 1–3.
Separately, a plan using the textbook sqrt(84.6%) = 0.91978 per direction overshoots to 90.47% (0.19 MWh over).

**Status:** fixed in this change — `E_TOL` is now 0.02 MWh (0.05% of capacity). The forecast LP rounded or floored
to 2 or 3 decimals is valid at seeds 20270322, 1, 2 and 3 (worst overshoot 0.006 MWh), and the reference and its
objective are unchanged; every rule-breaking example still breaks exactly its rule. The efficiency half stays open — proposal: if the
efficiency should be a real hidden fact, use an asymmetric per-direction value that clearly differs from
sqrt(round trip) (e.g. 0.95 charge / 0.89 discharge, auxiliaries included), so the wrong assumption costs value or
validity by a wide margin; the wider tolerance does not rescue the sqrt plan.

**Update (rule seeds):** the efficiency half is resolved. `efficiency` is a parameter with 92/92, 95/89 and 90/94.
Every pair gives the sheet's 84.6% round trip, so only the client can say how it splits, and the wrong split makes
the reference invalid (`soc_limits`) or 3–5% worse.

### F7 — Contractual rules are not grounded in the documents (medium, confirmed)

The agreement says the controller curtails solar whenever the site would exceed the export capacity, and revenue
is already capped at min(net, 25), so an informed optimizer never breaks `export_cap`; the rule exists only to
make the stale 30 MW belief invalid (that plan scores 19,977.0 EUR). The agreement lists 20 MW import with no time
window, yet `grid_charging_window` is classed contractual, and `end_soc` is an internal planning convention.

**Status:** open — proposal: add a dated DSO temporary operating notice that forbids battery export above 25 MW
(possibly a protection trip with a penalty) and an import-tariff or flexible-connection addendum carrying the
00:00–06:00 window. Reclassify `end_soc` as business policy (a weekly handover target) or justify it as an O&M
contract term. Making the stale-cap error cost money rather than validity matters only once F1 is settled.

**Update (rule seeds):** still open. The export cap is now below the battery's 20 MW (12–18 MW), so `export_cap`
also limits evening sales, not only the stale 30 MW belief; the controller still clips solar above it. Hard's
notices are stated by the client, not given as documents. Rule classes are unchanged.

### F8 — A European site that the ko/ja/zh texts did not place abroad (low, confirmed)

Numbers agree across the five locales, but the scenario is European (EUR, a Spanish site name, the EU
last-Sunday-of-March clock change) while the request never said where the site is, and Korea, Japan and China
have no summer time. The site was 야노베르데 / リャノ・ベルデ in ko and ja but translated as 绿原 in zh, the ko
price column `전일가격_EUR_MWh` reads as "previous-day price", and the request said "the farm next door" while the
agreement describes one co-located, jointly metered site.

**Status:** fixed in this change — the request (and its copy in each requirements.md) now says the battery is at
the client's site in Spain and the solar farm is co-located (같은 부지의 / 併設の / 同站 / del mismo emplazamiento),
in all five locales; the agreement names the site with "(Spain)" in each language; zh transliterates the name as
利亚诺韦尔德; the ko column is `하루전가격_EUR_MWh` (data and requirements).

### F9 — DST trap depended on the file name; idealized solar (low, partly)

`deliverable_spec` said "(only clock times that exist locally)". The spec is shown only to the extractor, which runs
when the agent's file is not named `dispatch.csv`; so a 672-row plan named `dispatch.csv` was fatal while the same
plan under another name was handed to an extractor told to drop non-existent times. The verifier notes the
"quietly fixed" outcome is untested (dropping those rows changes the SOC path). Solar also reaches the full 30 MWp
DC rating (18 quarters at exactly 30 MW) with solar noon at 12:45 local, early for a Spanish CET site (about 13:20).

**Status:** fixed in this change — the hint is removed from `deliverable_spec`, so the extractor gets no DST
guidance and the clock change is scored the same way however the file is named; requirements.md still states it
for the client. The solar part stays open — proposal: a DC/AC ratio (e.g. 30 MWp DC, 26 MW AC inverter limit) and
sun timing that matches a site in Spain now that the request names the country.

**Baseline:** the battery rests at 50% all week (solar alone): 0.00 EUR vs. the reference's 18,084.35 EUR at seed
20270322; at world seeds 1–3, 0.00 vs. 18,742.76 / 19,265.16 / 20,542.92 EUR. Valid everywhere; with a zero
baseline the normalized quality equals the plain ratio to the reference.

**Baseline (rule seeds):** unchanged and valid in every setting: 0.00 EUR against the reference's 5,816.98 (hard/s334) to
25,300.79 EUR (easy/s141) at world seed 20270322 (README has every setting and world seed). No end target is above
the 50% start, every SOC window holds 50%, and the export, import, reserve, DSO and outage rules bite only when the
battery moves.

## Rule seeds

The case had one fixed problem (`setting.json` was `{}`). It now declares three tiers and twelve parameters drawn
per rule seed, with a public panel of three seeds (141, 70, 334):

- **easy:** the SOC window, power, per-direction efficiency, end-of-week SOC, the amended export cap and the clock
  change. The client says the import window, the reserve and degradation are left out of this first plan.
- **medium:** adds the import window, the evening reserve (share and hours) and a degradation cost.
- **hard:** adds the dated notices and the trading desk's push (F4).

Parameters (pool in medium and hard; easy uses the first four): `soc_window` 10-90/15-85/5-95, `efficiency`
92/92/95/89/90/94, `end_soc` 50/40/30, `export_cap` 18/15/12, `import_window` 00-06/01-05/02-08, `reserve_pct`
20/30/15, `reserve_window` 17-21/18-22/16-20, `degradation` 5/15/25. Hard only: `works_end`, `dso_day`, `dso_mw`
10/5/8 and `outage_day`. A tier that does not use a parameter has one placeholder candidate (`any`, `none`, 0).
`derive_params` adds the numbers the texts state (MWh, decimals with a decimal comma in es, window times), and
`admissible` rejects a reserve at or below the warranty floor.

- **Panel.** The panel was searched so that every candidate of every parameter appears once per tier. Its first
  seed's hard setting binds every parameter on world seeds 20270322 and 1–3. Nine parameters make the reference
  invalid under another candidate; efficiency and end SOC let another reference beat it by 1.7–5.1%; degradation
  moves its objective by 38–84%.
- **Robustness.** Over 1,746 sampled combinations and world seeds (20270322 and 1–5), the reference was always
  valid with a positive profit, and every rule had an example that breaks only that rule. Over 236 random hard
  combinations and world seeds, each parameter bound in at least 91% of them.
- **Rules.** `reserve_20pct` is renamed `evening_reserve`, since the share is now drawn. `dso_limit` (contractual)
  and `maintenance_outage` (physical) are new in hard. `grid_charging_window` and `evening_reserve` apply from medium.
- **Targets.** Thirteen targets, with tiers: `degradation` is split out of `profit_scoring`, and `works_end`,
  `dso_limit` and `maintenance_outage` are new. All five locales carry the same placeholders and markers.
- **Code.** `score`, the reference, the baseline and the rule examples read the setting through `rules_of(setting)`.
  The scorer, the export limit per quarter (amended cap, 30 MW after the works end, the DSO's limit) and the SOC
  simulation are those of the fixed case. Under last week's values with the last-week switch, the planner
  reproduces the old reference exactly (18,084.35 EUR).
- **Data.** The data and request are byte-identical to the case before rule seeds, and the agent view is the same
  in every setting.
- **Rule examples.** The SOC, reserve and end-of-week examples are built from a SOC deficit or surplus that is paid
  back, so they hold under every draw.
- **Check of the conversion (fixed).** The requirements still said the export limit was cut to the drawn
  12/15/18 MW "from 1 March to 30 June 2027". Last week's log (15–21 March) was planned at 25 MW and discharges
  20 MW with no solar in 58 quarters, so by the client's own dates it broke the limit, which an agent could see
  in the data. The requirements in all five locales now date the drawn limit from the current phase of the works
  (Monday 22 March until they end, planned for 30 June); the log stays last week's evidence and the data is
  unchanged.
