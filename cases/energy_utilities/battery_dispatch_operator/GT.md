# Ground truth: energy_utilities/battery_dispatch_operator

The ground truth (GT) of a setting in five parts, with the reference as supporting evidence. Generated by `gt_report.py` from `dataset/audit.json` (`fdebench2 audit-case`, public world seed 20270322): 45 settings, easy 15, medium 15, hard 15 (each tier's public panel plus admissible parameter combinations). Damage is 1 − credit, credit = 0 for an invalid plan, else the quality score clipped to [0, 1]; a target bites in a setting when misreading it alone loses at least 0.10 of the credit (for a drawn parameter: its least harmful wrong candidate).

The end level, import window and reserve do have the log's value among their candidates; reading them off the log is a guess right one time in three, which the ablations price.

**Grid import is a weak fact.** `grid_charging` bites in fewer than half of its settings, and the shortcut `solar_only` (never draw from the grid, every other fact right) stays open where it bites least: at the forecast prices a night charge pays for its losses and degradation only at 5 EUR/MWh or on cloudy days, and the free solar above the export limit fills the battery for the evening anyway. There the best plan imports little or nothing and no window changes it; all six medium and hard settings of round 2026-11 (degradation 15 or 25 EUR/MWh) are such settings (`solar_only` 0.61-1.00). The natural misreading of the agreement, import at any hour, does break the rule in every medium and hard setting probed. It is not a way around the work: it needs every other fact and is the safe answer to the one question it skips, which matters only where a night charge pays. `grid_charging` stays a target because the files contradict the rule (the agreement allows import at any hour) and a plan that follows them is invalid. `validate.OPEN_AUDIT_FINDINGS` lists the shortcut with this reason.

## 2. Validity checks (hard gates)

A plan that breaks any of these is invalid and earns no credit. A file that cannot be read (no `dispatch.csv`, no charge or discharge column, a number that is not one, a quarter given twice) is fatal. The format is otherwise open: the time column under any common name (else the first column), the charge and discharge columns by a word in any of the five languages, comma or semicolon separated with decimal commas, and timestamps with seconds, a `T`, a one-digit hour, a day-first (or month-first) date or a UTC offset (converted to local time).

## 3. Objective (higher is better)

The battery's expected profit in EUR over the week: every quarter settled at its hour's forecast price (the client's best estimate; the actual prices differ, up or down). Per quarter, site net = solar + discharge − charge; export earns price x 0.25 x min(net, the quarter's export limit), import costs price x 0.25 x the import; less what the solar farm alone would have earned (price x 0.25 x min(solar, export limit)) and, in medium and hard, the degradation cost per MWh of charge plus discharge. The export limit is the amended cap, 30 MW from the works' end and the DSO's limit in its hours.

**The zero point** is the client's own planner (`baseline_solution`, requirements.md "How we run the battery today"), run with the setting's values: two cycles a day by price rank, the night fill leaving room for the midday solar, 1% (0.4 MWh) kept inside the warranty window and above the reserve, and no sale below the reserve until its hours are over (the morning sale included). It made last week's log (with last week's settings). The quality scale (reference − planner) / reference is 0.071–0.267 wide over the audit's settings (5% is the validator's floor).

**Uncertainty.** Until the audit the plan was settled at one draw of the actual prices (the forecast plus a daily bias and hourly noise). For a fixed plan the profit is linear in the price and the noise has mean zero, so the forecast optimum was already the best plan in expectation and the draw added only luck: on the public settings of world seeds 20270322 and 1-3 the same plan's profit moved by up to 14% (the client's planner) and 7% (the optimum) between the forecast and the draw, on a quality scale 7-29% wide. The profit at the week's actual prices is still reported as a metric (`profit_at_actual_prices_eur`).

## 4. Reference and bounds

- **Reference** (`reference_solution`): the plan of greatest expected profit under the setting's rules, exactly (`optimum`). The problem is a linear program with one state, the stored energy; a quarter's profit is a concave piecewise-linear function of the energy it moves, so the best profit still to come is one too, and a backward pass merges each quarter's segments into it (a sup-convolution) and cuts it at the SOC bounds. Pure Python, about 0.05 s; it matches scipy/HiGHS on every public setting.
- **Baseline** (quality 0, `baseline_solution`): the client's planner (part 3).
- **Best known** (`best_known_solution`): the reference itself, which no valid plan beats.
- **Bound** (`objective_bound`, proven): the linear program's optimum before rounding. It allows every plan the rules allow and more (charging and discharging in one quarter), so no valid plan earns more; the reference's 4-decimal plan is within 0.1 EUR of it.

- **quality_cap** = 1.0: the best known quality is 1.000 (median 1.000) and the gap to the bound 0.000000–0.000002. Before the audit the reference was a greedy two-cycle rule (the planner with two refinements) and a forecast LP reached 1.03-1.30 of it.

**Value of asking** (the GT's claim that the client must be asked): per tier, the best plan that never asks against the reference of each setting. `never asks, knows the fixed facts` is the best of the measured settings' references and the baseline scored over the tier (each reference is a full guess of the drawn values, picked with hindsight); `never asks (robust)` is `robust_solution`: one linear program (scipy) over the tier's pools, valid under every combination: the SOC tracked under every candidate split and kept inside every candidate window and above every candidate reserve, the highest end level, the lowest export limit until every candidate's works have ended, the lowest DSO limit on every candidate day, rest through every candidate outage, import only where every candidate window allows; charging priced at its expected cost over the candidate limits, degradation at the pool's mean. It is a genuine attempt, checked against every plan that plans for a subset of each parameter's candidates (a single one is a guess, valid only where it is right; degradation at any value of the pool). In easy all 189 distinct such plans were scored over all 81 combinations: the full hedge earns the most, 0.186 (0.109 for the best single guessed setting), so asking is worth 0.81 over the whole pool. On the audit's 15 easy settings the hedge earns 0.089; planning for a 15-18 MW cap would earn 0.19 there but 0.154 over all 81, so the 0.91 below is the luck of the sample, not a weak solver. In medium and hard a coordinate search over the same choices from four starts, on 150 sampled combinations, found at most 0.075 against the hedge's 0.061 in medium (0.000 on the audit's settings) and 0.010 against 0.000 in hard; no single change earns more than 0.049 (medium) or 0.011 (hard) on the audit's settings. Without asking, no plan found beats the client's own planner by much: the hedges cost more than the optimizer gains.
