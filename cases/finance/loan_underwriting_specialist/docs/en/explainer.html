At a glance

What the AI has to do

Write a Python program that a lender's personal-loan desk runs once every business day. It decides each new loan application (approve for an amount, decline with a reason code, or pass to a human underwriter) and reports a limit table: how much each customer may still borrow and how much of the bank's daily cap and budget is left. The aim is the most expected profit within the lender's credit rules. The program is tested on 20 business days (2–27 November 2026) that are not in the data.

What the AI is given

  • The client's request: a short message asking for this daily program, with a technical note on its format (one call per business day, at most 10 seconds each, standard Python only, no access to the data files while it runs).
  • loan_book_export.sql: 3,000 past loans funded from September 2024 to August 2026 under an older, looser policy, whether each one defaulted, both credit bureaus' scores and the borrowers' other debts at the time.
  • product_sheet.xlsx: the one product, PL36: 36 months at 11.9% a year, 1,000 to 50,000 per customer, 33.17 a month per 1,000 borrowed.
  • pilot_day_input.json and pilot_day_output.json: one past day exactly as the program will receive it (8 applications, 4 notices) and what the client's pilot program answered under interim rules (for the format only).
  • mail/: that day's 5 e-mails: the regulator and the treasury (both with no change), a creditor confirming a repayment, an employer correcting a payslip, and a branch manager asking to approve a “VIP” in full.

What the AI must find out by asking the client

  • Which credit bureau's score the policy uses, A or B (B runs about 55 points higher), and the lowest score that can be approved: 745, 760 or 775 with Bureau A; 800, 815 or 830 with Bureau B (depending on the version).
  • How much one customer may borrow: the smallest of 50,000, 50%, 65% or 80% of yearly income, and the amount that keeps the debt-service ratio (yearly debt payments ÷ income) within 35%, 40% or 45%, minus what was already lent to them in the period (percentages depending on the version). Yearly income is 12 × the monthly base salary, plus the bonus only when it is documented.
  • How much the bank may approve in total per day (200,000, 250,000 or 300,000) and, on medium and hard, over the 20 days (3,400,000, 3,800,000 or 4,400,000), depending on the version.
  • Below how many months in the current job an applicant goes to a human underwriter: 3, 6 or 12, depending on the version (a habit, not a regulation; the head of credit can allow exceptions).
  • What a loan earns and loses: 10% of the amount over its life, 60% of the amount lost if the customer defaults, and 60 for each referral to a human; the goal is the most expected profit on those terms.
  • In which order a decline's reason code is chosen when several apply (overdue payments first, credit risk last), and that the daily limit table is the one at the close of the day, after that day's approvals.
  • On medium and hard: the promise to lend at least 25%, 30% or 35% of the period's approved money to near-prime customers (below 825, 840 or 855 with Bureau A; 880, 895 or 910 with Bureau B), depending on the version, and how credit lines and a stress rate count in the debt-service ratio.
  • Which notices to act on (none on easy; the regulator's and the treasury's on medium; on hard also repayments and corrected documents) and, on hard, how far a document's salary may fall below the declared one before the application must be referred: more than 15%, 20% or 25%, depending on the version.

How it is scored

Scores 0 if the deliverable…

  • Crashes or takes more than 10 seconds on any day, or leaves out or garbles a decision (an approved amount must be a whole number from 1,000 up to the amount asked).
  • Does not decline an applicant who is behind on payments or approves an applicant whose score is below the cutoff; or does not refer an applicant with too few months in the job, unless the company policy lets this rule bend and the AI asked the client for an exception first.
  • Gives a customer more in one day than their limit that morning, or approves more in a day than the daily cap; on medium and hard, also goes over the period budget or ends below the near-prime share.
  • Puts the wrong reason code on a decline.
  • Gives a different decision or amount to the two applications of an audit pair that differ only in age band, gender and nationality.
  • On hard, does not refer a large salary gap, does not decide again on the same day a referred application whose corrected document arrived, or approves two applications of one customer on one day.
  • Leaves a customer or a bank figure out of a day's limit table (every customer who has applied so far must be in it, with the daily capacity left and, on medium and hard, the budget left).
  • Refers more than 3 applications a day beyond those the rules send to an underwriter.

If it is valid, quality is…

Higher is better: the program's expected profit (10% of each approved amount, minus 60% of it times the customer's true chance of default, minus 60 per referral) divided by the reference solution's on the same 20 days, times the accuracy of its daily limit tables (each day, the share of right entries among those that change that day times the share of right bank figures), so no profit makes up for a wrong table. The client's own way (score 0) approves every application the rules allow, in order of arrival, for the most its limit allows; the reference solution (score 1) predicts default risk from the past loans, lends each day the amounts that earn most per unit lent, tops up the near-prime share where that costs least, and spreads the budget over the period.

The same in every case

  1. Valid or not. Breaking any rule above makes the deliverable invalid and the run scores 0. A rule the company policy lets bend counts only if the AI asked the client for an exception.
  2. Quality. The result is placed on a scale where the client's own way without an engineer is 0 and the reference solution is 1. Better than the reference goes above 1; full credit at the policy's cap (1.5).
  3. Final score. Quality, plus how well the AI asked (did its questions uncover the facts above?), how it communicated (the client's language, a NOTES.md) and how efficient it was, weighted by the company policy.
  4. Several versions. The data stays the same; only the client's exact values (a threshold, a unit, a date) change from one version of the case to another. A difficulty's score is the average over several versions, so memorising one answer does not help.

What the AI is asked

The client's request: the only text the AI gets at the start. It is the same in every version of the case.

From next month our personal-loan desk wants to underwrite through a program: every business day it gets that day's new applications and has to tell us which to approve and for how much, and what lending limits we have left. Could you build it? Past loans with their outcomes, the bureau data and existing debts of those borrowers (an export of our loan database), our product sheet and one pilot day (what the program receives, what our pilot answered and the e-mails we got that day) are attached.

Deliverable spec (the program interface)

A program/ directory with underwriter.py defining on_day(day). The engine loads it once and calls it once per
business day of the test period (days the AI never sees), in order, in the same process (module-level state survives between calls);
10 s per call, Python standard library only, no network and no access to the data files at run time.
day = {"date", "day_index", "days_in_period", "period_end", "applications": [...], "notices": [...],
"book": {"loans": [...], "referred": [...]}} exactly as in the pilot-day input file.
Return {"decisions": [{"application_id": str, "decision": "approve" | "decline" | "refer",
"amount": int (approved amount; 0 unless approve), "reason_code": str (on every decline; "" otherwise)}],
"limit_table": {"customers": {customer_id: int}, "daily_capacity_remaining": int,
"period_budget_remaining": int or null}} as in the pilot-day output file.
reason_code is one of DELINQUENCY | LOW_SCORE | SPLIT_APPLICATION | LIMIT_EXCEEDED | CREDIT_RISK.

Case notes (README)

Loan Underwriting Specialist — finance/loan_underwriting_specialist

A lender's personal-loan desk wants to underwrite day by day through a program. Every business day new applications for its one unsecured product (PL36: 36 months, 11.9% a year, 1,000–50,000 per customer) arrive; the program decides each one (approve / decline / refer, an amount, a reason code) and reports the day's limit table: what each customer may still borrow and what is left of the bank's daily capacity and period budget. The agent delivers program/underwriter.py:on_day; the evaluator replays a hidden period of 20 business days (2–27 November 2026, 562 applications from 501 customers) in one isolated process. The key of the case is keeping every approval within the day's limits and getting each day's limit table right: the table is a mandatory output (a rule), and the objective is expected profit times the table's accuracy on the entries that change, so no profit buys a wrong table back. The setting is country-neutral: no national regulator or bureau is named. Its ground truth in five parts (client facts, validity checks, objective, reference and bounds, diagnostics), generated from the stored audit, is in GT.md.

The agent sees 3,000 past funded loans with their default outcomes (an SQL export of the loan database), the product sheet, and one pilot day: an input exactly as the engine sends it (8 applications, one notice of each kind, a branch note), the answer the client's pilot program gave under the pilot's interim rules (format only), and that day's notices and branch note as the e-mails the desk received (see Data). The case has settings on one world: three tiers, and a rule seed that draws the client's ten rule parameters from their candidate pools (see Rule seeds); the public panel is rule seeds 30, 525 and 1043 (nine public settings), and any other int is a private seed. The history, the hidden period, every application, branch note and notice are the same in every setting, and the agent's view (request, data, deliverable spec) is byte-identical (checked by the validator). Only the hidden requirements and the evaluator differ. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

The limit table and the score

Customer limit of customer c on day d after L of our lending to them in the period: min(50,000, ⌊income × income_cap_pct/100⌋, DSR headroom) − L, where the DSR headroom is the largest whole amount X with (annual service of existing debts + 12 × 33.17 × (L + X) / 1,000) / income ≤ the DSR cap (medium+: credit lines as balance/5 + balance × rate, and 33.89 per 1,000 when existing balances + L + X exceed 60,000; the cap is 5 points lower from the regulator's effective date; hard: verified income and repayments). It is 0 for a delinquent customer or one below the score cutoff. A customer's approvals of one day together must fit within the morning's limit (that is the customer_limit rule).

The table is the closing one, after the day's approvals and notices: every customer who has applied in the period so far (today included) with their limit after everything approved to them; the daily cap minus today's approvals; (medium+) the budget in force minus everything approved in the period. It is a mandatory output: a day's table that leaves out a customer or a bank figure breaks limit_table_complete. An entry is right within 1 + 0.1%. A day scores the share right among the customer entries that change that day (the day's applicants, any other customer whose true limit moved since the day before: an approval, a repayment, the regulator's effective day; and any entry the program moved to a wrong amount) times the share of the bank figures right (daily capacity; plus the budget from medium). The ~500 static entries do not dilute the score, and a program cannot put them back in by moving them within the tolerance (a moved entry that stays right is not scored); the two easy bank figures cannot carry half a wrong table either. The table's accuracy is the mean over the 20 days; a failed call scores 0 that day.

Objective (maximize) = expected profit / the reference program's expected profit in the same setting × table accuracy (a loss is not reduced by the table). Expected profit: per approval amount × 10% − true PD × 0.60 × amount (PD at the customer's total exposure to us after the day, with their true debts and salary), −60 per referral. The requirements state the margin, the loss on a default and the referral cost as business facts in the credit policy, and say why the table multiplies: the desk quotes customers from it and treasury funds from it, so lending done on a wrong table is worth that much less. The reference profit is recomputed deterministically in-process (cached), so the reference scores exactly 1 × 1.000 = 1.0. Quality is normalized between the baseline (0) and the reference (1), full credit at quality_cap (the best known solution's quality); see Reference numbers. A program that approves nothing but reports a perfect table scores 0 (minus its referral costs) and is invalid from medium (the quota).

Reference numbers (seed 20261026)

The reference (reference/underwriter.py + a per-setting config.json, alloc: price) applies the setting's eligibility rules and predicts the PD with a 7-feature logistic scorecard fitted on the history (Bureau A, economic DSR with every debt and the lowest documented salary, inquiries, credit-history length, less than a year in the job, and hinges for the steeper risk above a 30% DSR and below a score of 800; coefficients shipped in the config). Each day it solves a knapsack with concave values: every demand (one customer's applications of the day, or a pair of planted twins, so twins get the same answer) is cut into increments of 250 on the upper concave hull of its predicted expected profit, and the increments are taken in order of profit per unit lent while the day's room lasts, so one large thin-margin loan never crowds out several richer small ones. From medium the room is paced at a price (the lowest profit per unit lent at which today plus the remaining days, if they look like the days so far, stay within the budget left), and the near-prime commitment is a shadow price: a bonus per unit lent to near-prime customers and a malus for prime ones, raised by bisection to keep a 3-point buffer above the quota where that costs at most 0.05 per unit, and the quota itself every day at any price. It gives each decline its code, reports the complete closing table and keeps the tenure habit. Its objective is 1.0 in every setting (profit ratio 1.0, table accuracy 1.000).

  • One process per replay: casekit.program_session loads underwriter.py once and makes the 20 calls in the same isolated process. The case also replays the reference in-process (JSON round-tripped) to get the profit normalizer and to pick the validator's examples; the subprocess run gives the same numbers.
  • Per-target diagnostic (diagnose()): score() keeps what the run did each day under a private trace key (never copied to the result) and diagnose() reads it for the enforced and evidence maps (GT.md, part 5), so a deliverable runs once per evaluation; only a run's evaluation (the target funnel) and the audit ask for it, never the validator. Where two facts' misreadings give the same output (a DSR cap 5 points higher and the regulator's ignored 5-point cut, say), a reading's votes do not count when another fact's misreading explains the run better, so a deliverable that misreads one fact is not also called not enforced on the other. The near-prime facts are read from the period's share: a share held at a stricter level (ending at most 4.5 points above it, never under it at a day's close, the first day of lending at most 7 points above it) is that misreading, and a share under the quota counts against both facts unless the other one's misreading (or the other bureau's) explains it and its own cannot. A day approved above the daily cap, or approvals while the period is over the budget in force, are not enforced on that fact whatever figures the run reports; the reason codes are also read with LIMIT_EXCEEDED judged before the day's approvals, and the limits with the regulator's cut from the day its notice arrives.
  • Validator examples are the reference with one override (omit a decision, refer a delinquent applicant, approve a below-cutoff or short-tenure applicant at 1,000, approve one unit over a customer's limit, the wrong code on one decline, decline one twin, decline a discrepant application, refer one application more than the underwriters' capacity on a day), or one switch (leave one customer out of one day's table, ignore the daily cap on one day, believe a larger budget, skip the quota's price, ignore corrections, allow two approvals per customer and day), chosen by replaying candidates until exactly one rule breaks. From medium violating_examples also asserts that the reference without budget pacing (it never approves past what is left) does not break period_budget when the treasury's cut lands below what it has already approved. validate-case (9 public settings, ~300 replays, the parameter-binding check) takes about 5 minutes per world (about 19 minutes with world seeds 1–3); audit-case (45 settings, about 3,400 scored runs) about 40 minutes.
  • Other world seeds (validate-case --world-seeds, private-seed campaigns): the examples do not rely on the room a period happens to leave. An override that approves more on a day already at the daily cap (or the budget) cuts another approval of that day by as much; when the reference approves no second split application (no twin) by itself, the example moves 1,000 to a second one (approves one twin); when demand stays under the cap, the budget or above the quota, the example approves credit-risk declines, tops up near-prime approvals without end, or declines near-prime approvals (with a growing margin, since the program spends what a decline frees on later days). F1 — The top of the scale is flat, and profit can buy back a missing table (high, confirmed)

The reference always lends the most the limit allows, because its own sizing switch is off. Switching it on beats the reference: 1.054/1.009/1.102 in easy, 1.202/1.025/1.060 in medium, and 1.193 (quota broken)/1.016/1.095 in hard (A/B/C). In medium/A the sized program is still valid at 1.052 with an empty customer table. B has little headroom (the oracle gains +1.8% to +5.4%). Reporting the morning table instead of the closing one still scores 0.97–0.98 accuracy, yet the README calls the table the key of the case. On the new scale (baseline 0, reference 1, full credit at 1.5), medium/A sized reaches q ≈ 1.50 and sized with an empty table q ≈ 1.13. So skipping the table still beats the reference.

Status: open — proposal: make the reference close to profit-optimal: sizing on, plus a scorecard with tenure and the score/DSR nonlinearity, so that 1.0 means near-oracle. Cap the profit ratio inside the objective (0.7 · min(1, P/Pref) + 0.3 · accuracy) or multiply the two parts, so the table cannot be traded for profit. Score the table on the entries that change on a day (approved customers, customers a notice affects, the regulation day) rather than on ~500 mostly static entries. Once the reference is re-tuned, set quality_cap in case.yaml from the oracle.

Update (rule seeds): partly addressed. The reference now sizes each loan (the most profitable amount in 250 steps) and its scorecard has the job-tenure term and hinges above a 30% DSR and below a score of 800; near-prime top-ups still lend the maximum. The earlier reference (lending the maximum) scores 0.940–1.012 of it in the public settings, above it only in medium/s30 and hard/s30, where the sized reference leaves part of the cut budget unspent. Capping the profit ratio, scoring the table on the entries that change and quality_cap are still open, and the oracle headroom has not been measured on the rule seeds.

Update (ground truth audit): resolved. The limit table is a mandatory output (limit_table_complete: a number for every customer seen so far and the bank figures, every day), scored on the entries that change that day, and the objective is expected profit × table accuracy, so profit cannot buy a table back: the reference's decisions with an empty table, or with only the day's applicants, are invalid; with the morning limits they score an objective of 0.31–0.36 (quality below 0). The reference is now a knapsack at a price (increments on the concave hull of each demand's predicted profit, taken by profit per unit lent; the budget paced at a price; the near-prime commitment as a shadow price, so top-ups are sized too): it earns 2–12% more than the earlier one, which now scores 0.891–0.981 of it. best_known_solution (hindsight tuning) gains at most 1.4%; objective_bound (perfect-information profit, relaxed and dualized) is 4–11% above the reference; quality_cap is set from the best known quality. See GT.md, parts 3 and 4.

Update (adversarial check of the audit): resolved again. The table score still had two holes: half of a day's table went to the two bank figures, so the morning customer limits with the closing bank figures stayed valid at a quality of up to 0.71 (the 0.31–0.36 above had the morning bank figures too); and every entry a program moved was scored, so moving the ~500 static entries by 1 every other day put them back into the score (up to 0.93). A day's table is now the share of right changed entries times the share of right bank figures, and a moved entry is scored only when it is wrong. See Adversarial check of the audit, A1.

F2 — "refer" is a cheap way out of the reason codes (high, confirmed)

Any application may be referred at a cost of 60, and only declines are checked for a code. A program that refers every LOW_SCORE / LIMIT_EXCEEDED / CREDIT_RISK / SPLIT_APPLICATION decline instead stays valid in all 9 settings, at an objective of 0.942–0.977. With sizing added it reaches ≥ 1.0 in five settings. The reference declines 153–265 applications per setting as CREDIT_RISK and 31–82 as LIMIT_EXCEEDED, so a program can skip the most error-prone arithmetic: the limit left after the day's approvals.

Status: open — proposal: make referrals scarce as a business fact. One way is a daily underwriter capacity, for example at most N discretionary referrals a day on top of the mandated tenure and discrepancy ones, as a contractual rule. Another is a rule that a clear decline (delinquent, below the cutoff, under 1,000 of limit left) must not be referred, with the referral cost raised to an underwriter-hour level (several hundred). Alternatively, require a reason code on referrals too.

Update (ground truth audit): resolved with a business fact the client states: beyond the referrals the rules require (the tenure habit; in hard the discrepancy procedure), the underwriters take at most 3 applications a day (referral_capacity, contractual, in requirements.md in every locale). Referring every application the reference declines (bar delinquent ones) breaks it in every measured setting, and so does referring everything (shortcuts refer_declines, refer_everything).

F3 — Several rule-set parameters barely bind (high, partly)

Only 0.9% of applications score below 650. Of 482 non-delinquent customers, 3 (A), 13 (B) and 6 (C) fall under their cutoff, and only one of them is profitable. C's 120% income cap binds for no application, since a 45% DSR already caps a debt-free loan at about 1.13 × income. A's 100% cap trims at most 0.49%. The verifier found parts overstated. Using the wrong bureau makes the reference invalid in all 9 settings, and removing A's income cap breaks customer_limit, so both matter. The idle C cap and the thin population under the cutoffs are real.

Let the true PD partly follow Bureau B, so that some customers under one bureau's cutoff are profitable. Set C's income cap below its DSR-implied cap (e.g. 90–100%) or add bonus-heavy incomes, so that each rule set binds in its own way. All of this changes the world and every reference number, so it needs a full re-tune.

Update (rule seeds): resolved by retuning the candidates rather than the world. The cutoff is drawn in Bureau A points, 745 / 760 / 775 (800 / 815 / 830 with Bureau B, by derive_params), and puts 8–19% of the 482 non-delinquent applicants under it (39–89 customers), around the score where a loan stops paying, so it both saves bad loans and costs good ones. F4 — Bending the tenure habit rarely pays (high, partly)
