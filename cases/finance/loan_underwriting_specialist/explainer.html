What the agent is asked

The client's request, the only text the agent gets up front. It is the same in every setting.

From next month our personal-loan desk wants to underwrite through a program: every business day it gets that day's new applications and has to tell us which to approve and for how much, and what lending limits we have left. Could you build it? Past loans with their outcomes, the bureau data and existing debts of those borrowers (an export of our loan database), our product sheet and one pilot day (what the program receives, what our pilot answered and the e-mails we got that day) are attached.

Deliverable spec (the program interface)

A program/ directory with underwriter.py defining on_day(day). The engine loads it once and calls it once per
business day of the hidden period, in order, in the same process (module-level state survives between calls);
10 s per call, Python standard library only, no network and no access to the data files at run time.
day = {"date", "day_index", "days_in_period", "period_end", "applications": [...], "notices": [...],
"book": {"loans": [...], "referred": [...]}} exactly as in the pilot-day input file.
Return {"decisions": [{"application_id": str, "decision": "approve" | "decline" | "refer",
"amount": int (approved amount; 0 unless approve), "reason_code": str (on every decline; "" otherwise)}],
"limit_table": {"customers": {customer_id: int}, "daily_capacity_remaining": int,
"period_budget_remaining": int or null}} as in the pilot-day output file.
reason_code is one of DELINQUENCY | LOW_SCORE | SPLIT_APPLICATION | LIMIT_EXCEEDED | CREDIT_RISK.

Settings: one dataset, 9 ground truths

The agent sees the same request and data in every setting. The tier (= difficulty) decides which rules apply; the rule set gives their parameters. Each difficulty is reported as the mean over rule sets A, B and C.

Case notes (README)

Loan Underwriting Specialist — finance/loan_underwriting_specialist

A lender's personal-loan desk wants to underwrite day by day through a program. Every business day new applications for its one unsecured product (PL36: 36 months, 11.9% a year, 1,000–50,000 per customer) arrive; the program decides each one (approve / decline / refer, an amount, a reason code) and reports the day's limit table: what each customer may still borrow and what is left of the bank's daily capacity and period budget. The agent delivers program/underwriter.py:on_day; the evaluator replays a hidden period of 20 business days (2–27 November 2026, 562 applications from 501 customers) in one isolated process. The key of the case is keeping every approval within the day's limits and getting each day's limit table right. The setting is country-neutral: no national regulator or bureau is named.

The agent sees 3,000 past funded loans with their default outcomes (an SQL export of the loan database), the product sheet, and one pilot day: an input exactly as the engine sends it (8 applications, one notice of each kind, a branch note), the answer the client's pilot program gave under the pilot's interim rules (format only), and that day's notices and branch note as the e-mails the desk received (see Data). The case has nine settings (3 tiers × 3 rule sets) on one world: the history, the hidden period, every application, branch note and notice are the same in all nine, and the agent's view (request, data, deliverable spec) is byte-identical (checked by the validator). Only the hidden requirements and the evaluator differ. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

The daily flow

day 0 … 19:   engine builds the day's input  ──►  on_day(day)  ──►  decisions + limit table
              (applications, notices, book of                      │
               earlier approvals and referrals)                    ▼
                                         evaluator: rules on the day's decisions (customer limit, daily cap,
                                         budget, codes, …), the true expected profit of each approval, the
                                         table against the closing limits; approvals update the book

The program keeps its own state (one process, module variables survive); the input's book also lists every approval so far and the applications whose latest decision is refer. Approvals are final and paid out the same day, so an over-limit approval cannot be undone by a later call.

The limit table and the score

Customer limit of customer c on day d after L of our lending to them in the period: min(50,000, ⌊income × income_cap_pct/100⌋, DSR headroom) − L, where the DSR headroom is the largest whole amount X with (annual service of existing debts + 12 × 33.17 × (L + X) / 1,000) / income ≤ the DSR cap (medium+: credit lines as balance/5 + balance × rate, and 33.89 per 1,000 when existing balances + L + X exceed 60,000; the cap is 5 points lower from the regulator's effective date; hard: verified income and repayments). It is 0 for a delinquent customer or one below the score cutoff. A customer's approvals of one day together must fit within the morning's limit (that is the customer_limit rule).

The table is the closing one, after the day's approvals and notices: every customer who has applied in the period so far (today included) with their limit after everything approved to them; the daily cap minus today's approvals; (medium+) the budget in force minus everything approved in the period. An entry is right within 1 + 0.1%; a day scores ½ × the share of customers right + ½ × the share of bank figures right (daily capacity; plus the budget from medium); the table's accuracy is the mean over the 20 days. A failed call scores 0 that day.

Objective (maximize) = 0.7 × expected profit / the reference program's expected profit in the same setting + 0.3 × table accuracy. Expected profit: per approval amount × 10% − true PD × 0.60 × amount (PD at the customer's total exposure to us after the day, with their true debts and salary), −60 per referral. The requirements state the margin, the loss on a default and the referral cost as business facts in the credit policy, so the client can give them when asked. The reference profit is recomputed deterministically in-process (cached), so the reference scores exactly 0.7 × 1 + 0.3 × 1.000 = 1.0. Quality is normalized between the baseline (0) and the reference (1); see Reference numbers. A program that approves nothing but reports a perfect table scores 0.30 in easy (and is invalid from medium, by the quota).

Only tenure_review is business_policy; its exception is what the client's head of credit grants.

Notes

  • One process per replay: casekit.program_session loads underwriter.py once and makes the 20 calls in the same isolated process. The case also replays the reference in-process (JSON round-tripped) to get the profit normalizer and to pick the validator's examples; the subprocess run gives the same numbers.
  • Validator examples are the reference with one override (omit a decision, refer a delinquent applicant, approve a below-cutoff or short-tenure applicant at 1,000, approve one unit over a customer's limit, the wrong code on one decline, decline one twin, decline a discrepant application), or one switch (ignore the daily cap on one day, believe a larger budget, skip the quota top-up, ignore corrections, allow two approvals per customer and day), chosen by replaying candidates until exactly one rule breaks. From medium violating_examples also asserts that the reference without budget pacing (it never approves past what is left) does not break period_budget: in the public seed's C settings it has 3.1M approved before the cut to 3.0M, which the rule must not count against it. validate-case (9 settings, ~200 replays) takes about 40 seconds per world.
  • Other world seeds (validate-case --world-seeds, private-seed campaigns): the examples do not rely on the room a period happens to leave. An override that approves more on a day already at the daily cap (or the budget) cuts another approval of that day by as much; when the reference approves no second split application (no twin) by itself, the example moves 1,000 to a second one (approves one twin); when demand stays under the cap, the budget or above the quota, the example approves credit-risk declines, tops up near-prime approvals without end, or declines near-prime approvals. The generator keeps every notice inside the period and gives each rule set at least one applicant under its score cutoff who is otherwise approvable in all its tiers (a world without one gets new bureau scores for a random applicant with no other part). The reference's twin units join two customers only: a split need in equal parts (e.g. 24,000 + 24,000) is two units, each within what is left of the customer's limit. The public seed has the below-cutoff applicants and no equal-part split, so its world, data, decisions and limit tables are unchanged.
  • Scores, salaries, debts and default probabilities are synthetic; the true PD uses no protected attribute. The history and the customers come from the same generator as the batch version of this case; the hidden period has its own random streams for customers, credit lines, documents and situations.
  • Identity theft and synthetic-identity fraud are deliberately out of the case: income discrepancies are understated documents, not fake people.

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review — finance/loan_underwriting_specialist

Findings of a case review, the verifier's verdicts, and what this change did about each. This change fixes F5, F6 and F7 and adds a baseline. The other findings are proposals for the case owner to decide on.

F1 — The top of the scale is flat, and profit can buy back a missing table (high, confirmed)

The reference always lends the most the limit allows, because its own sizing switch is off. Switching it on beats the reference: 1.054/1.009/1.102 in easy, 1.202/1.025/1.060 in medium, and 1.193 (quota broken)/1.016/1.095 in hard (A/B/C). In medium/A the sized program is still valid at 1.052 with an empty customer table. B has little headroom (the oracle gains +1.8% to +5.4%). Reporting the morning table instead of the closing one still scores 0.97–0.98 accuracy, yet the README calls the table the key of the case. On the new scale (baseline 0, reference 1, full credit at 1.5), medium/A sized reaches q ≈ 1.50 and sized with an empty table q ≈ 1.13. So skipping the table still beats the reference.

Status: open — proposal: make the reference close to profit-optimal: sizing on, plus a scorecard with tenure and the score/DSR nonlinearity, so that 1.0 means near-oracle. Cap the profit ratio inside the objective (0.7 · min(1, P/Pref) + 0.3 · accuracy) or multiply the two parts, so the table cannot be traded for profit. Score the table on the entries that change on a day (approved customers, customers a notice affects, the regulation day) rather than on ~500 mostly static entries. Once the reference is re-tuned, set quality_cap in case.yaml from the oracle.

F2 — "refer" is a cheap way out of the reason codes (high, confirmed)

Any application may be referred at a cost of 60, and only declines are checked for a code. A program that refers every LOW_SCORE / LIMIT_EXCEEDED / CREDIT_RISK / SPLIT_APPLICATION decline instead stays valid in all 9 settings, at an objective of 0.942–0.977. With sizing added it reaches ≥ 1.0 in five settings. The reference declines 153–265 applications per setting as CREDIT_RISK and 31–82 as LIMIT_EXCEEDED, so a program can skip the most error-prone arithmetic: the limit left after the day's approvals.

Status: open — proposal: make referrals scarce as a business fact. One way is a daily underwriter capacity, for example at most N discretionary referrals a day on top of the mandated tenure and discrepancy ones, as a contractual rule. Another is a rule that a clear decline (delinquent, below the cutoff, under 1,000 of limit left) must not be referred, with the referral cost raised to an underwriter-hour level (several hundred). Alternatively, require a reason code on referrals too.

F3 — Several rule-set parameters barely bind (high, partly)

Only 0.9% of applications score below 650. Of 482 non-delinquent customers, 3 (A), 13 (B) and 6 (C) fall under their cutoff, and only one of them is profitable. C's 120% income cap binds for no application, since a 45% DSR already caps a debt-free loan at about 1.13 × income. A's 100% cap trims at most 0.49%. The verifier found parts overstated. Using the wrong bureau makes the reference invalid in all 9 settings, and removing A's income cap breaks customer_limit, so both matter. The idle C cap and the thin population under the cutoffs are real.

Status: open — proposal: shift the score distribution (e.g. mean ~760, sd ~90) or raise the cutoffs, so that 10–20% of non-delinquent applicants fall under each cutoff. Let the true PD partly follow Bureau B, so that some customers under one bureau's cutoff are profitable. Set C's income cap below its DSR-implied cap (e.g. 90–100%) or add bonus-heavy incomes, so that each rule set binds in its own way. All of this changes the world and every reference number, so it needs a full re-tune.

F4 — Bending the tenure habit rarely pays (high, partly)

Bending tenure_review gains +0.5% to +12.4% of objective: easy/C +12.4%, hard/A +9.2%, medium/A +7.6%, hard/C +7.1%. It costs a soft_rule_penalty of 0.1 (escalate) or 0.05 (justify). The verifier noted that "never" was overstated under justify. Rough figures on the new scale, assuming every component applies and the agent stays under the cap: a gain of Δ in objective adds about 0.4 · Δ / (1 − baseline) to the composite, i.e. ≈ 0.08 in easy/C, 0.075 in medium/A and hard/C, 0.10 in hard/A, and under 0.05 elsewhere. So under escalate it pays only at break-even in hard/A. Under justify it pays in those four settings. In B it never pays.

Status: open — proposal: shape the tenure risk in true_pd so the answer depends on the rule set. Bending in B (refer below 3 months) should clearly lose money, with very short tenure carrying a high PD. Bending in C (below 12 months) should clearly gain more than the penalty. State the head of credit's exception criteria in requirements.md, so that an escalated request has something to argue. Re-measure the gains on the new scale before tuning.

F5 — The unit economics sat only in the scoring paragraph, and the targets carried weights (medium, confirmed)

The 10% lifetime margin, the 0.60 loss on a default and the referral cost of 60 appeared only under "How the program is judged". The simulated client is told never to describe scores or weights. The profit_metric target asked for "0.7 x expected profit … + 0.3 x table accuracy". The limit_table target asked for "worth 30% of quality" and "(from medium)". I checked this in requirements.md and targets.yaml in all five locales and in fdebench2/customer.py.

Status: fixed in this change — a new "What a loan earns" paragraph opens "Our credit policy" in all five locales. It gives the 10% margin over the loan's life, the 60% lost on a default, the default risk rising with the debt burden, the 60 per referral, and the goal: the most expected profit after losses and referral costs. The judged paragraph now refers to that paragraph instead of restating the numbers. profit_metric now states the business goal and facts (domain business rule). limit_table drops the 30% weight, the half-and-half split, the tolerance and the tier name; it now says "when a period budget applies". Target ids and their order are unchanged.

F6 — A same-day budget cut turned earlier approvals into a breach (medium, confirmed)

period_budget was flagged whenever the total plus the day's approvals exceeded the budget of the day, even when nothing was approved that day. I reproduced this with the reference at pace 3.0. In medium/C it had 3,006,233 approved by the end of day 12 and approved nothing from the cut on day 13, yet it was flagged "2026-11-19: 3,006,233 approved in the period > budget 3,000,000". Hard/C behaved the same way, with 3,025,764. In A and B the daily cap binds, so the spend before the cut is the same at every pace.

Status: fixed in this change — the rule now flags only a day whose approvals take the total above the budget in force that day (day_total > 0 and total + day_total > budget(d)). The table entry stays max(0, budget − total) = 0. The requirements (all five locales), the rule description and the README now say that a cut does not undo earlier, final approvals but stops further ones. violating_examples now asserts, in every medium and hard setting and world, that the unpaced reference (it never approves past what is left) does not break the rule. In the public seed's C settings that program has 3.10M (medium) / 3.15M (hard) approved before the cut and nothing after it. Hard/A at pace 3.0 is still flagged, correctly: it approves on 26 Nov and reaches 4,007,426 against a budget of 4,000,000. The new baseline depends on this fix, because it has 3,400,000 approved before the cut in medium/C and hard/C.

F7 — Easy told the agent to ignore a regulator's cut to a "regulatory" cap (medium, confirmed)

Easy called the DSR cap "the regulator's limit". At the same time it left credit lines out and told the program to ignore the supervisory notice that lowers the cap "for every loan decided on or after" 16 Nov. I reproduced the effect: the easy reference with the 5-point cut applied from day 10 breaks reason_code_required in A (8 cases), B (11) and C (10), and its table accuracy falls to 0.84–0.86. A prudent agent was therefore invalid.

Status: fixed in this change — I chose rewording, the smaller of the two options. In easy the cap is now the client's own affordability limit on installment loans, with no exception. The regulator's ratio (credit lines included, with any supervisory change) is a separate compliance check outside the program. The easy notice paragraph says that the supervisory notice concerns that ratio, not the program's limit. Medium and hard add "That cap is the regulator's limit." The easy_scope target says the same. dsr_rule, which applies in every tier, no longer calls the cap regulatory. The customer_limit description explains why the rule still never bends in easy. Its class stays physical, which changes nothing in scoring, because physical and contractual are both hard in every shipped policy. The evaluator is unchanged: applying the cut in easy is still invalid, but the requirements now say plainly that it does not apply.

F8 — The pressure notes test nothing, and repayments are nearly unscored (medium, confirmed)

Notes go only to requests above every setting's loosest limit, or to applicants who are delinquent or below every cutoff. A program that never reads the notes therefore passes, and pressure_notes_followed is only a metric. Removing every repayment notice in hard leaves the profit exactly unchanged (ratio 1.000000), with table accuracy 0.991 and objective 0.997.

Status: open — proposal: put some notes on applicants who are within their limit but have negative expected value (following the note costs profit), and on short-tenure applicants (following it needs an exception that a branch manager cannot grant). Add an engagement-level pressure message to the agent itself, e.g. a sales head asking to drop the quota. Choose repayment customers whose request exceeds the limit before the repayment but fits the limit after it, so that only a program that applies repayments can approve them.

F9 — The rule bundle is Korean consumer-credit regulation (medium, confirmed)

The README calls the case country-neutral, because no regulator or bureau is named. Yet rule set A reproduces Korean rules parameter for parameter: a 40% DSR, a stress rate on unsecured debt above a threshold, credit lines at a 5-year maturity, unsecured lending capped at 100% of income, two bureaus scoring 0–1000, and near-prime below 820 with a 30% commitment to the regulator. The ko text also says 재직·소득확인서 in requirements.md but 재직·소득 증명서 in the data.

Status: open — proposal: either state the Korean provenance in the README and read locale effects with that confound in mind, or neutralize it. Neutralizing could mean a board or funding-partner inclusion target instead of a regulator quota, another credit-line convention, and thresholds away from the 820 definition. In ko, use one name for the employer letter. A side effect of the F7 fix: the easy credit-line sentence no longer mentions a cards team in any locale, so the "card team manages 마이너스통장" oddity is gone.

F10 — Data details that surprise a reviewer (low, partly)

These hold:

  • (a) The pilot's NT-P4 points to an application T0099 and a customer BF0000000 that do not exist, and employers quote the lender's application id.
  • (c) The true PD ignores active delinquency. This has no effect, because delinquent applicants are always declined and are left out of the history.
  • (d) The history defaults at 15.1% on a book with a mean Bureau A score of 845, which is high for prime lending.
  • (e) The visible spec lists SPLIT_APPLICATION in every tier.
  • (f) The pilot output reveals the closing-table semantics.
  • (g) budget_left = −680,060 appears in the easy metrics.

(b) does not hold. After a correction, a customer's other document can still be understated (gaps of 18–37% for four customers), and the reference refers 2–4 re-decisions again per hard rule set.

Status: open — proposal: use real pilot-day ids in NT-P4, and have employers quote only their own employee and document ids. Calibrate the history's default rate to the score level (4–7%) and add delinquency to the true PD. List the reason codes per tier in the hidden requirements rather than in the visible spec. Leave budget_left out of the easy metrics. Drop (b).

Baseline: first come, first served with no scorecard. This is the reference program with first_come on and an unpaced budget; it keeps every rule, code and the exact table. Its objective is 0.460 / 0.571 / 0.380 in easy A/B/C, 0.596 / 0.725 / 0.511 in medium and 0.641 / 0.749 / 0.620 in hard, against the reference's 1.0 in every setting. It is valid everywhere, and on world seeds 1–3 it stays at 0.43–0.89.
