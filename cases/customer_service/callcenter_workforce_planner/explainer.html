Case notes (README)

Call-Center Workforce Planner — customer_service/callcenter_workforce_planner

A multilingual customer-support center asks the agent for next week's roster (Monday 9 to Sunday 15 November 2026) for its support team: "enough people on the phones so customers aren't left waiting, in every language we support". The center takes calls in Korean, Japanese, English, Chinese and Spanish, 08:00–22:00 every day. The deliverable is roster.json ({"assignments": [{"agent", "day", "shift", "language"}, ...]}), one entry per agent per shift worked. The setting is country-neutral: no national labor law applies beyond the rules the client states, and the hourly rates carry no currency.

The case has no settings: the same problem, rules and ground truth at every difficulty. The engagement knobs (full spec vs. clarify first, client persona, data noise, events) apply on top, as for every case (see cases/README.md).

Data

File (en)Content
agents.xlsxSheet staff: 30 agents with contract (20 full-time, 10 part-time) and new_hire (4, all full-time). Sheet skills: one row per agent and language (the first row is the agent's main language)
call_forecast.csvagents_needed per day, half-hour slot (08:00–21:30) and language: 980 rows, 1–5 agents each, already converted from calls to people
leave.csv5 leave requests, from / to written day/month/year, status approved or rejected
shifts.csvFour 8-hour full-time shifts (S0800, S1000, S1200, S1400) and four 4-hour part-time shifts (P0800, P1200, P1600, P1800)
labor_cost.csvHourly rate per contract: 22.0 full-time, 18.0 part-time

File, sheet and column names and the categorical labels are localized (a Korean client sends 고객센터_상담원명단.xlsx with 고용형태 = 풀타임); IDs, codes and numbers are not.

Rules

RuleClassWhat it says
skill_validphysicalAn agent takes calls only in a language among their skills (one language for the whole shift)
min_restphysicalAt least 11 hours between two shifts of the same agent (so at most one a day)
shift_typecontractualFull-timers only 8-hour shifts, part-timers only 4-hour shifts
max_shiftscontractualAt most 5 shifts a week for a full-timer, 3 for a part-timer
leave_respectedcontractualNobody on a day of approved leave
buddy_rulebusiness policy (bendable)While a new hire is on the phones, at least one non-new agent is on the same language in every slot

Objective (minimize): 40 per missing agent per half-hour slot and language (the forecast, with Wednesday at 60% rounded up, minus the agents assigned) plus the labor cost of all shifts (hours × rate, a full-timer's 5th shift of the week at 1.5×). The client explains the 40 as its estimate, in the same units as the rates, of what one agent missing for half an hour costs in abandoned calls, callbacks and missed service levels.

Hidden facts (9 clarification targets)

one_language (a bilingual agent counts for one language only), slot_coverage (the forecast is already in people, checked per half-hour slot), holiday_wednesday (11 November is a regional public holiday in the client's main market: 60% of the forecast, rounded up; the file was not updated), contract_limits, min_rest, leave_dates (day/month/year, both ends included, rejected requests are not leave), overtime (4 regular shifts, the 5th at 1.5×), buddy_rule, understaffing_penalty (the 40 and the cost model).

Situations present

  • Hidden facts that decide validity: the rest rule (an optimizer that does not know it produces 13 min_rest violations at this seed), the contract shift types and caps, one language per shift, and the leave file (read as month/day, A20's day off on Monday 9 November becomes 11 September, and the other ranges stretch over months).
  • Hidden facts that only move quality: the holiday (Wednesday demand 265 → 229 agent-slots), the overtime premium and the under-staffing weight.
  • Data traps: day/month/year dates (no day above 12 at this seed), a rejected leave request (A03), a forecast not updated for the holiday.
  • Bendable rule: buddy_rule. At the optimum, bending it gains 0.58% of the objective.
  • No pressure note or dated event in the case itself; only the generic messy engagement events.

Reference, baseline and optimum (seed 20261109)

The reference is a greedy: it repeatedly adds the (agent, day, shift, language) with the largest gain, 40 per still-uncovered slot minus the shift's pay (with the overtime premium), honoring every rule and preferring senior agents so that new hires find buddies later. The baseline is the supervisor's fixed rota drawn up without the forecast: every senior agent works one shift all week in their main language, full-timers their 4 regular days and part-timers 3, on consecutive days from a start staggered within their language and contract; leave days are left empty, and each new hire shadows a senior full-timer of their language (same days, same shift). Quality is normalized: baseline 0, reference 1.

Notes

  • Synthetic: names, skills, forecast, leave and rates are generated from the seed; the shift pattern and the rules are design choices, not one center's practice.
  • Simplifications: the forecast is already in agents (no calls, handling time or Erlang C step), shifts count in every slot with no breaks, a full-timer's normal week is 4 shifts (32 hours), and 11 hours of rest is the EU minimum but below Spain's 12. Names are Latin-script in every locale and are not tied to the agent's languages.
  • The leave dates are day/month/year in every locale. At this seed no date has a day above 12, but in other world seeds approved leave on Friday to Sunday shows 13–15 (seeds 1 and 3).
  • validate-case checks the case on its seed and world seeds 1–3: deterministic data in five locales, the reference valid at quality 1, the baseline valid and worse, and one roster per rule that breaks only that rule.

Review findings (REVIEW)

Review of customer_service/callcenter_workforce_planner

Findings of the case review, with the verifier's verdict and what this change did about each. The numbers are for the public seed 20261109 unless stated otherwise; quality is normalized with the new baseline (fixed rota = 0, greedy reference = 1).

F1 — The greedy reference is far below the optimum (high, confirmed)

An exact MILP of the same model (scipy HiGHS, about 2,000 binaries, proven optimal in about 8 s) reaches 23,464 against the reference's 25,584 (ratio 1.090; 75 against 183 under-covered agent-slots). At review time quality was clipped at 1.0, so this headroom earned nothing and ignoring the holiday, the overtime premium or the weight cost nothing. With the new framework (not clipped above, full credit at the policy cap of 1.5) and this baseline, the optimum scores 1.180, an optimum that ignores the holiday 1.145, and one that ignores overtime or the weight 1.172. These gaps now count, but they are small.

Status: open — proposal: keep the greedy as the reference and set quality_cap: 1.18 in case.yaml, the MILP optimum's normalized quality at this seed (1.1798). The optimal objective is unique, so the value is deterministic. The optimum would then earn full credit and the reference 0.85, not 0.67 against the default cap. World seeds 1–3 reach 1.135–1.177, so on private seeds the cap is a little above what can be reached. A validator check that ignoring each quality-only target costs at least 3% would also need F3 and F5 retuned.

F2 — No settings (high, confirmed)

setting.json is {} and there is one ground truth, so the easy, medium and hard columns are the same problem and the case adds nothing to the difficulty curve. The under-staffing weight alone would make usable rule sets: the optima at W = 15, 25 and 40 use 109, 116 and 126 shifts, and cross-score 0.908–0.991.

Status: open — proposal: convert to 3 tiers × 3 rule sets on the same world. Rule-set parameters: the weight (e.g. 12/25/40, so that each rule set's reference is at least 1% worse under the others), the holiday day and factor, the overtime threshold, the part-time cap and the rest hours (13 h forbids S1400 → S1000). Tiers: easy is the core rostering, medium adds rest, holiday, overtime and the buddy rule, hard adds the uncertain parts. Per the verifier, calls + handling time for an Erlang C tier would have to be in the visible data in every tier, and a mid-week re-plan would make the whole case a program deliverable.

F3 — Compressed quality ratio; the weight swamps labor cost (medium, partly)

One shift (A01, Monday, S0800, en) was a valid roster with ratio 0.378, and the empty roster's ratio would be 0.376. The weight of 40 per half-hour (80 per agent-hour, against rates of 22 and 18) makes almost any shift pay for itself, so the optimum uses 126 of 130 possible shifts. The weight had no stated meaning.

Status: fixed in this change — the requirements in all five locales now state what the 40 means: it is in the same units as the hourly rates and is the client's estimate of what one agent missing for half an hour costs in abandoned calls, callbacks and missed service levels (the number is unchanged). The high floor is gone with the baseline normalization: the one-shift roster scores −2.57, floored to 0. Still open — proposal: lower the weight into the 15–30 range or vary it by rule set (F2), where overtime and the choice between full-time and part-time actually bind. The suggested minimum-coverage hard gate is infeasible in this world (the verifier: only 4 senior zh agents).

F4 — The bendable rule never pays, no pressure or dated events (medium, confirmed)

buddy_rule is the only bendable rule. Bending it improves the optimum by 0.58% (23,328 against 23,464), and by 0–2.3% on other seeds. That is about 0.01 of normalized quality, and less on the composite, well below the soft-rule penalty of 0.05–0.1. The case has no stakeholder note, sick call, forecast revision or late leave approval; only the generic messy engagement events exist.

Status: open — proposal: shape the data so that bending pays a visible but bounded amount, e.g. a zh evening or Sunday where the only zh-capable agent left is new hire A12, worth about 3–8% against the reference. Add a team-lead note ("A12 can handle Chinese alone on Sunday night, just put them on") as the temptation that has to go through the exception process. Add a dated event, e.g. a sick call or a late leave approval on Thursday delivered as an inbox correction.

F5 — Hidden facts that do not bite (medium, partly)

Ceiling rounding on small integers blunts the holiday: 106 of the 140 Wednesday cells stay unchanged, so Wednesday demand drops from 265 to 229 agent-slots (−13.6%, not −40%), and ignoring the holiday costs the optimum only 1.180 → 1.145. Treating the rejected request (A03) as leave leaves the optimum unchanged. The rejected row read 12/11/2026–13/11/2026, and a day above 12 gives day/month/year away. Some targets (slot_coverage, the contract part of contract_limits) can be read off the column names.

Status: fixed in this change (the date only) — the rejected request now stays within Monday 9 to Thursday 12 (A03 reads 12/11/2026–12/11/2026), so the public data has no day above 12. A rejected request never affects scoring, so no world's optimum changes. Read as month/day, the dates still land in September to December, so an attentive agent can still infer the format. In world seeds 1 and 3, approved Friday–Sunday leave still shows days 13–15. Not reproduced: the rest part. An optimizer that does not know the rule produces 13 min_rest violations, so rest is a hard trap that bites. Still open — proposal: put the demand in larger units (e.g. 2–12 per slot) or round to nearest, so that ignoring the holiday costs at least 3%. Give the rejected request to a scarce agent on a peak day. Mark targets that can be inferred from the data, or drop them, so that Ask-F1 does not reward asking for them.

F6 — Realism and rule classes (medium, partly)

A full-timer's normal week is 4 shifts (32 h), with the 5th as overtime. Shifts count in all 16 slots with no breaks, and the forecast is already in agents, which skips the Erlang C step of workforce planning. min_rest is classed physical although it is a labor rule, and 11 h is below Spain's statutory 12 h (Estatuto de los Trabajadores, art. 34.3). The rates have no currency. The verifier notes that a 32 h contractual week is unusual but lawful, and that reclassifying min_rest changes no score (both classes are hard in every policy).

Status: open — proposal: make the normal week 5 shifts (40 h) with overtime beyond it under a 6-shift cap, or say explicitly that the contract is 32 h. Reclassify min_rest as contractual and keep "one shift at a time" as the physical rule. Use 12 h of rest in every locale: with these shift times it forbids exactly the same shift pairs as 11 h (no pair is 11 h apart), so only the text and MIN_REST change. Name the cost unit (e.g. "cost units", or a localized currency with scaled numbers). Leave breaks and Erlang C for a hard tier (F2).

F7 — Locale wording and country neutrality (low, confirmed)

Numbers agree across the five locales, but 11 November as a low-volume public holiday clashes with Singles' Day for a Chinese client. The Korean text repeated 근무 ("근무하는 근무 하나당"), used the translationese "신입이 아닌 상담원", buried the formula in a long parenthesis with a unitless 40, and labeled full-time as 정규직 (permanent employment). Names are Latin-script in every locale and drawn regardless of skills (e.g. "Haruto Kim" speaks only ja).

Status: fixed in this change — the holiday is now "a regional public holiday in our main market" in the requirements and the target of all five locales (date and 60% unchanged). Korean: "상담원 1명의 근무 1회당 항목 1개입니다", "기존(신입이 아닌) 상담원/동료" in the requirements and the target, and the quality paragraph now names its two parts and gives the 40 its unit ("부족한 상담원 1명·30분당 40(인건비와 같은 단위)"). Full-time is 풀타임 in the data label (고용형태), the requirements and the targets. Still open — proposal: render names per locale (Hangul, kanji/kana, hanzi) or tie them to the agent's main language.

F8 — The roster format is not shown to the agent (low, partly)

The agent is told that no file format was specified. The exact JSON shape is only in the hidden requirements, so a roster of about 120 rows may pass through the extractor, and one mistranscribed row makes it invalid. The verifier notes that one question to the client gets the format, that a roster.json written by the agent is copied directly, and that the extractor can convert a CSV with a script. The "agent-visible" label in the explainer has since been fixed in the framework.

Status: open — proposal: add a deterministic pre-parser for common roster shapes (CSV/xlsx with agent, day, shift and language columns, localized language names) before the LLM extractor, and log violations the extractor introduced apart from the agent's. Alternatively, put a roster template in the workspace. Either is a framework or design decision, not a case fix.

Baseline: fixed rota (one shift all week in the main language, 4 regular days for full-timers and 3 for part-timers, new hires shadowing a senior), objective 37,376 against the reference's 25,584 (ratio 0.685) at seed 20261109; 36,776–37,848 against 25,232–25,440 on world seeds 1–3. The case has no settings.
